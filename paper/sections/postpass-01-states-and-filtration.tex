\section{Physical states, response relations, and the torsion filtration}
\label{sec:states-filtration}

We work on the path graph on $\mathbf N_{>0}$.  A \emph{physical state} is a finite support whose connected components all have cardinality $2$ or $3$ and are separated by at least one unused vertex.  If $S$ is a physical state, put
\[
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n,
\qquad
g(S)=|\pi_0(S)|,
\qquad
\operatorname{res}(S)=[W(S)]\in \mathbf Q/\mathbf Z.
\]
Whenever two finite states are physically compatible, their literal union is again a state and
\[
W(S\sqcup T)=W(S)+W(T),\qquad
g(S\sqcup T)=g(S)+g(T),\qquad
\operatorname{res}(S\sqcup T)=\operatorname{res}(S)+\operatorname{res}(T).
\]
Thus physical union is a partial commutative multiplication equipped with additive observations.

\subsection{Finite decorated relations}

The proof uses finite relations whose edges carry literal physical labels.  If $X$ and $Y$ are finite target sets, a decorated response from $X$ to $Y$ is a finite relation
\[
R\subseteq X\times \operatorname{Phys}\times Y
\]
whose projection to $Y$ is surjective.  We retain the physical edge label until all compatibility conditions have been checked.  Only afterwards do we pass to the regular image seen by the next consumer.

We do \emph{not} assert that arbitrary right-total decorated relations are closed under unrestricted composition.  The only compositions used below occur on finite diagrams for which two additional facts have been proved:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the ordinary pullback of composable relation edges still covers the intended target; and
\item the resulting finite tuple of physical labels factors through the genuine $n$-ary multiplication domain $D_n$ of the partial physical monoid.
\end{enumerate}
On such a diagram we take the ordinary pullback, form the literal compatible union of the labels, and then take the regular image.  This is the partial/composable response calculus used throughout the paper.

Let $P_n$ denote the ordinary pullback of an $n$-step composable string.  Forgetting labels to the observable pair $(\operatorname{res},g)$ is in general only oplax: composing observable relations may allow tuples that are not physically composable.  If the label map
\[
P_n\longrightarrow \operatorname{Phys}^n
\]
factors through $D_n$, then the oplax comparison is an equality on that finite diagram.  We refer to this as \emph{$n$-ary strictification}.  The reciprocal value is a subadditive filtration under every such composition:
\[
\|R_n\circ\cdots\circ R_1\|_W
\le \sum_{i=1}^n\|R_i\|_W.
\]
All later physical pasting is performed only after the required $P_n\to D_n$ factorization has been established.

\subsection{Finite torsion in $\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z$}

Put
\[
A=\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z,
\qquad
H_n=\{x\in A:nx=0\}
\quad(n\ge1).
\]
The following elementary description is the only structure of the torsion filtration that we need.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:torsion-filtration}
For $m,n\ge1$,
\[
H_n=\langle[1/n]\rangle,
\qquad |H_n|=n,
\]
\[
H_m\le H_n\iff m\mid n,
\qquad
H_m\vee H_n=H_{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}.
\]
Consequently the nonzero join-irreducible stages are exactly $H_{p^e}$, where $p$ is prime and $e\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $[q]\in H_n$, then $nq\in\mathbf Z$, so $[q]=[j/n]$ for some $j\in\mathbf Z$; hence $H_n=\langle[1/n]\rangle$ and has order $n$.  The inclusion criterion follows by testing the generator $[1/m]$.  Let $L=\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)$.  We have $H_m,H_n\le H_L$.  Since $\gcd(L/m,L/n)=1$, there exist $a,b\in\mathbf Z$ with
\[
aL/m+bL/n=1.
\]
Dividing by $L$ shows $[1/L]\in H_m+H_n$, hence $H_m\vee H_n=H_L$.  The join-irreducibles of the divisibility lattice are precisely the prime powers.
\end{proof}

A \emph{current} is therefore indexed by a prime power
\[
Q=p^e.
\]
Let $F_Q$ be the join of current stages of rank at most $Q$, and let $F_{<Q}$ be the join of current stages of smaller rank.  Then the associated simple factor
\[
S_Q=F_Q/F_{<Q}
\]
has order $p$: the $p$-adic valuation of the lower lcm is $e-1$, while adjoining $p^e$ raises it by exactly one.  In particular $S_Q$ is canonically a one-dimensional $\mathbf F_p$-object.

For a real cutoff $X\ge1$, define the endpoint stage
\[
E_X=H_{\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\lfloor X\rfloor)}.
\]
Every element $[a/b]\in A$ belongs to $H_b$, so $(E_X)$ is cofinal in the finite subgroups of $A$ and every fixed class of $A$ dies in $A/E_X$ for all sufficiently large $X$.

We shall also use the literal identification of a current step with the corresponding endpoint step.  If $J$ has rank $Q_J$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-current-step}
E_{Q_J-1}=F_{<J},
\qquad
E_{Q_J}=F_J.
\end{equation}
Indeed $Q_R<Q_J$ is equivalent to $Q_R\le Q_J-1$, while current ranks are injective.  Thus the associated-graded local response $F_{<J}\to F_J$ is literally the step of the global endpoint filtration at $Q_J$.
