\section{The finite affine-ray provider}
\label{sec:affine-provider}

We prove the historical Prefix/MasterSlab theorem only in the form needed by
Proposition~\ref{prop:affine-ray-projection}. The proof has three layers:
\[
\text{finite initial affine cover}
\longrightarrow
\text{simple compact-stage bridges}
\longrightarrow
\text{equal-value profile translations}.
\tag{A1}\label{eq:affine-dag}
\]
The first layer contains only finitely many exceptional calculations. The
second is a canonical cyclic subset-sum construction. The third is generated
by two exact reciprocal identities.

Write $e_2=(1,0)$ and $e_3=(0,1)$ for the standard generators of the component
profile monoid $P_{23}=\N^2$.

\subsection{Equal-value translation fibres}

Put
\[
P(n)=\frac1n+\frac1{n+1}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Remote grade translation]\label{lem:grade-neutral}
For every positive integer $a$ the rational identity
\[
P(a)+P(a^2+3a+1)
=
P(a+2)
+P\!\left(\frac{a(a+3)}2\right)
+P(a(a+3))
\tag{A2}\label{eq:grade-translation}
\]
holds. For every $a\ge3$, the displayed terms form two admissible physical
states with equal reciprocal value and component profiles $2e_2$ and $3e_2$.
Thus their grade difference is $1$ and their ternary-count difference is $0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Subtract $P(a+2)$ from $P(a)$ and reduce to the common denominator
$a(a+1)(a+2)(a+3)$. The resulting rational function is exactly the sum of the
other two terms on the right minus $P(a^2+3a+1)$.

For $a\ge3$, the starts occurring on either side are distinct and separated by
at least one unused vertex, so the displayed $P$-terms are genuinely separate
binary components. The component-profile assertion follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Remote defect translation]\label{lem:defect-neutral}
For every even $a\ge4$, put
\[
b=\frac{a(a+2)}2-2.
\]
Then
\[
P(a)+P(b)
=P(a+1)+P(b)+\frac1{b+2}.
\tag{A3}\label{eq:defect-translation}
\]
Equivalently, the physical alternatives
\[
[a,a+1]\sqcup[b,b+1]
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
[a+1,a+2]\sqcup[b,b+2]
\tag{A4}\label{eq:defect-switch}
\]
have equal reciprocal value and equal total grade $2$, while their component
profiles are
\[
2e_2
\quad\text{and}\quad
e_2+e_3.
\]
Thus the profile translation is exactly $e_3-e_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since
\[
P(a)-P(a+1)
=\frac1a-\frac1{a+2}
=\frac{2}{a(a+2)}
=\frac1{b+2},
\]
we obtain \eqref{eq:defect-translation}. For even $a\ge4$ the two displayed
components on either side are disjoint and non-adjacent, and the component
profiles follow directly.
\end{proof}

As $a\to\infty$ in either lemma, all supports and mutual gaps tend to infinity
and the common reciprocal mass tends to zero. Therefore, after every finite
physical constraint and below every positive mass budget, both equal-value
translation fibres have remote physical points. Finite collections can be
built successively so that every selector is directly physically compatible.

Categorically these constructions are points of the pullback of the equal-value
kernel pair
\[
C\times_W C
\]
along the corresponding component-profile translation. The explicit formulas
disappear after proving nonemptiness of those pullbacks.

\subsection{A complete bridge at one simple compact jump}

Write
\[
L_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n).
\]
This notation is only a coordinate on the compact subgroup lattice of
$\Q/\Z$. At a prime-power jump $n=p^e$ put
\[
D=L_{n-1},
\qquad
L_n=pD.
\]
The new simple quotient has order $p$.

Let $k=k(p)$ be the least positive integer such that
\[
T_k=\frac{k(k+1)}2\ge p-1
\]
and put $R_p=\{1,\ldots,k\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Complete coefficient row]\label{lem:complete-bridge}
The subset-sum map
\[
\operatorname{FinSub}(R_p)
\longrightarrow\Z/p\Z,
\qquad
S\longmapsto\sum_{r\in S}r
\]
is surjective. It has a strict physical lift by endpoint switches, all of the
same grade, covering the new simple compact quotient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The subset sums of $\{1,\ldots,j\}$ fill $[0,T_j]$: after adjoining $j$, the
old interval $[0,T_{j-1}]$ and its translate $[j,j+T_{j-1}]$ overlap or are
adjacent. Thus $R_p$ fills $[0,T_k]$, which contains $[0,p-1]$.

Minimality gives $k\le p-1$. Hence every $r\le k$ satisfies $r<n$ and so
$r\mid D$. Set
\[
a_r=\frac{pD}{r}.
\]
Use the endpoint switch
\[
[a_r+1,a_r+2]
\longleftrightarrow
[a_r,a_r+2].
\]
The reciprocal-value difference is
\[
\frac1{a_r}=\frac{r}{pD}.
\]
Every coordinate contributes one component in either state, so all selector
branches have the same grade $k$. Reduction modulo the previous compact stage
therefore realizes exactly the subset-sum map onto the new simple quotient.
\end{proof}

Minimality of $k$ also gives
\[
k^2=O(p),
\qquad
T_k=O(p),
\]
so one bridge row has reciprocal mass
\[
O(D^{-1}).
\tag{A5}\label{eq:bridge-mass}
\]
Moreover, if $r<s$, then
\[
a_r-a_s
=\frac{pD(s-r)}{rs}
\gg D,
\]
so late bridge rows are internally and mutually strict. All finite early
exceptions are absorbed into the initial seed.

\subsection{Finite initial seed}

\begin{lemma}[Finite affine seed]\label{lem:finite-seed}
There exists a finite strict binary physical family $P_*$ at a finite compact
stage such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item its residue image is one complete affine fibre;
\item its branch grades have a finite maximum $M_*$;
\item it is compatible with every bridge after a finite exceptional regime;
\item after adding the mass majorant of every future bridge, all branch values
remain below $2$ by a positive margin.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{app:finite-seed} gives a completely explicit finite certificate.
Its six residue states represent
\[
0,\frac16,\frac13,\frac12,\frac23,\frac56
\]
and hence the complete initial cyclic fibre. The same finite table checks the
non-asymptotic bridge interactions.

For the value margin, the seed mass is at most $5/6$. The explicit bridge
estimate behind \eqref{eq:bridge-mass} gives mass $<6/D$ at lower modulus $D$.
After the initial stage the successive nontrivial lower moduli at least double,
so the whole future bridge tail is less than
\[
\frac6{60}\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}=\frac15.
\]
Thus every future seed-plus-bridge branch has mass less than
\[
\frac56+\frac15=\frac{31}{30}<2.
\]
The numerical constants are finite proof data and do not survive the seed
interface.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The filtered Prefix cover}

Starting from $P_*$, compose one complete bridge at each subsequent simple
compact jump through a base $B$. Since every bridge covers the next simple
quotient, finite induction gives a complete affine fibre at the stage
corresponding to $B$.

The bridge grades are fixed, but the finitely many seed branches need not have
the same grade. Let $M_*$ be their maximum. For a seed branch of grade $g$,
use the full cardinality fibre
\[
\operatorname{FinSub}_{M_*-g}(I)
\]
of an $M_*$-element collection of mutually compatible remote grade-translation
coordinates from Lemma~\ref{lem:grade-neutral}. Choosing the high alternative
on exactly $M_*-g$ coordinates changes the grade by exactly $M_*-g$; the common
low-state contribution is the same for every seed branch. Thus all branches
have one common final grade, while reciprocal value, ternary count, and residue
are unchanged. The neutral coordinates can be made arbitrarily light, so the
positive margin of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-seed} survives.

The construction yields a proper filtered family of finite homogeneous affine
covers. Its source map to the eligible Tail-source filter satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Tendsto}
(\beta,\mathcal F_B,\mathcal F_R).
\tag{A6}\label{eq:prefix-tendsto}
\]
We recall the transparency estimate. Every signed-inverse atom has a globally
injective distinguished centre and a uniformly bounded envelope, so each fixed
physical envelope deletes only $O(1)$ raw occurrences globally. The fixed seed
and grade-equalization families contribute finitely many envelopes. A bridge
row meeting a current window has lower modulus in a bounded multiplicative
range; the linear Chebyshev bounds proved in Appendix~\ref{app:prime-supply}
force its prime-power index to be $O(\log Q)$. Successive nontrivial lower
moduli at least double, so only $O(\log\log Q)$ bridge rows meet the window,
each with $O(\sqrt{\log Q})$ envelopes. Hence
\[
N_Q(\text{Prefix})
=O(\sqrt{\log Q}\log\log Q+1)
=o(Q/\log Q).
\tag{A7}\label{eq:prefix-window}
\]
Sparse deletion therefore leaves a fixed positive relative fraction eventually.
The same argument, together with properness of the prime-power predecessor map
at infinity, covers boundary predecessor rows. This proves the uniform
transparency response and \eqref{eq:prefix-tendsto}.

\subsection{Defect filling as a parameterized product cover}

Fix one late Prefix base $b$. Let $P_b$ be its finite normalized affine cover
and put
\[
d_0(b)=\max_{x\in P_b}b_3(x).
\]
Let $L\ge d_0(b)$. Construct $L$ mutually compatible remote defect-translation
coordinates using Lemma~\ref{lem:defect-neutral}, with positive coordinate
budgets whose total is smaller than the available Prefix margin.

For a Prefix state $x$ and a requested defect $d\in[d_0(b),L]$,
\[
0\le d-b_3(x)\le L.
\]
Pull the full cardinality map
\[
\operatorname{card}:\operatorname{FinSub}(\{1,\ldots,L\})
\longrightarrow\{0,\ldots,L\}
\]
back along $(x,d)\mapsto d-b_3(x)$. Since the cardinality map is surjective,
this selector pullback is regular epi.

Every selected high alternative replaces one binary component by one ternary
component while preserving reciprocal value and total grade. Hence the
resulting branch has ternary count exactly $d$, and the underlying affine
correction and total grade are independent of the selector. Thus
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
S_{b,L} \arrow[rr,two heads,"{(\operatorname{required},b_3)}"] &&
\operatorname{Required}(\phi_{b,L})
\times[d_0(b),L].
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{A8}\label{eq:masterslab-cover}
\]

Because every defect coordinate has arbitrarily small mass, the complete slab
retains a positive uniform value margin. Place successive coordinates beyond
the square of the preceding support maximum. Then at most one such coordinate
meets any fixed current-scale window eventually, uniformly in $L$; the defect
cube contributes only $O(1)$ envelopes to that window. Together with
\eqref{eq:prefix-window}, the whole slab remains row-transparent with one
positive uniform transparency constant.

\begin{theorem}[MasterSlab provider]\label{thm:masterslab}
Over a covering good-scale response $(c,\eta)$, eventually in the Prefix base
$b$, for every $L\ge d_0(b)$ there is a finite normalized physical family
$S_{b,L}$ satisfying \eqref{eq:masterslab-cover}, with one common total grade,
branchwise value in $(0,2-\eta)$, and row transparency at level $c$.
\end{theorem}

Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:affine-ray-projection} to
Theorem~\ref{thm:masterslab} gives the filtered affine ray used in
Section~\ref{sec:germs}.
