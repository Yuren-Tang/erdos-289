\section{The finite affine-ray provider}
\label{sec:affine-provider}

We prove the historical Prefix/MasterSlab theorem only in the form needed by
the projection of Proposition~\ref{prop:affine-ray-projection}.  The proof has
three layers:
\[
\text{finite initial affine cover}
\longrightarrow
\text{simple compact-stage bridges}
\longrightarrow
\text{equal-value profile translations}.
\tag{\ref{sec:affine-provider}.1}
\]
The first layer contains only finitely many exceptional calculations.  The
second is a canonical cyclic subset-sum construction.  The third is generated
by two exact reciprocal identities.

\subsection{Equal-value translation fibres}

Put
\[
P(n)=\frac1n+\frac1{n+1}.
\]
The following two identities generate the profile adjustments required by the
provider.

\begin{lemma}[Remote grade translation]\label{lem:grade-neutral}
For every positive integer $a$,
\[
P(a)+P(a^2+3a+1)
=
P(a+2)
+P\!\left(\frac{a(a+3)}2\right)
+P(a(a+3)).
\tag{\ref{sec:affine-provider}.2}
\]
The two corresponding physical states have equal reciprocal value, component
profiles $2e_2$ and $3e_2$, and hence grade difference $1$ and ternary-count
difference $0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Subtract $P(a+2)$ from $P(a)$ and reduce to the common denominator
$a(a+1)(a+2)(a+3)$.  The resulting rational function is exactly the sum of
the other two terms on the right minus $P(a^2+3a+1)$.  The component-profile
statement is immediate from the displayed binary blocks.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Remote defect translation]\label{lem:defect-neutral}
For every even $a\ge4$, put
\[
b=\frac{a(a+2)}2-2.
\]
Then
\[
P(a)+P(b)
=P(a+1)+P(b)+\frac1{b+2}.
\tag{\ref{sec:affine-provider}.3}
\]
Equivalently, the physical alternatives
\[
[a,a+1]\sqcup[b,b+1]
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
[a+1,a+2]\sqcup[b,b+2]
\tag{\ref{sec:affine-provider}.4}
\]
have equal reciprocal value and equal total grade $2$, while their component
profiles are
\[
2e_2
\quad\text{and}\quad
e_2+e_3.
\]
Thus the profile translation is exactly
\[
e_3-e_2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since
\[
P(a)-P(a+1)
=\frac1a-\frac1{a+2}
=\frac{2}{a(a+2)}
=\frac1{b+2},
\]
we obtain \eqref{sec:affine-provider}.3.  The component profiles follow from
the two displayed supports.
\end{proof}

As $a\to\infty$ in either lemma, the supports and their mutual gaps tend to
infinity and their common mass tends to zero.  Therefore, after every finite
physical constraint and below every positive mass budget, both translation
fibres have remote physical points.  Finite collections can be built
successively so that every selector is directly physically compatible.

Categorically these constructions are points of the pullback of the equal-value
kernel pair
\[
C\times_W C
\]
along the corresponding component-profile translation.  The explicit
polynomial formulas disappear after proving nonemptiness of those pullbacks.

\subsection{A complete bridge at one simple compact jump}

Write
\[
L_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n).
\]
This notation is used only as a coordinate on the compact subgroup lattice of
$\Q/\Z$.  At a prime-power jump $n=p^e$ put
\[
D=L_{n-1},
\qquad
L_n=pD.
\]
The new simple quotient has order $p$.

Let $k=k(p)$ be the least positive integer such that
\[
T_k=\frac{k(k+1)}2\ge p-1
\]
and put
\[
R_p=\{1,\ldots,k\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Complete coefficient row]\label{lem:complete-bridge}
The subset-sum map
\[
\operatorname{FinSub}(R_p)
\longrightarrow\Z/p\Z,
\qquad
S\longmapsto\sum_{r\in S}r
\]
is surjective.  It has a strict physical lift by endpoint switches, all of the
same grade, covering the new simple compact quotient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The subset sums of $\{1,\ldots,j\}$ fill the whole interval $[0,T_j]$: when
$j$ is adjoined, the old interval $[0,T_{j-1}]$ and its translate
$[j,j+T_{j-1}]$ overlap or are adjacent.  Thus $R_p$ fills $[0,T_k]$, which
contains $[0,p-1]$.

Minimality gives $k\le p-1$.  Hence every $r\le k$ satisfies $r\mid D$.
Set
\[
a_r=\frac{pD}{r}.
\]
Use the endpoint switch
\[
[a_r+1,a_r+2]
\longleftrightarrow
[a_r,a_r+2].
\]
The difference of reciprocal values is
\[
\frac1{a_r}=\frac{r}{pD}.
\]
Every coordinate contributes one component in either state, so all selector
branches have the same grade $k$.  Reduction modulo the previous lattice
therefore realizes exactly the preceding subset-sum map onto the simple
quotient.
\end{proof}

The same proof gives two useful estimates.  Minimality of $k$ implies
\[
k^2=O(p),
\qquad
T_k=O(p),
\]
so one bridge row has reciprocal mass
\[
O(D^{-1}).
\tag{\ref{sec:affine-provider}.5}
\]
Moreover, if $r<s$, then
\[
a_r-a_s
=\frac{pD(s-r)}{rs}
\gg D,
\]
so late bridge rows are internally and mutually strict.  All finite early
exceptions are absorbed into the initial seed.

\subsection{Finite initial seed}

Only a finite starting stage is not covered by the asymptotic bridge
construction.

\begin{lemma}[Finite affine seed]\label{lem:finite-seed}
There exists a finite strict binary physical family $P_*$ at a finite compact
stage with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item its residue image is one complete affine fibre;
\item its branch grades have a finite maximum $M_*$;
\item it is compatible with every bridge after a finite exceptional regime;
\item after adding the mass majorant of every future bridge, all branch values
remain below $2$ by a positive margin.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A fully explicit finite certificate is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:finite-seed}.  Its six residue states represent
\[
0,\frac16,\frac13,\frac12,\frac23,\frac56
\]
and hence the complete initial cyclic fibre.  The same finite table checks the
only non-asymptotic bridge interactions.

For the value margin, the seed mass is at most $5/6$.  By
\eqref{sec:affine-provider}.5 a bridge with lower modulus $D$ has mass less
than $6/D$.  After the initial stage the successive nontrivial lower moduli at
least double, so the whole future bridge tail is less than
\[
\frac6{60}\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}=\frac15.
\]
Thus every future seed-plus-bridge branch has mass less than
\[
\frac56+\frac15=\frac{31}{30}<2.
\]
This proves existence of a positive open margin.  The displayed constants are
finite verification data and do not survive the seed interface.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The filtered Prefix cover}

Starting from $P_*$, compose one complete bridge at each subsequent simple
compact jump through a base $B$.  Since every bridge covers the next simple
quotient, finite induction gives a complete affine fibre at the stage
corresponding to $B$.

The bridge grades are fixed, but the finitely many seed branches need not have
the same grade.  Let $M_*$ be their maximum.  For a seed branch of grade $g$,
use the full cardinality fibre
\[
\operatorname{FinSub}_{M_*-g}(I)
\]
of an $M_*$-element collection of remote grade-translation coordinates from
Lemma~\ref{lem:grade-neutral}.  Pullback stability gives all selectors at once;
choosing the high alternative on exactly $M_*-g$ coordinates raises every seed
branch to the same total grade without changing reciprocal value or residue.
The neutral coordinates can be made arbitrarily light, so the positive margin
of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-seed} survives.

The construction yields a proper filtered family of finite homogeneous affine
covers.  Its source map to the eligible Tail-source filter satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Tendsto}
(\beta,\mathcal F_B,\mathcal F_R).
\tag{\ref{sec:affine-provider}.6}
\]
We briefly recall why the physical obstacle remains transparent.

Every signed-inverse atom has a globally injective distinguished centre and a
uniformly bounded envelope.  Hence each fixed physical envelope deletes only
$O(1)$ raw occurrences globally.  The fixed seed and grade-equalization
families contribute only finitely many envelopes.  A bridge row which meets a
current window must have lower modulus in a bounded multiplicative range; the
linear growth of the Chebyshev function forces its prime-power index to be
$O(\log Q)$.  Successive nontrivial lower moduli at least double, so only
$O(\log\log Q)$ bridge rows meet the window, each with $O(\sqrt{\log Q})$
envelopes.  Thus
\[
N_Q(\text{Prefix})
=O(\sqrt{\log Q}\log\log Q+1)
=o(Q/\log Q).
\tag{\ref{sec:affine-provider}.7}
\]
Sparse deletion from the raw row therefore leaves a fixed positive relative
fraction eventually.  The same argument, together with properness of the
prime-power predecessor map at infinity, covers boundary predecessor rows.
This proves the uniform transparency response and \eqref{sec:affine-provider}.6.

\subsection{Defect filling as a parameterized product cover}

Fix one late Prefix base $b$.  Let $P_b$ be its finite normalized affine cover
and put
\[
d_0(b)=\max_{x\in P_b}b_3(x).
\]
Let $L\ge d_0(b)$.  Construct $L$ mutually compatible remote defect-translation
coordinates using Lemma~\ref{lem:defect-neutral}, with positive coordinate
budgets whose total is smaller than the available Prefix margin.

For a Prefix state $x$ and a requested defect
\[
d\in[d_0(b),L],
\]
we have
\[
0\le d-b_3(x)\le L.
\]
Take the canonical pullback of the full cardinality map
\[
\operatorname{card}:\operatorname{FinSub}(\{1,\ldots,L\})
\longrightarrow\{0,\ldots,L\}
\]
along
\[
x\longmapsto d-b_3(x).
\]
Since the cardinality map is surjective, this selector pullback is regular epi.

Every selected high alternative replaces one binary component by one ternary
component while preserving reciprocal value and total grade.  Hence the
resulting branch has ternary count exactly $d$, and the underlying affine
correction and total grade are independent of the selector.

Thus the whole branch object fits into a regular-epimorphic product cover
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
S_{b,L} \arrow[rr,two heads,"{(\operatorname{required},b_3)}"] &&
\operatorname{Required}(\phi_{b,L})
\times[d_0(b),L].
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{\ref{sec:affine-provider}.8}
\]

Because every defect coordinate has arbitrarily small mass, the complete slab
retains a positive uniform value margin.  The coordinates can be placed
successively beyond the square of the preceding support maximum.  Consequently
at most one such coordinate meets any fixed current-scale window eventually;
the complete defect cube contributes only $O(1)$ envelopes to that window.
Together with \eqref{sec:affine-provider}.7, the whole slab remains
row-transparent with one positive uniform transparency constant.

We have therefore proved the stronger historical provider needed by the R10
projection.

\begin{theorem}[MasterSlab provider]\label{thm:masterslab}
Over a covering good-scale response $(c,\eta)$, eventually in the Prefix base
$b$, for every $L\ge d_0(b)$ there is a finite normalized physical family
$S_{b,L}$ satisfying \eqref{sec:affine-provider}.8, with one common total
grade, branchwise value in $(0,2-\eta)$, and row transparency at level $c$.
\end{theorem}

Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:affine-ray-projection} to
Theorem~\ref{thm:masterslab} gives the filtered affine ray used in
Section~\ref{sec:germs}.
