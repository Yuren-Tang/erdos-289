\section{Filtered response germs and exactification}
\label{sec:germs}

The final exactification argument does not consume the full historical Prefix,
MasterSlab, or BinaryTail objects. This section records the two regular-image
projections which reduce those stronger providers to the source-filtered germs
actually used by the root.

\subsection{Target restriction of a zero-correction cover}

Let
\[
A=\Q/\Z
\]
and let
\[
H\le G\le A
\]
be finite subgroups. Write
\[
q_{H,G}:A/H\longrightarrow A/G.
\]
The required residue object for the zero correction from $H$ to $G$ is
canonically
\[
\Req_0(H,G):=\ker q_{H,G}\cong G/H.
\]
No choice of representatives is involved.

\begin{lemma}[Target restriction]\label{lem:target-restriction}
Suppose a normalized physical family $T$ covers the zero correction from $H$
to $G$. Thus its realizer projection is a regular epimorphism
\[
p_T:\Real_T(H,G;0)\twoheadrightarrow\Req_0(H,G).
\]
Let $S$ be a parameter object and let
\[
r:S\to A/H
\]
be a generalized residue such that
\[
q_{H,G}r=0.
\]
Then there is a canonical pullback response
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\Real_{T,r} \arrow[r] \arrow[d,two heads]
& \Real_T(H,G;0) \arrow[d,two heads,"p_T"]\\
S \arrow[r,"r"'] & \Req_0(H,G)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
whose left vertical map is regular epi. Binaryity, component profile,
resource upper bounds, and compatibility with a fixed obstacle are inherited
by restriction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The condition $q_{H,G}r=0$ is exactly the universal property which factors
$r$ through $\ker q_{H,G}$. The displayed square is therefore canonical.
Regular epimorphisms are stable under pullback. The remaining properties are
predicates or observables inherited by the restricted physical family.
\end{proof}

For $r\in A/H$, let
\[
H[r]=q_H^{-1}(\langle r\rangle)
\]
be the least finite extension of $H$ killing $r$. If $G\ge H[r]$, then
$r\in\ker q_{H,G}$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:target-restriction} applies.

\subsection{The late-source targeted Tail germ}

Fix a raw reciprocal row system $R$ of the kind constructed in
Section~\ref{sec:ltar-provider}. Its eligible source object carries a proper
filter $\mathcal F_R$, and every eligible source $\beta$ determines a finite
subgroup
\[
H_\beta\le A.
\]

For a row-transparent finite obstacle $O$, a residue $r\in A/H_\beta$, a
tolerance $\varepsilon>0$, and $h\in\N$, let
\[
\LTar_R(\varepsilon,c,\beta,O,r,h)
\]
denote the normalized solution object of binary physical responses with
\[
(b_2,b_3)=(h,0),
\qquad
W_+\le\varepsilon,
\qquad
q_{H_\beta}([W])=r,
\]
compatible with $O$. The branchwise mass notation $W_+$ and transparency
parameter $c$ are defined explicitly in Section~\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.

\begin{theorem}[Late-source targeted residue saturation]
\label{thm:ltar-closed}
For every $R$, every $c>0$, and every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
\forall^\infty\beta\,[\mathcal F_R],
\quad
\forall O,
\quad
\forall r\in A/H_\beta,
\quad
\forall^\infty h,
\]
the projection
\[
\LTar_R(\varepsilon,c,\beta,O,r,h)\longrightarrow1
\]
is regular epi.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the stronger BinaryTail theorem at the same tolerance and source. Once a
good source $\beta$ and a residue $r\in A/H_\beta$ have been fixed, form the
canonical least absorber
\[
K_r=H_\beta[r].
\]
Instantiate BinaryTail at the compact request $K_r$. Its eventual endpoint
$G\ge K_r$ supplies a full zero-correction cover from $H_\beta$ to $G$.
Since $G$ kills $r$, Lemma~\ref{lem:target-restriction} restricts this cover to
the required residue $r$.

No quantifier is interchanged: the good-source locus of BinaryTail is already
uniform in the obstacle and compact request; after fixing $\beta$, the residue
$r$ determines $K_r$, and the eventual grade threshold is allowed to depend
on $r$. The endpoint, compact request, least absorber, and unused parts of the
zero-correction fibre disappear after this pullback.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Fixed-source smallness is impossible]
The source eventuality in Theorem~\ref{thm:ltar-closed} is essential. If
\[
H_n=n^{-1}\Z/\Z,
\]
then a positive value $0<W<1$ with zero residue modulo $H_n$ has $W\ge1/n$.
Hence a fixed source cannot support positive zero-residue states of arbitrarily
small mass. The tolerance quantifier must precede the eventual source
quantifier.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Projection of MasterSlab to a filtered affine ray}

Let $(B,\mathcal F_B)$ be a Prefix base and let
\[
\beta:B\to S_R
\]
be its source map. The Prefix theorem supplies
\[
\operatorname{Tendsto}(\beta,\mathcal F_B,\mathcal F_R).
\tag{F1}
\]

Over a generalized good-scale point $(c,\eta)$, the stronger MasterSlab
theorem gives, for all sufficiently late $b$ and every
\[
L\ge d_0(b),
\qquad d_0(b)=\delta_b^{\sup},
\]
a normalized finite family $S_{b,L}$ for which
\[
(\operatorname{required},\delta_3):
S_{b,L}\twoheadrightarrow
\operatorname{Required}(\phi_{b,L})
\times[d_0(b),L]
\tag{F2}
\]
is regular epi. All branches have one common total grade, reciprocal value in
$(0,2-\eta)$, and the whole family is row-transparent with parameter $c$ at
the source $\beta(b)$.

\begin{proposition}[Affine-ray fibre projection]
\label{prop:affine-ray-projection}
For every sufficiently late $b$ and every $d\ge d_0(b)$ there is a finite
normalized physical family
\[
F_{b,d}\twoheadrightarrow q_{H_b}^{-1}(a_{b,d})
\]
covering one affine coset in $A$, with constant component profile
\[
(m_{b,d},d)
\]
for a derived $m_{b,d}\in\N$, reciprocal value in $(0,2-\eta)$, and the same
row-transparency lower bound $c$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the MasterSlab theorem substitute $L=d$. Pull (F2) back along the terminal
defect fibre
\[
\operatorname{Required}(\phi_{b,d})\times\{d\}
\hookrightarrow
\operatorname{Required}(\phi_{b,d})\times[d_0(b),d].
\]
Pullback stability gives
\[
S^{\mathrm{fib}}_{b,d}\twoheadrightarrow
\operatorname{Required}(\phi_{b,d}).
\]
Because the correction starts at the zero compact stage and ends at $H_b$,
its required object is canonically the literal affine coset
\[
\operatorname{Required}(\phi_{b,d})
=q_{H_b}^{-1}(a_{b,d})\subseteq A.
\]
Thus the covering map records the actual centered value class $[W(f)]\in A$,
not merely its residue in $A/H_b$.

The fibre has exact ternary count $d$ and inherits the common total grade
$K_{b,d}$, hence its component profile is
\[
(b_2,b_3)=(K_{b,d}-d,d).
\]
The value margin is branchwise and therefore restricts to the fibre.
Transparency is hereditary in the required direction. All other defect
fibres, selector data, neutral coordinates, and cross-defect commonality are
forgotten after this pullback by regular-image factorization.
\end{proof}

The family of Proposition~\ref{prop:affine-ray-projection}, together with
(F1), is the \emph{filtered affine ray}. The root uses no common affine
correction or common affine grade across different defects.

\subsection{Meeting the two source germs without a chosen source}

The generalized margin coordinate $\eta$ supplies the Tail tolerance
\[
\varepsilon=\eta/2.
\]
For fixed $(c,\eta)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:ltar-closed} gives a
$\mathcal F_R$-large set of good Tail sources. Its inverse image under $\beta$
is $\mathcal F_B$-large by (F1). Intersecting with the affine-ray large locus
gives another $\mathcal F_B$-large subobject
\[
U_{\mathrm{meet}}
=U_{\mathrm{aff}}
\cap
\beta^{-1}(U_{\mathrm{tail}}).
\tag{F3}
\]
Properness makes $U_{\mathrm{meet}}$ nonempty.

Factor the defect-floor map through its regular image:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
U_{\mathrm{meet}} \arrow[rr,two heads,"e"]
\arrow[drr,"d_0"']
&& D_0 \arrow[d,hook]\\
&& \N.
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{F4}
\]
The subobject $D_0\subseteq\N$ is nonempty, so it has a least element
$d_*:=\min D_0$. Pull $e$ back along the corresponding point
$\{d_*\}\to D_0$:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
U_* \arrow[r] \arrow[d,two heads]
& U_{\mathrm{meet}} \arrow[d,two heads,"e"]\\
\{d_*\} \arrow[r] & D_0.
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{F5}
\]
Thus $U_*\to1$ is covering by pullback stability. We henceforth work over this
covering parameter base. Every source in $U_*$ has defect floor exactly $d_*$;
no preferred source or section is selected.

The source is now held fixed over $U_*$. This is essential for the present
provider: the Prefix defect floor rises as the source moves later, while the
available Tail resource improves.

\begin{proposition}[Source--defect tradeoff]
\label{prop:source-defect-tradeoff}
For the current Prefix provider,
\[
d_0(b)=\delta_b^{\sup}\longrightarrow\infty
\]
along the moving source filter. Consequently the source may not be
existentially reselected with the final grade after a fixed defect $d$ has
been chosen.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a finite full product family $F$, write
\[
\Delta(F)=\max_{x\in F}\delta_3(x).
\]
Additivity of $\delta_3$ and the presence of every product branch give
\[
\Delta(F\star G)=\Delta(F)+\Delta(G).
\]
Every nontrivial universal bridge contains a Boolean endpoint switch with a
length-three alternative, so its defect supremum is at least one. The moving
Prefix crosses infinitely many such bridge stages. Its defect supremum is
therefore the seed supremum plus an unbounded sum of positive bridge costs.
The MasterSlab affine ray begins at this supremum. Moving the source later can
thus eventually push a fixed defect below the available affine ray.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Coset cancellation}

Work over the covering base $U_*$. Fix $d\ge d_*$ and write $H$ for the
corresponding source subgroup. The affine response gives a finite family
\[
p_F:F_d\twoheadrightarrow q_H^{-1}(a_d)
\]
with component profile $(m_d,d)$, where $p_F(f)=[W(f)]$. Its complete physical
footprint is used as the finite row-transparent obstacle for the Tail theorem.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:ltar-closed} at the same source $H$, the opposite
residue $-a_d\in A/H$, and tolerance $\eta/2$. For all sufficiently large $h$
obtain a compatible binary Tail family $T_h$ of profile $(h,0)$ with
$W_+(T_h)\le\eta/2$ and a centered-value map
\[
\tau:T_h\longrightarrow A,
\qquad
q_H\tau=-a_d.
\]
Hence inversion factors canonically through the affine coset:
\[
-\tau:T_h\longrightarrow q_H^{-1}(a_d).
\]
Form the literal matching pullback
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
M_{d,h} \arrow[r] \arrow[d,two heads]
& F_d \arrow[d,two heads,"p_F"]\\
T_h \arrow[r,"-\tau"']
& q_H^{-1}(a_d).
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{F6}
\]
The left vertical map is regular epi. Every matched pair $(f,t)$ satisfies
\[
[W(f)]=-[W(t)]\quad\text{in }A,
\]
and therefore
\[
[W(f)+W(t)]=0\quad\text{in }\Q/\Z.
\tag{F7}
\]
Because the Tail was constructed against the complete affine obstacle, every
matched pair is physically compatible, so its union $f\sqcup t$ is an actual
physical state.

Moreover
\[
0<W(f)<2-\eta,
\qquad
0\le W(t)\le W_+(T_h)\le\eta/2,
\]
so
\[
0<W(f\sqcup t)<2.
\]
Together with (F7), this gives
\[
W(f\sqcup t)\in\Z\cap(0,2)=\{1\}.
\]
Thus the reciprocal value is exactly $1$.

Component profiles add:
\[
(m_d,d)+(h,0)=(m_d+h,d).
\tag{F8}
\]
Hence the realizable binary counts at defect $d$ contain a translate of a
final subset of $\N$ and are themselves final.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fibrewise}]
The construction above is performed after base change along the regular
epimorphism $U_*\to1$. For every $d\ge d_*$, equation (F8) gives all
sufficiently large binary counts in the exact component-profile spectrum over
that cover. Existence of such a physical state is a subterminal statement in
the external parameters $(d,b)$; inverse image on subobjects is conservative
along a regular epimorphism. Hence the existence statement descends from
$U_*$ to the terminal base. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:fibrewise} without
choosing a source section.
\end{proof}

For Erdős Problem 289 itself no finite meet is required: fix one
$d\ge d_*$; adding this fixed $d$ to the final binary-count set gives a final
set of total component counts. Finite intersections over $d\in[d_*,D]$ give
the stronger finite-defect rectangle as a corollary.
