\section{Eventual coverage of component counts}
\label{sec:scalar-finality}

We now discard the interval labels entirely.  The only information retained from Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-profile} is the required incoming width $\delta_\rho(Q)$ and, at prime steps, the positive width increment $\mu_\rho(q)$.  The goal is to prove eventual coverage of all component counts before choosing an independent set of intervals.

Enumerate the prime powers
\[
\{Q=p^e:Q\ge Q_{\mathrm{tr}}\}
\]
in increasing order.  For an integer interval $I=[A,B]$ write $w(I)=B-A$.  At a prime $q$, Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-profile} gives
\[
[A,B]\longmapsto
[A+\delta_\rho(q),\,B+\delta_\rho(q)+\mu_\rho(q)],
\tag{6.1}\label{eq:prime-action}
\]
so the width increases by $\mu_\rho(q)$.  At a composite prime power $Q=p^e$, $e\ge2$, it gives
\[
[A,B]\longmapsto[A+\delta_\rho(Q),B],
\tag{6.2}\label{eq:composite-action}
\]
so the width decreases by $\delta_\rho(Q)$.  A step is available whenever the incoming width is at least $\delta_\rho(Q)$; in that case the old and new integer intervals overlap.

Put
\[
\Phi(X)=\frac X{\log X}.
\]
Define the maximal required width
\[
\Delta(X)=\max_{Q\le X}\delta_\rho(Q)
\]
over the displayed prime-power domain, and the accumulated composite loss
\[
\mathcal L(X)=
\sum_{\substack{p^e\le X\\ e\ge2}}\delta_\rho(p^e).
\tag{6.3}\label{eq:cumulative-debt}
\]
By \eqref{eq:d-composite},
\[
\Delta(X)=O_\rho((\log X)^2)=o(\Phi(X)).
\tag{6.4}\label{eq:max-demand-subscale}
\]
The number of prime powers $p^e\le X$ with $e\ge2$ is $O(\sqrt X\log X)$, so
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L(X)=O_\rho(\sqrt X\,\log^3X)
=o(\Phi(X)).}
\tag{6.5}\label{eq:debt-subscale}
\]

\subsection{Bounded-lag prime compensation}

Let $\Lambda\ge2$ be the multiplier in Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-supply-main}.  Put
\[
\Lambda_s=4\Lambda.
\tag{6.6}\label{eq:sponsor-lag}
\]
Every sufficiently large prime-power rank $Q$ admits an earlier prime $q$ such that
\[
q<Q\le\Lambda_s q.
\tag{6.7}\label{eq:sponsor-bounded-lag}
\]
Indeed, let
\[
Y=\left\lfloor\frac{Q}{2\Lambda}\right\rfloor.
\]
For large $Q$, Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-supply-main} gives a prime $Y<q\le\Lambda Y$.  Writing
\[
Q=(2\Lambda)Y+r,
\qquad0\le r<2\Lambda,
\]
shows $q<Q$ and, for $Y\ge1$,
\[
Q<2\Lambda(Y+1)\le4\Lambda Y<4\Lambda q.
\]
After enlarging the onset, $q$ also lies above $Q_{\mathrm{tr}}$.

By \eqref{eq:mint-prime},
\[
\mu_\rho(q)
\gg_\rho\frac q{\log q}
\asymp_{\rho,\Lambda_s}\frac Q{\log Q}
=\Theta(\Phi(Q)).
\tag{6.8}\label{eq:sponsor-mint}
\]
Thus every sufficiently large step has a preceding prime step, at bounded multiplicative distance, whose positive width increment is of order $Q/\log Q$.

\subsection{Initial width and continuation}

Let $B_\dagger$ be any prescribed lower cutoff.  Define
\[
R(B)=\Delta(\Lambda_sB)+\mathcal L(\Lambda_sB).
\tag{6.9}\label{eq:startup-request}
\]
By \eqref{eq:max-demand-subscale} and \eqref{eq:debt-subscale},
\[
R(B)=o(B/\log B).
\tag{6.10}\label{eq:startup-subscale-scalar}
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:wide-start} can therefore be applied with $L(B)=R(B)$.  Choose $B$ large enough that $B\ge B_\dagger$, $B\ge Q_{\mathrm{tr}}$, and all preceding asymptotic conclusions hold, and fix one wide affine initial cover with component-count interval of width at least $R(B)$ and centre $\gamma_B$.

If the bounded-lag prime associated with a step $Q$ lies at or before $B$, then $Q\le\Lambda_sB$.  Ignoring all positive increments in this finite transition, the initial width $R(B)$ covers both the largest single requirement and the total composite loss through $\Lambda_sB$.

After the associated prime always lies in the post-$B$ sequence, keep only the positive increment from that prime and subtract all composite losses up to the current step.  Immediately before a step of rank $Q$,
\[
w(Q)\ge\mu_\rho(q)-\mathcal L(Q).
\tag{6.11}\label{eq:sponsored-width}
\]
Equations \eqref{eq:sponsor-mint} and \eqref{eq:debt-subscale} give $w(Q)\gg\Phi(Q)$, whereas the required incoming width is $o(\Phi(Q))$ by \eqref{eq:max-demand-subscale}.  Hence all sufficiently large steps are available; together with the finite transition above, the entire sequence after $B$ is defined.

Successive component-count intervals overlap.  Their upper endpoints are unbounded because the positive increments at prime steps are unbounded.  Hence their union contains a final ray in $\N$.

\subsection{Vanishing of the affine centre}

The initial affine fibre has centre
\[
\gamma_B\in A/E_B.
\]
Each later prime-power step transports this class along the quotient maps of the endpoint filtration.  By cofinality of $(E_X)$, there is a finite cutoff after which the image of $\gamma_B$ in $A/E_X$ is zero.  Removing a finite initial segment does not change the final-ray conclusion.

Let
\[
\mathcal H_B=\{X\in\N:X\ge B\}.
\tag{6.12}\label{eq:cutoff-base}
\]
For $X\in\mathcal H_B$, let $I_X$ be the component-count interval obtained after all prime-power steps of rank at most $X$ have been processed.  For $k\in\N$ define
\[
\mathcal X_k=
\{X\in\mathcal H_B:
\gamma_B\mapsto0\text{ in }A/E_X,
\quad k\in I_X\}.
\tag{6.13}\label{eq:term-k}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Eventual component-count coverage]\label{thm:scalar-terminality}
For every lower cutoff $B_\dagger$ one can choose $B\ge B_\dagger$ and a wide affine initial cover at $B$ such that
\[
\exists k_0\ \forall k\ge k_0:
\qquad \mathcal X_k\ne\varnothing.
\]
The theorem is entirely observable: it uses no incompatibility graph, independent transversal, joint interval union, or reciprocal-mass tail from the later realization.
\end{theorem}
