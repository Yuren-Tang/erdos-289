\section{Signed-inverse families at prime-power steps}
\label{sec:signed-inverse}

For every sufficiently large prime power $Q=p^e$ we construct a finite family $\mathcal A_Q\subset\mathcal C$ of two-point interval components.  The construction serves two purposes: its image in the simple quotient $S_Q$ is large and uniformly distributed, while its members have uniformly controlled incompatibility and reciprocal mass.  The auxiliary carrier, sign, and coefficient coordinates used below disappear after Proposition~\ref{prop:signed-inverse-capabilities}.

Fix the integer $\Lambda$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-supply-main} and put
\[
B_{\mathrm{car}}=\Lambda+1.
\]
Let $Q=p^e$ and define the integer cutoff
\[
X_Q=Q\operatorname{div}B_{\mathrm{car}}.
\tag{3.1}\label{eq:carrier-base}
\]
Choose carrier primes
\[
X_Q<b\le\Lambda X_Q,
\qquad (b,Q)=1.
\tag{3.2}\label{eq:carrier-band}
\]
Writing
\[
Q=B_{\mathrm{car}}X_Q+r,
\qquad 0\le r<B_{\mathrm{car}},
\]
shows that every carrier satisfies
\[
B_{\mathrm{car}}b>Q,
\qquad
b\le\Lambda X_Q<B_{\mathrm{car}}X_Q\le Q.
\tag{3.3}\label{eq:carrier-exact}
\]
For $X_Q\ge1$ one also has
\[
B_{\mathrm{car}}X_Q\le Q<2B_{\mathrm{car}}X_Q,
\]
so $X_Q\asymp Q$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-supply-main}, applied at the integer cutoff $X_Q$, therefore gives $\asymp Q/\log Q$ possible carriers; imposing $(b,Q)=1$ removes at most the prime $p$.

For each carrier $b$, let $c_+\in\{1,\ldots,Q-1\}$ be the inverse of $b$ modulo $Q$, put $c_-=Q-c_+$, and write
\[
bc_+=Qk_++1,
\qquad
bc_-=Qk_--1.
\tag{3.4}\label{eq:signed-inverse}
\]
Then
\[
1\le k_\pm<b<Q,
\qquad
k_++k_-=b.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Since $p\nmid b$, the two coefficients cannot both be divisible by $p$.  Choose a sign deterministically: if $e=1$, take the larger coefficient; if $e\ge2$, take $+$ unless $p\mid k_+$, and take $-$ otherwise.  With $k=k(Q,b)$ the chosen coefficient, define
\[
I(Q,b)=
\begin{cases}
[Qk,Qk+1],&+,\\
[Qk-1,Qk],&-.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.6}\label{eq:signed-block}
\]
If $Q=p$ is prime, this rule gives
\[
k\ge b/2>X_Q/2\gg p.
\tag{3.7}\label{eq:atomic-k-lower}
\]

\subsection{Transversality and deterministic deduplication}

The distinguished denominator $Qk$ has largest prime-power stage exactly $Q$: the coefficient $k$ is a $p$-unit, and every prime-power factor contributed by $k$ has rank below $Q$ because $k<Q$.

For the companion denominator $bc_\pm=Qk\pm1$, both factors $b$ and $c_\pm$ are below $Q$.  Hence a new prime-power factor of rank at least $Q$ can occur only if the carrier prime occurs again in $c_\pm$, that is, if
\[
b\mid c_\pm.
\]
Equivalently $b^2\mid Qk\pm1$.  Write $c_\pm=\ell b$.  From $c_\pm<Q<B_{\mathrm{car}}b$ one obtains $1\le\ell<B_{\mathrm{car}}$, and \eqref{eq:signed-inverse} gives
\[
\ell b^2\equiv\pm1\pmod Q.
\tag{3.8}\label{eq:bad-carrier}
\]
For fixed $(\ell,\pm)$ this congruence has uniformly boundedly many unit roots modulo $p^e$: at most two when $p$ is odd and at most four when $p=2$.  Thus only $O_{B_{\mathrm{car}}}(1)$ carriers fail transversality.

Different carriers can produce the same coefficient $k$.  Every carrier producing that coefficient divides one of
\[
Qk-1,\qquad Qk+1,
\]
both below $Q^2$.  If $Q>B_{\mathrm{car}}^3$, no one of these integers can contain three distinct carrier primes, because each carrier exceeds $Q/B_{\mathrm{car}}$.  Thus the multiplicity of a coefficient is uniformly bounded.  Keep, for each coefficient, the occurrence with least carrier.  The resulting coefficient set still has cardinality
\[
\asymp Q/\log Q.
\tag{3.9}\label{eq:coef-scale}
\]

If $e\ge2$, order the remaining coefficients
\[
k_1<\cdots<k_N
\]
and retain the upper half.  Since $k_i\ge i$, every retained coefficient then satisfies
\[
k\gg Q/\log Q.
\tag{3.10}\label{eq:coef-lower}
\]
If $Q=p$ is prime, retain all coefficients.  Let $\mathcal A_Q$ be the resulting family of intervals \eqref{eq:signed-block}.

\subsection{The simple-quotient value}

Modulo $F_{<Q}$, the companion reciprocal has only smaller prime-power stages, so the class contributed by $I(Q,b)$ is the class of $1/(Qk)$.  Choose $u\in\mathbf Z$ with
\[
uk\equiv1\pmod p,
\qquad uk=1+pt.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{u}{Q}-\frac1{Qk}
=\frac{t}{p^{e-1}k}.
\tag{3.11}\label{eq:simple-coordinate-calculation}
\]
Every prime-power factor of the denominator on the right has rank below $Q$: the $p$-part is at most $p^{e-1}$, and every factor coming from $k$ is below $Q$.  Hence the difference belongs to $F_{<Q}$.  Relative to the canonical generator represented by $1/Q$, the simple-quotient map
\[
\phi_Q:\mathcal A_Q\longrightarrow S_Q^\times
\]
is therefore
\[
\phi_Q(I(Q,b))=k^{-1}\pmod p.
\tag{3.12}\label{eq:simple-value}
\]
The interval $I(Q,b)$ itself recovers $Q$ and $k$: exactly one of its two denominators has largest prime-power stage $Q$, namely $Qk$.  Thus the distinguished-point map $I(Q,b)\mapsto Qk$ is globally injective.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform estimates for the signed-inverse families]\label{prop:signed-inverse-capabilities}
There is $Q_0$ such that for every prime power $Q=p^e\ge Q_0$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is $\kappa>0$, independent of $Q$, for which
\[
|\mathcal A_Q|\ge \kappa\frac Q{\log Q}.
\tag{3.13}\label{eq:family-size}
\]
\item For every $s\in S_Q^\times$,
\[
|\phi_Q^{-1}(s)|\le Q/p.
\tag{3.14}\label{eq:fibre-bound}
\]
\item The incompatibility graph on $\bigcup_{Q\ge Q_0}\mathcal A_Q$ has uniformly bounded degree.  More generally, for every fixed $L\ge1$ there is $C_L$ such that an arbitrary interval envelope of cardinality at most $L$ is incompatible with at most $C_L$ members of the union.
\item Every support point $z$ of a member of $\mathcal A_Q$ satisfies
\[
c\frac{Q^2}{\log Q}\le z\le2Q^2
\tag{3.15}\label{eq:support-window}
\]
for a fixed $c>0$.  If $Q$ is prime then $z\gg Q^2$.
\item There are nonnegative numbers $\omega_Q$ such that every local correspondence formed from the family $\mathcal A_Q$ below has reciprocal mass at most $\omega_Q$, and
\[
\sum_Q\omega_Q<\infty.
\tag{3.16}\label{eq:role-summable}
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows from the carrier count, the bounded exceptional set, bounded coefficient multiplicity and, for $e\ge2$, the upper-half thinning.  For \eqref{eq:fibre-bound}, equality of simple-quotient values implies equality of $k$ modulo $p$.  A fixed nonzero residue class modulo $p$ occurs exactly $Q/p$ times among $1\le k<Q=p^e$, and the deterministic deduplication retains at most one interval for each coefficient.

For incompatibility, each interval occupies two adjacent integers around its globally injective distinguished point $Qk$.  If an external envelope has cardinality at most $L$, every incompatible member has its distinguished point in a fixed one-step enlargement of that envelope.  The enlargement contains $O(L)$ integer sites and global injectivity leaves at most one retained interval per site.

The support bounds follow from $z=Qk+O(1)$ together with \eqref{eq:coef-lower}; the prime lower bound follows from \eqref{eq:atomic-k-lower}.  Finally, a prime family has $O(p/\log p)$ intervals, each of reciprocal mass $O(p^{-2})$, so one may take
\[
\omega_p=O\!\left(\frac1{p\log p}\right).
\]
Summing over the fixed geometric prime bands from Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-supply-main} converges.  For $Q=p^e$, $e\ge2$, a local correspondence uses at most $p$ intervals, each of mass $O(\log Q/Q^2)$, so
\[
\omega_{p^e}
=O\!\left(\frac{p\log Q}{Q^2}\right)
=O\!\left(\frac{e\log p}{p^{2e-1}}\right).
\]
The sum over $e\ge2$ and then over $p$ is bounded by a constant multiple of $\sum_p p^{-2}$.
\end{proof}

In particular, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $Q_1$ such that every finite set of prime-power steps of rank at least $Q_1$ has total reciprocal-mass bound below $\varepsilon$.
