\section{Signed-inverse current families}
\label{sec:signed-inverse}

We now construct, simultaneously for all sufficiently late currents, the finite binary families that feed both the local additive channel and the global packing argument.  The construction is deterministic; all carrier, sign, and coefficient coordinates disappear at the theorem boundary.

Fix the integer comparable-prime multiplier $\Lambda$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-supply-main}, and put
\[
B_{\mathrm{car}}=\Lambda+1.
\]
Let the current rank be $Q=p^e$ and define the natural cutoff
\[
X_Q=Q\operatorname{div}B_{\mathrm{car}}.
\tag{3.1}\label{eq:carrier-base}
\]
Choose carrier primes
\[
X_Q<b\le\Lambda X_Q,
\qquad \gcd(b,Q)=1.
\tag{3.2}\label{eq:carrier-band}
\]
Writing
\[
Q=B_{\mathrm{car}}X_Q+r,
\qquad 0\le r<B_{\mathrm{car}},
\]
shows that every carrier satisfies the exact inequalities
\[
B_{\mathrm{car}}b>Q,
\qquad
b\le\Lambda X_Q<B_{\mathrm{car}}X_Q\le Q.
\tag{3.3}\label{eq:carrier-exact}
\]
For $X_Q\ge1$ one also has
\[
B_{\mathrm{car}}X_Q\le Q<2B_{\mathrm{car}}X_Q,
\]
so $X_Q\asymp Q$ with constants depending only on $B_{\mathrm{car}}$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-supply-main}, applied literally at the integer cutoff $X_Q$, therefore gives $\asymp Q/\log Q$ raw carriers; the coprimality condition removes at most the base prime $p$.

For each carrier $b$, let $c_+\in\{1,\ldots,Q-1\}$ be the inverse of $b$ modulo $Q$, put $c_-=Q-c_+$, and define
\[
bc_+=Qk_++1,
\qquad
bc_-=Qk_--1.
\tag{3.4}\label{eq:signed-inverse}
\]
Then
\[
1\le k_\pm<b<Q,
\qquad
k_++k_-=b.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Since $p\nmid b$, the two coefficients cannot both be divisible by $p$.  We choose an orientation deterministically: for $e=1$ take the larger coefficient; for $e\ge2$ take $+$ unless $p\mid k_+$, in which case take $-$.  Let $k=k(Q,b)$ be the selected coefficient and let the associated binary atom be
\[
a(Q,b)=
\begin{cases}
[Qk,Qk+1],&+,\\
[Qk-1,Qk],&-.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.6}
\]
In the atomic case $Q=p$, this rule gives
\[
k\ge b/2>X_Q/2\gg p.
\tag{3.7}\label{eq:atomic-k-lower}
\]

\subsection{Transversality and deterministic deduplication}

The distinguished denominator $Qk$ has maximal current exactly $Q$: the chosen coefficient is a $p$-unit, and every prime-power factor contributed by $k$ has smaller rank because $k<Q$.  The only possible obstruction is that the carrier $b$ also divides the companion denominator.  Then $c_\pm=\ell b$ for some integer $\ell\ge1$.  Since $c_\pm<Q<B_{\mathrm{car}}b$ by \eqref{eq:carrier-exact}, one has $1\le\ell<B_{\mathrm{car}}$, and \eqref{eq:signed-inverse} gives
\[
\ell b^2\equiv\pm1\pmod Q.
\tag{3.8}\label{eq:bad-carrier}
\]
For fixed $(\ell,\pm)$ this congruence has uniformly boundedly many unit roots modulo $p^e$: at most two for odd $p$ and at most four for $p=2$.  Hence only $O_{B_{\mathrm{car}}}(1)$ carrier candidates fail transversality.

Different carriers may produce the same coefficient.  For a fixed $k$, every such carrier divides one of
\[
Qk-1,\qquad Qk+1,
\]
both of which are $<Q^2$ for $1\le k<Q$ and $Q>1$.  On the late locus $Q>B_{\mathrm{car}}^3$, three distinct carriers $b_1,b_2,b_3$ would satisfy
\[
B_{\mathrm{car}}^3b_1b_2b_3>Q^3>B_{\mathrm{car}}^3Q^2,
\]
so $b_1b_2b_3>Q^2$, impossible if all three divide the same integer $Qk\pm1$.  Thus each coefficient has uniformly bounded carrier multiplicity.  Keep, for each coefficient, the occurrence with least carrier.  This gives a deterministic deduplicated coefficient set of cardinality
\[
\asymp Q/\log Q.
\tag{3.9}\label{eq:coef-scale}
\]

For a non-atomic current, order the distinct coefficients
\[
k_1<\cdots<k_N
\]
and keep the upper half.  Since $k_i\ge i$, every retained non-atomic coefficient satisfies
\[
k\gg Q/\log Q.
\tag{3.10}\label{eq:coef-lower}
\]
For an atomic current keep all coefficients.  Write $A_Q$ for the resulting finite set of transverse binary atoms.

\subsection{Simple values}

Modulo the lower endpoint stage $F_{<Q}$, the companion reciprocal has only lower currents, so the current-step class of the atom is the class of the distinguished term $1/(Qk)$.  To compute it, choose $u\in\mathbf Z$ with
\[
uk\equiv1\pmod p,
\qquad uk=1+pt.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{u}{Q}-\frac1{Qk}
=\frac{uk-1}{Qk}
=\frac{t}{p^{e-1}k}.
\tag{3.11}\label{eq:simple-coordinate-calculation}
\]
Every prime-power factor of the denominator on the right has rank strictly below $Q=p^e$: its $p$-part is at most $p^{e-1}$ and every prime-power factor coming from $k$ is $<Q$ because $k<Q$.  Thus the difference lies in $F_{<Q}$.  Relative to the canonical generator represented by $1/Q$, the simple-factor coordinate is therefore
\[
sv_Q(a)=u=k^{-1}\pmod p
\quad\in S_Q^\times.
\tag{3.12}\label{eq:simple-value}
\]
The atom itself recovers $Q$ and $k$: exactly one support denominator has maximal current $Q$, namely $Qk$.  Hence the map from retained atoms to distinguished centres $Qk$ is globally injective.

\begin{proposition}[Signed-inverse capabilities]\label{prop:signed-inverse-capabilities}
There is a late-current threshold beyond which the families $A_Q$ satisfy the following uniformly.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is $\kappa>0$ such that
\[
|A_Q|\ge \kappa\frac Q{\log Q}.
\tag{3.13}
\]
\item Every simple value is nonzero, and for every $s\in S_Q^\times$,
\[
|sv_Q^{-1}(s)|\le Q/p.
\tag{3.14}\label{eq:fibre-bound}
\]
\item The conflict graph on the union of all late rows has uniformly bounded degree.
\item Every support vertex $z$ of a retained atom satisfies
\[
c\frac{Q^2}{\log Q}\le z\le2Q^2
\tag{3.15}\label{eq:support-window}
\]
for a fixed $c>0$.  At prime currents one has the stronger lower scale $z\gg Q^2$.
\item There is a nonnegative row majorant $\operatorname{roleMass}(Q)$ such that every local current response uses reciprocal value at most $\operatorname{roleMass}(Q)$ and
\[
\sum_Q\operatorname{roleMass}(Q)<\infty.
\tag{3.16}\label{eq:role-summable}
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cardinality statement follows from the carrier count, bounded exceptional set, bounded coefficient multiplicity, and the upper-half thinning.  For \eqref{eq:fibre-bound}, equality of simple values implies equality of $k$ modulo $p$.  A fixed nonzero residue class modulo $p$ occurs exactly $Q/p$ times among the integers $1\le k<Q=p^e$; deterministic deduplication keeps at most one atom per coefficient.

For the conflict bound, each atom occupies two adjacent vertices around its globally injective centre $Qk$.  An interval envelope of bounded length can conflict only with centres in a fixed bounded enlargement of that envelope, so it conflicts with only $O(1)$ retained atoms across all currents.

The support window follows from $z=Qk+O(1)$ together with \eqref{eq:coef-lower}; the prime lower bound follows from \eqref{eq:atomic-k-lower}.  Finally, prime rows have $O(p/\log p)$ atoms of individual mass $O(p^{-2})$, so
\[
\operatorname{roleMass}(p)=O\!\left(\frac1{p\log p}\right).
\]
Grouping primes into the fixed geometric bands supplied by Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-supply-main} gives a convergent series.  For $Q=p^e$, $e\ge2$, a local response uses at most $p$ atoms, each of mass $O(\log Q/Q^2)$, hence
\[
\operatorname{roleMass}(p^e)
=O\!\left(\frac{p\log Q}{Q^2}\right)
=O\!\left(\frac{e\log p}{p^{2e-1}}\right).
\]
Summing first over $e\ge2$ and then over $p$ is bounded by a constant multiple of $\sum_p p^{-2}$.  This proves \eqref{eq:role-summable}.
\end{proof}

A useful consequence of \eqref{eq:role-summable} is the tail form: for every positive real $\varepsilon$ there is a rank cutoff $Q_0$ such that every finite set of currents of rank at least $Q_0$ has total role mass below $\varepsilon$.
