\section{Terminal exactification and the proof of Erdős Problem 289}
\label{sec:terminalization}

Only Set-level base change remains.  Let
\[
\mathcal T=
\sum_{X\in\operatorname{Horizon}_B}
\operatorname{Tor}_{E_X}(\gamma_X)\times I_X
\]
be the total target bundle.  If $\ell\in\operatorname{Lift}(X)$, its finite response $R_X$ covers the corresponding target fibre.  Define the universal edge object
\[
\mathcal E=
\{(X,\ell,t,e):
\ell\in\operatorname{Lift}(X),\
t\in T_X,\
e\in R_X\text{ lands at }t\}.
\]
By construction there is a surjection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-edge-surjection}
\mathcal E
\twoheadrightarrow
\operatorname{Real}\times_{\operatorname{Horizon}_B}\mathcal T.
\end{equation}
Every physical label in $\mathcal E$ has reciprocal value $<2$ by \eqref{eq:physical-W-less-2}.

Fix a sufficiently large grade $k$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-terminality} gives the nonempty terminal set
\[
\operatorname{Term}_k
=
\{X\in\operatorname{Horizon}_B:
\gamma_X=0,\ k\in I_X\}.
\]
Pull the realization surjection \eqref{eq:real-surjection} back along
\[
\operatorname{Term}_k\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Horizon}_B.
\]
Since surjections in $\mathbf{Set}$ are stable under pullback, we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Real}_k\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Term}_k.
\tag{8.1}\label{eq:real-k}
\]
For every $X\in\operatorname{Term}_k$, the target point
\[
(0,k)\in\operatorname{Tor}_{E_X}(0)\times I_X
\]
is canonical.  Pull \eqref{eq:universal-edge-surjection} back along this target section and \eqref{eq:real-k}.  Cartesian pasting gives a surjection
\[
\mathcal E_k\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Real}_k.
\tag{8.2}\label{eq:terminal-edge-cover}
\]
Since $\operatorname{Term}_k$ is nonempty, so is $\mathcal E_k$.  Any point of this finite solution object gives a literal physical state $S$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal-state}
\operatorname{res}(S)=0,
\qquad
g(S)=k,
\qquad
W(S)<2.
\end{equation}
No coherent choice over different $k$ is asserted or needed.

The observations fit into the commutative square
\[
\begin{CD}
\operatorname{Phys} @>{W}>> \mathbf Q\\
@V{\operatorname{res}}VV @VV{q_{\mathbf Z}}V\\
\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z @= \mathbf Q/\mathbf Z.
\end{CD}
\tag{8.3}\label{eq:value-residue-square}
\]
Thus $\operatorname{res}(S)=0$ implies
\[
W(S)\in\ker(q_{\mathbf Z})=\mathbf Z.
\]
Because $g(S)=k>0$, the state is nonempty, so $W(S)>0$.  Together with \eqref{eq:terminal-state},
\[
0<W(S)<2,
\qquad W(S)\in\mathbf Z,
\]
and therefore
\[
W(S)=1.
\]
The $k$ connected components of $S$ are pairwise disjoint, nonadjacent finite intervals of positive integers, each of length $2$ or $3$.  We have proved the public theorem.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:erdos289}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$, there exist exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint and nonadjacent finite intervals of positive integers, each of length at least $2$, such that the sum of the reciprocals of all integers in these intervals is $1$.
\end{theorem}
