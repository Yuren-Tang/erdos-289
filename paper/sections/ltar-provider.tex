\section{The reciprocal provider below the late-source response}
\label{sec:ltar-provider}

Theorem~\ref{thm:ltar-closed} is a regular-image projection of the stronger
BinaryTail theorem. We now prove the latter. Arithmetic coordinates are kept
only until they certify the intrinsic property consumed above them.

\subsection{Filtration and provider notation}

Throughout this section
\[
A=\Q/\Z.
\]
For a prime power $Q=p^e$, let
\[
J_Q:=\ker([Q]:A\to A).
\]
Ordering prime powers by their numerical value, define the cumulative compact
filtration
\[
F_{<Q}:=\bigvee_{R<Q,\ R\text{ a prime power}}J_R,
\qquad
F_Q:=F_{<Q}\vee J_Q,
\]
and its simple factor
\[
S_Q:=F_Q/F_{<Q}.
\]
The group $S_Q$ has prime order $p$. We call $Q$ a \emph{current}. A binary
physical atom is \emph{filtered-transverse at $Q$} if its centered residue lies
in $F_Q$ and has nonzero image in $S_Q$.

A \emph{raw reciprocal row system} $R$ is a family of finite binary rows
$(R_Q)_Q$ on a cofinal set of currents, with the filtered-transversality,
finite-conflict, simple-value and resource properties established below. Its
eligible source filter $\mathcal F_R$ is the cofinal filter of finite compact
stages beyond which these uniform properties hold.

For an actual positive reciprocal state $x$ write
\[
W_+(x):=W(x).
\]
For a finite normalized family $F$, $W_+(F)$ denotes the maximum of $W(x)$ over
its branches. Thus $W_+(F)\le\varepsilon$ is exactly a branchwise reciprocal-mass
bound.

A finite obstacle $O$ is \emph{$c$-transparent} at a source if, after removing
raw occurrences incompatible with $O$, every sufficiently late row used by the
finite-endpoint construction, including the required boundary-predecessor
rows, retains at least a fixed relative proportion $c>0$ before the subsequent
bounded quota packing. Only the existence of such a positive proportion is
used below.

The dependency is
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& \text{comparable-prime supply} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{signed-inverse transverse reservoirs} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{bounded-degree physical packing} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{bounded-thinning local role supply} \arrow[d] &\\
\text{prime-order restricted fold} \arrow[dr]
&& \text{equal-value resolver slots} \arrow[dl]\\
& \text{full local simple-factor covers} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{filtered torsor lifting} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{same-witness grade/resource aggregation} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{BinaryTail} \arrow[d] &\\
& \mathrm{LTAR}_{289}. &
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{T1}\label{eq:tail-dag}
\]

Only four boxes contain non-formal mathematics specific to their natural
subjects: prime distribution, the signed-inverse reciprocal construction,
finite graph packing, and the restricted-fold theorem. The remaining arrows
are finite-limit, image, filtration, and summability arguments.

\subsection{Prime-order restricted folds}

Let $S$ be a finite additive group of prime order $p$ and let
\[
A_0\hookrightarrow S,
\qquad |A_0|=r.
\]
For $0\le h\le r$, addition induces
\[
\Sigma_h:\operatorname{FinSub}_h(A_0)\longrightarrow S,
\qquad
B\longmapsto\sum_{a\in B}a.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Restricted fold]\label{thm:restricted-fold}
If
\[
h(r-h)+1\ge p,
\]
then $\Sigma_h$ is surjective.
\end{theorem}

This is the restricted-sum theorem of Dias da Silva and Hamidoune
\cite{DiasDaSilvaHamidoune1994} in exactly the form consumed here. A
self-contained polynomial proof of the stronger image bound is included in
Appendix~\ref{app:restricted-fold}. The theorem statement itself depends only
on the prime-order additive group; no chosen field coordinate survives.

Write
\[
I_D(p,r)
=\{0\le h\le r:p\le h(r-h)+1\}.
\tag{T2}\label{eq:d-threshold}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Supercritical interval]\label{lem:supercritical}
Fix $0<\theta<1/2$. For all sufficiently large fixed $C$ and all
sufficiently large $r$, the implication
\[
Cp\le r^2
\]
gives
\[
[t,r-t]\subseteq I_D(p,r),
\qquad
t=\left\lceil\frac{2p}{r}\right\rceil,
\qquad t\le\theta r.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We have
\[
t\le\frac{2p}{r}+1\le\frac{2r}{C}+1.
\]
Choose $C$ and then $r$ so that the right side is at most $\theta r$. Thus
$t\le r/2$. Since $t\ge2p/r$,
\[
t(r-t)\ge tr/2\ge p.
\]
The function $h(r-h)$ is increasing up to $r/2$ and symmetric about $r/2$,
so the whole displayed interval is supported.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The signed-inverse transverse reservoir}

Fix a current $Q=p^e$. Choose the dilation $\Lambda>1$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-band} and then a constant $B>\Lambda$. For large
$Q$, let the carrier $b$ range over primes in
\[
\frac QB<b\le\frac{\Lambda Q}{B}<Q.
\tag{T3}\label{eq:carrier-band}
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-band}, applied at $X=Q/B$, gives
\[
\#\{b\}=\Theta\!\left(\frac Q{\log Q}\right).
\tag{T4}\label{eq:carrier-count}
\]

Every sufficiently late carrier is coprime to $Q$. Indeed, if $e=1$, then
$b<Q=p$, so $b\ne p$. If $e\ge2$, then eventually
\[
\frac QB>p
\]
because $p^{e-1}>B$, hence $b>p$ and again the prime $b$ is different from
$p$. Thus $b$ has an inverse modulo $Q$.

For one carrier, let $c_+\in(0,Q)$ be the inverse of $b$ modulo $Q$ and set
\[
c_-=Q-c_+.
\]
Define
\[
bc_+=Qk_++1,
\qquad
bc_-=Qk_--1.
\tag{T5}\label{eq:signed-inverse}
\]
Then
\[
k_++k_-=b,
\qquad
1\le k_\pm<b<Q.
\tag{T6}\label{eq:complementary-k}
\]
At least one of $k_+,k_-$ is a $p$-unit: otherwise $p$ would divide their sum
$b$. Choose such an orientation. The two integers
\[
Qk_\sigma,\qquad bc_\sigma=Qk_\sigma+\sigma1
\]
are consecutive; their two-point support is our binary atom.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded downwardness deletion]\label{lem:downward}
Apart from $O_B(1)$ carriers, the chosen atom has current $Q$ as the unique
largest maximal prime-power factor of its distinguished denominator, and every
other maximal prime-power factor in either denominator is strictly smaller
than $Q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The only possible failure is $b\mid c_\sigma$. Since $b>Q/B$ and
$0<c_\sigma<Q$, this forces
\[
c_\sigma=\lambda b,
\qquad1\le\lambda<B.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:signed-inverse} gives
\[
\lambda b^2\equiv\sigma1\pmod Q.
\]
For fixed $(\lambda,\sigma)$ this unit quadratic congruence has uniformly
boundedly many roots modulo $p^e$; the carrier interval has length $<Q$, so
each residue root contributes at most one carrier integer. There are only
finitely many $(\lambda,\sigma)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded coefficient multiplicity]\label{lem:coeff-mult}
A fixed coefficient $k$ arises from only $O_B(1)$ carrier/orientation pairs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every plus-carrier producing $k$ divides $Qk+1<Q^2+1$, while every
minus-carrier divides $Qk-1<Q^2$. All carriers exceed $Q/B$. A product of more
than a fixed number of distinct such primes exceeds $Q^2+1$.
\end{proof}

After Lemmas~\ref{lem:downward} and \ref{lem:coeff-mult} we retain
\[
N_Q\gg Q/\log Q
\]
distinct $p$-unit coefficients. Arrange them increasingly and keep their upper
half. Then
\[
k\gg Q/\log Q,
\qquad
Qk\gg Q^2/\log Q,
\]
and every retained atom has mass
\[
W_+(a)=O(\log Q/Q^2).
\tag{T7}\label{eq:generic-atom-mass}
\]
For $Q=p$ prime, choose the larger of $k_+,k_-$. Since their sum is
$b\asymp p$, one obtains
\[
k\asymp p,
\qquad
W_+(a)=O(p^{-2}).
\tag{T8}\label{eq:prime-atom-mass}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Simple-factor value]\label{lem:simple-factor-value}
Let $k$ be the selected $p$-unit coefficient, and choose an integer $u$ with
\[
uk\equiv1\pmod p.
\]
The image of the corresponding binary atom in $S_Q=F_Q/F_{<Q}$ is
\[
u[1/Q].
\]
In particular it is nonzero. After identifying the prime-order quotient with
$\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z$ by the generator $[1/Q]$, its coordinate is
$k^{-1}\pmod p$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the distinguished reciprocal term,
\[
\frac1{Qk}-\frac uQ
=\frac{1-uk}{Qk}.
\tag{T9}\label{eq:simple-factor-reduction}
\]
Because $p\mid(1-uk)$, the reduced denominator of the right-hand side divides
$p^{e-1}k$. Every maximal prime-power factor of $k$ is strictly smaller than
$Q$ because $k<Q$, and the $p$-primary factor has height at most $p^{e-1}$.
Hence the right-hand side represents a point of $F_{<Q}$. Therefore
\[
[1/(Qk)]=u[1/Q]\quad\text{in }S_Q.
\]

By Lemma~\ref{lem:downward}, every maximal prime-power factor of the companion
denominator $bc_\sigma=Qk+\sigma1$ is also strictly smaller than $Q$. Thus
$[1/(bc_\sigma)]\in F_{<Q}$ and contributes zero in $S_Q$. The whole binary
atom consequently has image $u[1/Q]$.

The class $[1/Q]$ generates the new order-$p$ quotient, and $p\nmid u$ because
$uk\equiv1\pmod p$. Hence $u[1/Q]\ne0$.
\end{proof}

A fixed simple-factor value determines $k$ modulo $p$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:simple-factor-value}. Among integers $1\le k<Q$, one residue
class modulo $p$ contains at most $\lceil Q/p\rceil$ values. Combining this
with Lemma~\ref{lem:coeff-mult} gives the required $O(Q/p)$ bound on each
simple-value fibre of raw occurrences. At a prime current $Q=p$, distinct
retained coefficients therefore have distinct simple values.

Finally the distinguished centre $Qk$ is globally injective over all retained
current rows: its unique largest maximal prime-power factor recovers $Q$ and
then $k$. The physical envelope of an atom has uniformly bounded diameter.
Thus the conflict graph of all candidate atoms has uniformly bounded degree.

\begin{proposition}[Filtered transverse reservoirs]
\label{prop:transverse-reservoir}
For every sufficiently late current $Q=p^e$ there is a finite row $R_Q$ of
binary physical atoms such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item every atom is filtered-transverse at $Q$;
\item $|R_Q|\to\infty$ with $Q$;
\item the simple-value fibres satisfy the bounded-thinning estimates required
below;
\item after every fixed bounded thinning the relevant minimal, first-successor,
and deep-successor role subobjects remain inhabited;
\item the global conflict graph has uniformly bounded degree;
\item the canonical role-resource tail is summable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The quantitative estimates above prove the proposition; they are not fields of
its interface.

\subsection{Finite graph strictification}

\begin{theorem}[Quota packing]\label{thm:quota-packing}
For every maximum degree $\Delta$ there is $L=L(\Delta)$ such that every
finite graph of maximum degree at most $\Delta$, equipped with a finite row
partition, has an independent set meeting row $R_i$ in at least
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{|R_i|}{L}\right\rfloor
\]
vertices.
\end{theorem}

One may take $L(0)=1$ and $L(\Delta)=2\Delta$ for $\Delta>0$. The underlying
independent-transversal theorem is due to Haxell \cite{Haxell1995}; for
completeness Appendix~\ref{app:packing} gives a self-contained finite proof and
derives the displayed quota form.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:quota-packing} once to the global conflict graph of all
rows participating below a finite endpoint, together with all boundary rows
required by the filtration. We obtain one globally independent packed pool.
All later local state families are assembled from disjoint subsets of this
same pool. Hence physical compatibility is settled before any local additive
fold is taken.

A fixed finite obstacle deletes only finitely many occurrences globally, while
late raw rows tend to infinity. Prefix transparency and quota packing are
therefore only bounded thinnings, and their thinning denominators multiply.

\subsection{Local role supply after bounded thinning}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-thinning role supply]
\label{prop:roles}
Fix a thinning denominator $L$ and a constant $C>0$. At every sufficiently
late current the packed row contains the following disjoint data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If the current is minimal in its primary $p$-power chain, a
representative block $\mathrm{Rep}$ on which the simple-value map is injective
and
\[
Cp\le |\mathrm{Rep}|^2.
\]
\item If its immediate predecessor is minimal, disjoint blocks
$\mathrm{Rep}$ and $\mathrm{Export}$ with
\[
Cp\le |\mathrm{Rep}|^2,
\qquad
|\mathrm{Export}|=p-1,
\]
and simple value injective on $\mathrm{Rep}$.
\item If its immediate predecessor is non-minimal, disjoint blocks
$\mathrm{Own}$ and $\mathrm{Export}$, both of cardinality $p-1$, with
$\mathrm{Own}$ contained in one nonzero simple-value fibre.
\end{enumerate}
The associated role resource is summable along the current filtration.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only this proof uses the explicit rates. Bounded thinning preserves a fixed
fraction of the raw row. At a prime current the simple-value map is injective
and $p/\log p$ eventually dominates $\sqrt{Cp}$. At $Q=p^2$, every
simple-value fibre has size $O(p)$; after reserving $p-1$ export points,
bounded-fibre counting leaves an image of size $\gg p/\log p$, again enough
for a representative block of size $\asymp\sqrt{Cp}$. At $Q=p^e$, $e\ge3$,
the raw capacity is eventually much larger than $(p-1)^2$; after reserving
$p-1$ export points, pigeonhole over the $p-1$ nonzero simple values gives a
fibre containing $p-1$ own points.

The resource assertion follows from \eqref{eq:generic-atom-mass} and
\eqref{eq:prime-atom-mass}; the resulting role costs are dominated by the
convergent positive series verified in Section~\ref{subsec:resource-sum}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local covers}

For a minimal current, or a current immediately above one, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:restricted-fold} to the injective representative image.
Lemma~\ref{lem:supercritical} gives a nonempty interval of supported grades and
a regular-epimorphic cover of the entire simple quotient at every such grade.

For a deeper current $Q=p^e$ let $c\ne0$ be the common simple value of the own
block. If the primary predecessor $p^{e-1}$ lies above the chosen source, take
$N$ to be the $p-1$ predecessor occurrences reserved by its preceding export.
If the current is the first deep current crossing the source boundary, include
the predecessor row $p^{e-1}$ as an auxiliary boundary row in the one global
packing and take $N$ from that row. The definition of a transparent obstacle
includes exactly this boundary-predecessor requirement. In either case $N$ is
disjoint from the current roles, has cardinality $p-1$, and is current-neutral
because its residue factors through $F_{<Q}$.

At grade $p-1$ use the family
\[
\coprod_{j=0}^{p-1}
\operatorname{FinSub}_j(\mathrm{Own})
\times
\operatorname{FinSub}_{p-1-j}(N).
\tag{T10}\label{eq:deep-resolver}
\]
Its simple value is $jc$. As $j$ runs from $0$ to $p-1$, these values exhaust
the prime-order simple factor. Thus every deeper current has a full local
cover, although only at one resolver grade.

The construction is made inside one global packed witness. Consequently all
local covers selected later are simultaneously physically compatible.

\subsection{Filtered torsor lifting}

Associated-graded values alone do not determine lower residue shadows. For
example, in
\[
H_p\le H_{p^2}\le\Q/\Z,
\]
the points $1/p^2$ and $(1+p)/p^2$ have the same image modulo $H_p$ but differ
by $1/p$ in the lower stage. There is no canonical splitting of the
filtration.

\begin{lemma}[Filtered torsor lifting]\label{lem:torsor-lifting}
Let
\[
H_0\le H_1\le\cdots\le H_m
\]
be a finite chain in an abelian group. Suppose $I_i\subseteq H_i$ satisfies
\[
I_i+H_{i-1}=H_i
\tag{T11}\label{eq:torsor-local-cover}
\]
for every $i$. If $T_0$ is an $H_0$-torsor, then
\[
T_0+I_1+\cdots+I_m
\]
is an $H_m$-torsor.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $T=t+H_{i-1}$ at one induction step. Then
\[
T+I_i
=t+(H_{i-1}+I_i)
=t+H_i
\]
by \eqref{eq:torsor-local-cover}. Thus adding the local image changes an
$H_{i-1}$-torsor into an $H_i$-torsor. Induction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

For the centered BinaryTail construction, start from the zero source torsor
$T_0=H_{\rm src}\subseteq A$. Order the finitely many simple compact jumps
between the source and endpoint to obtain
\[
H_{\rm src}=H_0\le H_1\le\cdots\le H_m=H_{\rm end}.
\]
At each jump the local regular-epimorphic correction cover says precisely that
its image $I_i$ maps onto $H_i/H_{i-1}$, equivalently
$I_i+H_{i-1}=H_i$. Lemma~\ref{lem:torsor-lifting} therefore gives the full
endpoint torsor $H_{\rm end}$. Modulo $H_{\rm src}$ this is exactly the entire
required zero-correction fibre $H_{\rm end}/H_{\rm src}$. Literal
matched-realizer composition simultaneously keeps the actual lower correction
forced by each later realizer, so no section $H_i/H_{i-1}\to H_i$ is used.

\subsection{Grade and resource aggregation}

Fix a sufficiently late source and a finite endpoint. For one common packed
witness, each current has an intrinsic supported-grade set: an interval for
the width-producing currents, a nonempty restricted-fold interval for the
first resolver layer, and a singleton resolver grade for deeper layers. Their
finite Minkowski sum is an integer interval
\[
[A(X),B(X)]\cap\N.
\]
The exact grade-tuple object is the full fibre
\[
\left\{(h_J)_J:
 h_J\in I_J,\quad\sum_Jh_J=h\right\}
\longrightarrow [A(X),B(X)]\cap\N,
\tag{T12}\label{eq:exact-grade-tuple}
\]
and is regular epi by definition of the Minkowski sum. No tuple is chosen.

The quantitative comparison is written out in Section~\ref{subsec:grade-overlap}:
a fixed-ratio band of atomic currents supplies width of order
$Y^{3/2}/\log Y$, while the next lower-endpoint displacement has a strictly
smaller leading coefficient after the canonical supercritical trimming, and
the first- and deep-successor displacements are lower-order. Hence the endpoint
intervals eventually overlap or are adjacent, and their union is a final
subset of $\N$.

For one exact grade tuple, use the actual local covers belonging to that same
tuple and same global packing. Their branchwise mass majorants add.
Section~\ref{subsec:resource-sum} proves that the resulting nonnegative current
series converges and therefore that its tail tends to zero with the source.

\subsection{Same-witness physical composition}

For a requested grade $h$ in the endpoint interval, work over the full fibre
\eqref{eq:exact-grade-tuple}. A generalized point contains the same global
packing, the same disjoint role refinements, and the actual normalized local
cover for every chosen $h_J$. All local physical states are therefore
compatible.

Compose this literal tuple physically first. Additivity gives total component
profile $(h,0)$ and the branchwise resource bound above. Filtered torsor
lifting and the local simple-factor epimorphisms show that the composite covers
the full centered correction from the source stage to the chosen endpoint.
Only after this physical union do we take the regular image/CoverCore and
forget packing, roles, local grade choices, and endpoint presentation.

\begin{theorem}[BinaryTail]\label{thm:binary-tail}
For every positive tolerance and transparency parameter, eventually in the
eligible source filter, for every finite transparent obstacle and every finite
compact endpoint request, all sufficiently large binary grades admit a
compatible centered physical correction cover whose total branchwise reciprocal
mass is at most the prescribed tolerance.
\end{theorem}

The preceding construction proves Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-tail}. Applying the
target-restriction projection of Section~\ref{sec:germs} yields
Theorem~\ref{thm:ltar-closed}.
