\section{The reciprocal provider below the late-source response}
\label{sec:ltar-provider}

Theorem~\ref{thm:ltar-closed} is a regular-image projection of the stronger
BinaryTail theorem.  We now prove the latter.  The proof is deliberately
organized so that arithmetic coordinates disappear as soon as they have
certified the intrinsic property consumed above them.

The dependency is
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& \text{comparable-prime supply} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{signed-inverse transverse reservoirs} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{bounded-degree physical packing} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{bounded-thinning local role supply} \arrow[d] &\\
\text{prime-order restricted fold} \arrow[dr]
&& \text{equal-value resolver slots} \arrow[dl]\\
& \text{full local simple-factor covers} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{filtered torsor lifting} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{same-witness grade/resource aggregation} \arrow[d] &\\
& \text{BinaryTail} \arrow[d] &\\
& \mathrm{LTAR}_{289}. &
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.1}
\]

Only four boxes contain non-formal mathematics specific to their natural
subjects: prime distribution, the signed-inverse reciprocal construction,
finite graph packing, and the restricted-fold theorem.  The remaining arrows
are finite-limit, image, filtration, and summability arguments.

\subsection{Prime-order restricted folds}

Let $S$ be a finite additive group of prime order $p$ and let
\[
A\hookrightarrow S,
\qquad |A|=r.
\]
For $0\le h\le r$, addition induces
\[
\Sigma_h:\operatorname{FinSub}_h(A)\longrightarrow S,
\qquad
B\longmapsto\sum_{a\in B}a.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Restricted fold]\label{thm:restricted-fold}
If
\[
h(r-h)+1\ge p,
\]
then $\Sigma_h$ is surjective.
\end{theorem}

This is the Dias da Silva--Hamidoune theorem in exactly the form consumed by
the proof.  The statement depends only on the prime-order additive group.  A
coordinate $S\cong\Z/p\Z$, polynomial method, or field structure belongs only
to a proof of the theorem and not to the interface.

We shall repeatedly use the supported-grade interval
\[
I_D(p,r)
=
\{0\le h\le r:p\le h(r-h)+1\}.
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.2}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Supercritical interval]\label{lem:supercritical}
Fix $0<\theta<1/2$.  For all sufficiently large fixed $C$ and all
sufficiently large $r$, the implication
\[
Cp\le r^2
\]
gives
\[
[t,r-t]\subseteq I_D(p,r),
\qquad
t=\left\lceil\frac{2p}{r}\right\rceil,
\qquad t\le\theta r.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We have
\[
t\le\frac{2p}{r}+1\le\frac{2r}{C}+1.
\]
Choose $C$ and then $r$ so that the right side is at most $\theta r$.
Thus $t\le r/2$.  Since $t\ge2p/r$,
\[
t(r-t)\ge tr/2\ge p.
\]
The function $h(r-h)$ is increasing up to $r/2$ and symmetric about $r/2$,
so the whole displayed interval is supported.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The signed-inverse transverse reservoir}

Fix a prime-power current
\[
Q=p^e.
\]
Choose constants $\Lambda>1$ and $B>\Lambda$ once and for all.  For large $Q$
let the carrier $b$ range over primes in
\[
\frac QB<b\le\frac{\Lambda Q}{B}<Q.
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.3}
\]
The standard prime-number estimates give
\[
\#\{b\}=\Theta\!\left(\frac Q{\log Q}\right).
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.4}
\]

For one carrier, let $c_+\in(0,Q)$ be the inverse of $b$ modulo $Q$ and set
\[
c_-=Q-c_+.
\]
Define
\[
bc_+=Qk_++1,
\qquad
bc_-=Qk_--1.
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.5}
\]
Then
\[
k_++k_-=b,
\qquad
1\le k_\pm<b<Q.
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.6}
\]
At least one of $k_+,k_-$ is a $p$-unit.  Choose such an orientation.  The two
integers
\[
Qk_\sigma,\qquad bc_\sigma=Qk_\sigma+\sigma1
\]
are consecutive; their two-point support is our binary atom.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded downwardness deletion]\label{lem:downward}
Apart from $O_B(1)$ carriers, the chosen atom has current $Q$ as the unique
largest maximal prime-power factor of its distinguished denominator and every
other maximal prime-power factor in either denominator is strictly smaller
than $Q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The only possible failure is $b\mid c_\sigma$.  Since
$b>Q/B$ and $0<c_\sigma<Q$, this forces
\[
c_\sigma=\lambda b,
\qquad1\le\lambda<B.
\]
Equation~\eqref{sec:ltar-provider}.5 then gives
\[
\lambda b^2\equiv\sigma1\pmod Q.
\]
For fixed $(\lambda,\sigma)$ this unit quadratic congruence has uniformly
boundedly many roots modulo $p^e$; the carrier interval has length $<Q$, so
each residue root contributes at most one carrier integer.  There are only
finitely many $(\lambda,\sigma)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded coefficient multiplicity]\label{lem:coeff-mult}
A fixed coefficient $k$ arises from only $O_B(1)$ carrier/orientation pairs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every plus-carrier producing $k$ divides $Qk+1<Q^2+1$, while every
minus-carrier divides $Qk-1<Q^2$.  All carriers exceed $Q/B$.  A product of
more than a fixed number of distinct such primes exceeds $Q^2+1$.
\end{proof}

After Lemmas~\ref{lem:downward} and \ref{lem:coeff-mult} we retain
\[
N_Q\gg Q/\log Q
\]
distinct $p$-unit coefficients.  Arrange them increasingly and keep their
upper half.  Then
\[
k\gg Q/\log Q,
\qquad
Qk\gg Q^2/\log Q,
\]
and every retained atom has mass
\[
W_+(a)=O(\log Q/Q^2).
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.7}
\]
For $Q=p$ prime, choose the larger of $k_+,k_-$.  Since their sum is
$b\asymp p$, one obtains the sharper
\[
k\asymp p,
\qquad
W_+(a)=O(p^{-2}).
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.8}
\]

The intrinsic simple-factor value of the atom is nonzero in
\[
S_Q=F_Q/F_{<Q}.
\]
In the coordinates above it is $k^{-1}\pmod p$, but that formula is discarded
after transversality has been proved.  One simple value has at most $O(Q/p)$
preimages among the raw occurrences.

Finally the distinguished centre $Qk$ is globally injective over all retained
current rows: its unique largest maximal prime-power factor recovers $Q$ and
then $k$.  The physical envelope of an atom has uniformly bounded diameter.
Thus the conflict graph of all candidate atoms has uniformly bounded degree.

We record what survives.

\begin{proposition}[Filtered transverse reservoirs]
\label{prop:transverse-reservoir}
For every sufficiently late prime-power current $Q=p^e$ there is a finite row
$R_Q$ of binary physical atoms such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item every atom is filtered-transverse at $Q$;
\item $|R_Q|\to\infty$ with $Q$;
\item the simple-value fibres satisfy the bounded-thinning estimates required
below;
\item after every fixed bounded thinning the relevant atomic, first-successor,
and deep-successor role subobjects remain inhabited;
\item the global conflict graph has uniformly bounded degree;
\item the canonical role-resource tail is summable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The quantitative estimates above are one proof of the proposition.  They are
not fields of the proposition.

\subsection{Finite graph strictification}

We use the following graph-theoretic theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Quota packing]\label{thm:quota-packing}
For every maximum degree $\Delta$ there is $L=L(\Delta)$ such that every
finite graph of maximum degree at most $\Delta$, equipped with a finite row
partition, has an independent set meeting row $R_i$ in at least
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{|R_i|}{L}\right\rfloor
\]
vertices.
\end{theorem}

One may take $L(0)=1$ and $L(\Delta)=2\Delta$ for $\Delta>0$: split each row
into disjoint chunks of size $2\Delta$ and apply the independent-transversal
theorem to the chunk partition.  A self-contained finite proof is recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:packing}.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:quota-packing} once to the global conflict graph of all
rows participating below a finite endpoint, together with all boundary rows
required by the filtration.  We obtain one globally independent packed pool.
All later local state families are assembled from disjoint subsets of this
same pool.  Hence physical compatibility is settled before any local additive
fold is taken.

A fixed finite obstacle deletes only finitely many occurrences globally, while
late raw rows tend to infinity.  Prefix transparency and quota packing are
therefore only bounded thinnings.  Their thinning denominators multiply.

\subsection{Local role supply after bounded thinning}

The following is the consumer-minimal consequence of the signed-inverse
estimates.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-thinning role supply]
\label{prop:roles}
Fix a thinning denominator $L$ and a constant $C>0$.  At every sufficiently
late current the packed row contains the following disjoint data.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If the current is minimal in its primary chain, a representative block
$\mathrm{Rep}$ on which the simple-value map is injective and
\[
Cp\le |\mathrm{Rep}|^2.
\]
\item If its immediate predecessor is minimal, disjoint blocks
$\mathrm{Rep}$ and $\mathrm{Export}$ with
\[
Cp\le |\mathrm{Rep}|^2,
\qquad
|\mathrm{Export}|=p-1,
\]
and simple value injective on $\mathrm{Rep}$.
\item If its immediate predecessor is non-minimal, disjoint blocks
$\mathrm{Own}$ and $\mathrm{Export}$, both of cardinality $p-1$, with
$\mathrm{Own}$ contained in one nonzero simple-value fibre.
\end{enumerate}
The associated role resource is summable along the current filtration.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only the proof uses the rates from the previous subsection.  Bounded thinning
preserves a fixed fraction of the raw row.  At a prime current the simple-value
map is injective and $p/\log p$ eventually dominates $\sqrt{Cp}$.  At
$Q=p^2$, every simple-value fibre has size $O(p)$; after reserving $p-1$
export points, bounded-fibre counting leaves an image of size
$\gg p/\log p$, again enough for a representative block of size
$\asymp\sqrt{Cp}$.  At $Q=p^e$, $e\ge3$, the raw capacity is eventually much
larger than $(p-1)^2$; after reserving $p-1$ export points, pigeonhole over the
$p-1$ nonzero simple values gives a fibre containing $p-1$ own points.

The resource assertion follows from the atom bounds
\eqref{sec:ltar-provider}.7--\eqref{sec:ltar-provider}.8; the resulting role
costs are dominated by convergent positive series.  In particular the total
resource beyond a sufficiently late source can be made smaller than any
prescribed $\varepsilon>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local covers}

For a minimal current, or a current immediately above one, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:restricted-fold} to the injective representative image.
Lemma~\ref{lem:supercritical} gives a long interval of supported grades and a
regular-epimorphic cover of the entire simple quotient at every such grade.

For a deeper current let $c\ne0$ be the common simple value of the own block
and let $N$ be a predecessor block of $p-1$ current-neutral atoms supplied by
the preceding export.  At grade $p-1$ use the family
\[
\coprod_{j=0}^{p-1}
\operatorname{FinSub}_j(\mathrm{Own})
\times
\operatorname{FinSub}_{p-1-j}(N).
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.9}
\]
Its simple value is $jc$.  As $j$ runs from $0$ to $p-1$, these values exhaust
the prime-order simple factor.  Thus every later current also has a full local
cover, although only at one resolver grade.

The construction is made inside one global packed witness.  Consequently all
local covers selected later are simultaneously physically compatible.

\subsection{Filtered torsor lifting}

The associated graded values alone do not determine lower residue shadows.
Indeed, in
\[
H_p\le H_{p^2}\le\Q/\Z,
\]
the points $1/p^2$ and $(1+p)/p^2$ have the same image modulo $H_p$ but differ
by $1/p$ in the lower stage.  There is no canonical splitting of the
filtration.

The correct replacement for triangular coordinates is the following.

\begin{lemma}[Filtered torsor lifting]\label{lem:torsor-lifting}
Let
\[
H_0\le H_1\le\cdots\le H_m
\]
be a finite chain in an abelian group.  Suppose $I_i\subseteq H_i$ satisfies
\[
I_i+H_{i-1}=H_i
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.10}
\]
for every $i$.  If $T_0$ is an $H_0$-torsor, then
\[
T_0+I_1+\cdots+I_m
\]
is an $H_m$-torsor.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $T$ is an $H_{i-1}$-torsor, then
\[
(T+I_i)+H_i
=T+(I_i+H_i)
=T+H_i,
\]
and \eqref{sec:ltar-provider}.10 says that every new quotient class occurs.
Induct on $i$.
\end{proof}

In physical terms, condition \eqref{sec:ltar-provider}.10 is supplied by the
local regular-epimorphic correction cover.  Literal matched-realizer
composition chooses whatever lower correction is forced by the actual later
realizer.  No section $H_i/H_{i-1}\to H_i$ and no upper-triangular coefficient
matrix is required.

\subsection{Grade aggregation}

Fix a sufficiently late source and a finite endpoint.  For one common packed
witness, each current has an intrinsic supported-grade set:

\begin{itemize}
\item a long interval for the width-producing currents;
\item a nonempty D-threshold interval for the first resolver layer;
\item a singleton resolver grade for deeper layers.
\end{itemize}

Their finite Minkowski sum is therefore an integer interval
\[
[A(X),B(X)]\cap\N.
\]
The exact grade-tuple object is the full fibre
\[
\left\{(h_J)_J:
 h_J\in I_J,\quad\sum_Jh_J=h\right\}
\longrightarrow [A(X),B(X)]\cap\N,
\tag{\ref{sec:ltar-provider}.11}
\]
and is regular epi by definition of the Minkowski sum.  No tuple is chosen.

To see eventual overlap, use the quantitative signed-inverse proof only at this
last provider layer.  A fixed-ratio band of large prime currents contains
$\gg Y/\log Y$ rows, each with enough surviving distinct simple values that
its D-supported interval has macroscopic width.  The resulting total width
dominates the lower displacement contributed by the next band and by the
proper-power resolver grades.  Consequently, for a geometric sequence of
endpoints,
\[
A(\Lambda Y)\le B(Y)+1
\]
for all sufficiently large $Y$.  The union of the endpoint intervals is
therefore a final subset of $\N$.

Equivalently, one may formulate this final comparison through the
bounded-thinning supercritical estimate of Proposition~\ref{prop:roles}; the
explicit $Q/\log Q$ rates used to verify that estimate do not belong to the
resulting response theorem.

\subsection{Resource aggregation}

For one exact grade tuple, take the actual local covers belonging to that same
tuple and same global packing.  Their resource bounds add.  The signed-inverse
certificate gives summable majorants; one convenient set of proof bounds is
\[
O\!\left(\frac1{p\log p}\right)
\quad(Q=p),
\]
\[
O\!\left(\frac{\log p}{p^3}\right)
\quad(Q=p^2),
\]
and
\[
O\!\left(\frac{e\log p}{p^{2e-3}}\right)
\quad(Q=p^e,\ e\ge3).
\]
The prime series converges by grouping primes into fixed-ratio bands and using
the corresponding upper prime-count estimate; the other two are dominated by
convergent integer/geometric series.  Hence
\[
\operatorname{TailResource}(B)\longrightarrow0
\]
as the source base $B$ tends to infinity.

This is precisely why the small-resource quantifier belongs outside the
source-eventual operator rather than to a fixed-source hom-object.

\subsection{Same-witness physical composition}

For a requested grade $h$ in the endpoint interval, work over the full fibre
\eqref{sec:ltar-provider}.11.  A generalized point contains the same global
packing, the same disjoint role refinements, and the actual normalized local
cover for every chosen $h_J$.  All local physical states are therefore
compatible.

Compose this literal tuple physically first.  Additivity gives total component
profile
\[
(h,0)
\]
and the resource bound obtained above.  Filtered torsor lifting and the local
simple-factor epimorphisms show that the composite covers the full centered
correction from the source stage to the chosen endpoint.  Only after this
physical union do we take the regular image/CoverCore and forget packing,
roles, local grade choices, and endpoint presentation.

We have proved the stronger theorem used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:ltar-closed}.

\begin{theorem}[BinaryTail]
\label{thm:binary-tail}
For every positive tolerance and transparency parameter, eventually in the
eligible source filter, for every finite transparent obstacle and every finite
compact endpoint request, all sufficiently large binary grades admit a
compatible centered physical correction cover whose total resource is at most
the prescribed tolerance.
\end{theorem}

Applying the target-restriction projection of
Section~\ref{sec:germs} to Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-tail} yields
Theorem~\ref{thm:ltar-closed}.
