% R16 direct targeted Tail provider.

\section{The direct source-filtered Tail provider}
\label{sec:ltar-provider}

The Tail root consumes one object only: a finite binary physical family of
prescribed grade whose residue hits one prescribed endpoint-source coset and
whose branchwise reciprocal mass is small.  We construct it directly from
local homogeneous simple covers.  All eventual statements in this section are
with respect to the ordinary filter $B\to\infty$ on $\N$; there is no separate
eligible-base predicate.

\subsection{Filtration and transverse raw rows}

Put
\[
A=\Q/\Z.
\]
For a prime power $Q=p^e$ let
\[
J_Q=\ker([Q]:A\to A),
\]
and define
\[
F_{<Q}=\bigvee_{R<Q,\ R\ {\rm prime\ power}}J_R,
\qquad
F_Q=F_{<Q}\vee J_Q,
\qquad
S_Q=F_Q/F_{<Q}.
\]
The new $p$-primary height is one step, so $S_Q$ has order $p$.  We call $Q$ a
\emph{current}.  A binary atom is filtered-transverse at $Q$ when its centered
residue lies in $F_Q$ and has nonzero image in $S_Q$.

Let $\Lambda>1$ be the fixed dilation supplied by
Appendix~\ref{app:prime-supply}.  Choose once $\kappa_0>1$ and put
$\kappa_1=\Lambda\kappa_0$.  The signed-inverse construction uses carrier
primes in
\[
Q/\kappa_1<b<Q/\kappa_0.
\]
Thus comparable-prime supply applies with $X=Q/\kappa_1$.
For a sufficiently large current $Q=p^e$ these carriers are different from
$p$.  Let $c_+$ be the inverse of $b$ modulo $Q$, put $c_-=Q-c_+$, and write
\[
bc_+=Qk_++1,
\qquad
bc_-=Qk_--1.
\tag{T1}\label{eq:signed-inverse-r14}
\]
Then
\[
k_++k_-=b,
\qquad
1\le k_\pm<b<Q,
\]
so at least one $k_\sigma$ is a $p$-unit.

For each sign, only $O(1)$ carriers fail filtered downwardness.  Indeed the
only way the companion denominator $bc_\sigma$ can acquire a maximal
prime-power factor not below $Q$ is that the carrier prime $b$ divides
$c_\sigma$.  Then
\[
c_\sigma=\lambda b,
\qquad 1\le\lambda<\kappa_1,
\]
and the inverse relation gives
\[
\lambda b^2\equiv\sigma1\pmod Q.
\]
For each fixed $(\lambda,\sigma)$ this unit quadratic congruence has uniformly
boundedly many roots modulo the prime power $Q$, and the carrier interval has
length $<Q$.  Deleting these finite exceptional sets leaves every admissible
$p$-unit orientation filtered downward.  The consecutive pair
\[
\{Qk_\sigma,Qk_\sigma+\sigma1\}
\]
is then a filtered-transverse binary atom.

Its simple value is intrinsic.  If $u k_\sigma\equiv1\pmod p$, then
\[
\frac1{Qk_\sigma}-\frac uQ
=
\frac{1-u k_\sigma}{Qk_\sigma}
\in F_{<Q},
\]
because the numerator removes one $p$-height and every prime-power factor of
$k_\sigma$ is below $Q$.  The companion reciprocal is also in $F_{<Q}$ by the
downwardness deletion.  Hence the simple-factor image is the nonzero class
$u[1/Q]\in S_Q$.

A fixed coefficient $k$ comes from only $O(1)$ carrier/orientation pairs.  For
such a pair the carrier prime divides one of
\[
Qk-1,\qquad Qk+1,
\]
which are $<Q^2+1$.  Since every carrier is $>Q/\kappa_1$, the product of more
than a fixed number of distinct carriers would exceed $Q^2+1$.  Thin to one
occurrence for each retained coefficient.  Comparable-prime supply, the
finite downwardness deletion and this bounded-multiplicity thinning give
\[
|R_Q|\gg Q/\log Q.
\tag{T2}\label{eq:raw-row-size}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Distinguished centres and bounded conflicts]
\label{lem:centres-conflicts}
For every retained signed-inverse atom at current $Q=p^e$, put
\[
\operatorname{ctr}(x)=Qk.
\]
The centre map is injective on the union of all retained rows.  Every atom has
support contained in
\[
[\operatorname{ctr}(x)-1,\operatorname{ctr}(x)+1],
\]
and the conflict graph of retained atoms has uniformly bounded maximum degree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $k<Q$ and $p\nmid k$, the prime power $Q=p^e$ is the unique largest
prime-power divisor of $Qk$: every prime-power divisor coming from $k$ is
strictly smaller than $Q$, while the $p$-height of $Qk$ is exactly $e$.
Hence the centre determines $Q$.  Inside one current the retained $k$ are
distinct, so the centre map is globally injective.

The atom is either $\{Qk,Qk+1\}$ or $\{Qk-1,Qk\}$, hence lies in the displayed
three-point interval.  If two such binary atoms overlap or are adjacent, their
centres differ by at most three.  Injectivity of the integer centres therefore
gives an absolute bound on the number of neighbours of any atom.
\end{proof}

For every proper current retain the upper half, by numerical value, of the
distinct usable coefficients.  Since there are $\gg Q/\log Q$ distinct
positive integers, the retained coefficients satisfy
\[
k\gg Q/\log Q.
\]
At a prime current $Q=p$, both $k_+$ and $k_-$ are $p$-units.  After the
simultaneous downwardness deletion take
\[
k^*=\max(k_+,k_-).
\]
Since $k_++k_-=b\asymp p$, bounded coefficient multiplicity leaves
$\gg p/\log p$ distinct coefficients with $k^*\asymp p$.
Consequently there are fixed constants $0<c_{\rm win}<C_{\rm win}$ such that
every retained centre at every sufficiently large current lies in the
\emph{current window}
\[
I_Q=
\left[c_{\rm win}\frac{Q^2}{\log Q},\ C_{\rm win}Q^2\right].
\tag{T3}\label{eq:tail-current-window}
\]
The corresponding branchwise reciprocal-mass bounds are
\[
O(p^{-2})\quad(Q=p),
\qquad
O(\log Q/Q^2)\quad(Q=p^e,\ e\ge2).
\tag{T4}\label{eq:raw-resource}
\]
At a prime current the retained simple values are distinct.  At $Q=p^2$, a
fixed simple value fixes $k$ modulo $p$; there are at most $p$ such integers
$1\le k<p^2$, and bounded coefficient multiplicity therefore gives a
simple-value fibre of size $O(p)$.

\subsection{Complete envelopes and sparse survival}

A physical state $x$ is \emph{compatible with a finite obstacle family $O$}
if every component of $x$ is disjoint and non-adjacent to every component that
occurs in any branch of $O$.  A family is obstacle-compatible when each of its
branches is.  This condition depends only on the complete footprint of $O$.

For a component interval $I=[a,b]$ define its complete conflict envelope by
\[
\operatorname{Env}(I)=[a-2,b+2]\cap\N_{>0}.
\]
For a finite obstacle family $O$, define $\operatorname{Env}(O)$ to be the
\emph{finite image set} of all complete component envelopes occurring in at
least one branch of $O$; repetitions of the same component or envelope in
different branches are identified.  Put
\[
N_Q(O)=
\#\{I\in\operatorname{Env}(O):I\cap I_Q\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
This branch-insensitive image is exactly the complete footprint consumed by
physical compatibility.  If a retained atom at current $Q$ conflicts with a
component $I$, then its centre lies in $\operatorname{Env}(I)$.  Since every
component has size at most three, each complete envelope contains only $O(1)$
integer centres; by Lemma~\ref{lem:centres-conflicts} it therefore kills only
$O(1)$ retained atoms.

For a source cutoff $B$, recall the boundary currents
\[
\operatorname{Boundary}(B)
=
\{Q=p^e:e\ge3,\ p^{e-1}\le B<p^e\}.
\]
Their predecessor ranks $Q^-=p^{e-1}$ tend to infinity uniformly with $B$ by
\eqref{eq:boundary-escape}.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform sparse survival]
\label{lem:sparse-survival}
For each $B$, let $\mathcal O_B$ be any collection of finite obstacle families.
Assume
\[
\sup_B\ \sup_{O\in\mathcal O_B} N_Q(O)
=o(Q/\log Q)
\qquad(Q\to\infty).
\tag{T5}\label{eq:obstacle-little-o}
\]
Then there are $c>0$ and $B_{\rm surv}$ such that for every
$B\ge B_{\rm surv}$ and every $O\in\mathcal O_B$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every current $Q>B$, deleting all atoms conflicting with $O$ leaves
at least $cQ/\log Q$ atoms in $R_Q$;
\item for every $Q\in\operatorname{Boundary}(B)$, the same conclusion holds
in the predecessor row $R_{Q^-}$ with $Q^-$ in place of $Q$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:raw-row-size}, on a current suffix there is $\kappa>0$ with
$|R_Q|\ge\kappa Q/\log Q$.  The preceding envelope argument shows that the
number deleted by any $O\in\mathcal O_B$ is $O(N_Q(O))$, with one absolute
implied constant.  By \eqref{eq:obstacle-little-o} this is eventually at most
$(\kappa/2)Q/\log Q$, simultaneously in $B$ and $O$.  Thus one may take any
fixed $c<\kappa/2$.
For boundary predecessors, their ranks escape to infinity uniformly as
$B\to\infty$, so the same current-suffix estimate applies; the double supremum
in \eqref{eq:obstacle-little-o} permits the obstacle even when the predecessor
rank lies below the numerical source cutoff.
\end{proof}

A finite obstacle is called $c$-transparent at source $E_B$ when the two
survival conclusions of Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-survival} hold.  Thus
transparency is an explicit row-survival property, not additional structure
stored on the obstacle.

After obstacle deletion, form one conflict graph on all surviving occurrences
in the finitely many current rows up to a proof-local endpoint, together with
any boundary-predecessor row needed at the source.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:centres-conflicts} its maximum degree is bounded absolutely.
Apply the quota-packing consequence of Haxell's theorem from
Appendix~\ref{app:packing} once.  The resulting packed rows are disjoint
subsets of one globally independent set and each retain a fixed positive
fraction of the surviving row.  Hence every finite subset selected later from
the packed universe is a physical binary state.

\subsection{Disjoint local allocations inside one packing}

The same packed row can have two simultaneous consumers: its own simple cover
and, for exponent at least two, a predecessor role for the next $p$-power.
These consumers are allocated before local families are formed.

\begin{lemma}[Disjoint allocation]
\label{lem:disjoint-allocation}
After increasing the source cutoff once, the packed rows may be refined so
that, for every $p^e$ with $e\ge2$, a successor-donor subpool of cardinality
$p-1$ is disjoint from an own-cover pool that still has cardinality
$\gg p^e/(e\log p)$ and inherits the simple-value-fibre bounds.  All such
refinements are made inside the one global independent packing.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The packed row has cardinality $\gg p^e/(e\log p)$, whereas
\[
\frac{p}{p^e/(e\log p)}
=\frac{e\log p}{p^{e-1}}
\longrightarrow0
\]
as the prime-power current $p^e\to\infty$ with $e\ge2$.  Take the full
finite-cardinality fibre of $(p-1)$-subsets as the donor allocation and use its
complement as the own pool.  Removing $p-1$ points changes neither the
asymptotic row bound nor the inherited upper bound on simple-value fibres.
Doing this dependently in each finite row up to the endpoint produces mutually
disjoint role subpools without selecting a section.  Since all subpools lie in
the already independent packing, cross-row and cross-role physical
compatibility is automatic.
\end{proof}

At a boundary predecessor only the donor subpool is consumed above the source.
Prime rows need no successor-donor role in the R14 construction.

\subsection{The common local interface}

Let
\[
F^-\le F,
\qquad S=F/F^-
\]
be one prime-order simple step.  A \emph{homogeneous simple cover} of grade
$m$ and reciprocal resource $\rho$ is a finite binary physical family $X$
such that every branch is obstacle-compatible, has grade exactly $m$, its
ordinary reciprocal mass $W$ is at most $\rho$, its observation lies in $F$,
and
\[
X\longrightarrow S
\]
is surjective.  For a finite positive binary family, the resource used in this
paper is therefore simply the branchwise maximum of $W$; no second resource
observable is present.

\subsection{Width-bearing prime currents}

Let $Q=p$ be prime.  After obstacle deletion and global packing, the simple
value image contains a set
\[
X_p\subset S_p\cong\mathbf F_p,
\qquad
N_p:=|X_p|\gg p/\log p
\]
of distinct points.  The dense convolution theorem of
Appendix~\ref{app:atomic-convolution} applies.  Choose once a proof-local
constant $C$ beyond its threshold and define
\[
\boxed{
m_p:=\left\lceil C(\log p)^3\right\rceil.}
\tag{T6}\label{eq:mp-definition}
\]
For every fixed $L>1$, eventually
\[
Lm_p\le N_p/2,
\]
and every
\[
M\in[m_p,Lm_p]
\tag{T7}\label{eq:atomic-grade-interval}
\]
has a homogeneous simple cover.  Pulling the $M$ disjoint two-point slots from
the convolution theorem back to packed physical atoms gives a family every
branch of which contains exactly one atom from each slot and therefore has
grade $M$.  Uniformly on this interval its reciprocal resource is
\[
O((\log p)^4/p^2).
\tag{T8}\label{eq:atomic-resource}
\]

\subsection{Proper prime powers}

We use one elementary prime-cyclic fact.  Translate $p-1$ two-point slots to
$\{0,d_i\}$ with $d_i\ne0$.  Starting from $A_0=\{0\}$, put
\[
A_i=A_{i-1}\cup(A_{i-1}+d_i).
\]
If $A_{i-1}\ne\mathbf F_p$, then $A_{i-1}+d_i\ne A_{i-1}$, because one
nonzero translation generates the cyclic group.  Hence the support grows by at
least one until it is all of $\mathbf F_p$.  Thus $p-1$ mutually compatible
same-grade two-point slots with nonzero simple difference form a homogeneous
simple cover.

At $Q=p^2$, first remove the successor-donor quota supplied by
Lemma~\ref{lem:disjoint-allocation}.  The remaining own pool still has
$\gg p^2/\log p$ atoms and every simple-value fibre has size $O(p)$.  In
particular no one value class contains half of the pool for sufficiently large
$p$; pairing atoms of different value therefore yields at least $p-1$
disjoint unequal-value pairs.  They form a cover of common grade $p-1$ and
reciprocal resource
\[
O(\log p/p^3).
\tag{T9}\label{eq:square-resource}
\]

For $Q=p^e$, $e\ge3$, choose $p-1$ atoms from the own pool at the current and
use the already reserved $p-1$ successor-donor atoms from the immediate
predecessor row $p^{e-1}$.  Predecessor observations lie in the source of the
new simple quotient and are current-neutral, whereas every current atom has
nonzero simple value.  Pairing them gives $p-1$ same-grade slots and hence a
full simple cover of grade $p-1$.  If the predecessor straddles the source
boundary, it is exactly one of the boundary rows covered by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-survival}.  A summable reciprocal-mass majorant is
\[
O\!\left(\frac{e\log p}{p^{2e-3}}\right)
\qquad(e\ge3).
\tag{T10}\label{eq:deep-resource}
\]

Above this subsection every proper prime power is represented only by a
homogeneous simple cover of grade $p-1$.

\subsection{Target-specific filtered lifting}

Let
\[
G_0\le G_1\le\cdots\le G_n
\]
be a finite source-to-endpoint filtration with prime-order factors
$S_i=G_i/G_{i-1}$.  Let
\[
v_i:X_i\to G_i
\]
be homogeneous simple-cover families such that $X_i\to S_i$ is surjective.

\begin{lemma}[Targeted filtered lifting]\label{lem:targeted-filtered-lifting}
For every generalized target
\[
r:T\to G_n/G_0,
\]
the object of tuples $(x_n,\ldots,x_1)$ satisfying
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n v_i(x_i)=r\pmod{G_0}
\tag{T11}\label{eq:targeted-match}
\]
projects surjectively to $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Project $r$ to the last simple quotient and pull back the surjection
$X_n\to S_n$.  Retain the full pullback fibre.  For an actual branch $x_n$,
the difference $r-v_n(x_n)$ lands canonically in $G_{n-1}/G_0$.  Repeat
through the filtration.  Every step is a pullback of a surjection, hence the
finite composite is surjective; at the bottom the residual lies in $G_0$.
No splitting or representative section is chosen.
\end{proof}

Because all local families are formed inside disjoint subpools of one global
independent packing, a matched tuple maps by literal physical union to a binary
family.  Local grades and branchwise reciprocal masses add.

\subsection{Grade finality}

Fix a sufficiently large source index $B$ and a finite numerical endpoint
$X>B$.  For each prime current choose independently
\[
M_p\in[m_p,Lm_p],
\]
while every proper prime power contributes its fixed resolver grade $p-1$.
The exact image of the local-grade addition map is one integer interval
\[
[A_B(X),B_{B,L}(X)]\cap\N.
\tag{T12}\label{eq:r14-grade-interval}
\]
No grade tuple is selected: for a requested total grade we retain the whole
addition fibre.

Comparable-prime supply gives
\[
\sum_{B<p\le X}m_p\asymp X(\log X)^2
\tag{T13}\label{eq:mp-sum}
\]
for $X$ sufficiently beyond the fixed source.  Proper prime powers add only a
lower-order shift: squares contribute $O(X)$, and exponents $e\ge3$ contribute
$O(X^{2/3}\log X)$.

Let $\Lambda>1$ be the fixed endpoint dilation from comparable-prime supply.
For one constant $C_{\rm step}>0$,
\[
A_B(\Lambda X)-A_B(X)
\le C_{\rm step}X(\log X)^2
\]
eventually, whereas for one constant $c_{\rm width}>0$,
\[
B_{B,L}(X)-A_B(X)
=(L-1)\sum_{B<p\le X}m_p
\ge c_{\rm width}(L-1)X(\log X)^2.
\]
Choose $L$ once with
$c_{\rm width}(L-1)>C_{\rm step}$.  Then
\[
A_B(\Lambda X)\le B_{B,L}(X)+1
\tag{T14}\label{eq:r14-overlap}
\]
eventually.  Consecutive endpoint intervals overlap or are adjacent and their
lower endpoints tend to infinity; their union is final in $\N$.

\subsection{Resource decay}

The prime-current majorant has convergent sum
\[
\sum_p\frac{(\log p)^4}{p^2}<\infty.
\]
The square and higher-power majorants are dominated by convergent
integer/geometric series.  Boundary-predecessor terms form a subseries whose
indices escape every fixed finite set as $B\to\infty$.  Hence the total
branchwise reciprocal mass beyond source $E_B$ is the tail of one convergent
positive series and tends to zero as $B\to\infty$.

\subsection{Direct late-source targeted residue response}

Let $E_B$ be a sufficiently late source, let $O$ be a $c$-transparent finite
obstacle there, and let
\[
r\in A/E_B.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-cofinal}, the point $r$ is represented in
$E_X/E_B$ for some proof-local finite endpoint $E_X\ge E_B$.  Start the
geometric endpoint sequence beyond $X$.  For each total grade in the final
interval, retain the full local-grade-tuple fibre and then apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:targeted-filtered-lifting} to the same local physical families.
The resulting matched family satisfies
\[
q_{E_B}(\operatorname{res}(t))=r
\]
for every branch $t$ and carries the same branchwise reciprocal-mass bound.

\begin{theorem}[Direct late-source targeted saturation]\label{thm:ltar-closed}
Fix $c>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$.  There exists $B_{\rm tail}$ such that for
every $B\ge B_{\rm tail}$, every $c$-transparent finite obstacle $O$ at source
$E_B$, and every $r\in A/E_B$, there is a grade threshold $h_0(B,O,r)$ such
that every $h\ge h_0(B,O,r)$ is realized by a finite obstacle-compatible
binary physical family of component profile $(h,0)$ satisfying
\[
q_{E_B}(\operatorname{res}(t))=r,
\qquad
W(t)<\varepsilon
\]
for every branch $t$.
\end{theorem}

The source threshold $B_{\rm tail}$ is uniform in $O$ and $r$; after $B$ is
fixed the grade threshold may depend on $O$ and $r$ through the proof-local
endpoint and finite packing problem.  This is exactly the quantifier order
forced by the fixed-source mesh obstruction.
