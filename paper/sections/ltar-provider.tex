% R14 direct targeted Tail provider.

\section{The direct source-filtered Tail provider}
\label{sec:ltar-provider}

The Tail root consumes one object only: a finite binary physical family of
prescribed grade whose residue hits one prescribed source coset and whose
branchwise reciprocal resource is small.  We construct it directly from local
homogeneous simple covers.  The historical full BinaryTail correction cover,
least absorber and restricted-sum theorem are not needed in this route.

\subsection{Filtration and transverse raw rows}

Put
\[
A=\Q/\Z.
\]
For a prime power $Q=p^e$ let
\[
J_Q=\ker([Q]:A\to A),
\]
and define
\[
F_{<Q}=\bigvee_{R<Q,\ R\ {m prime\ power}}J_R,
\qquad
F_Q=F_{<Q}\vee J_Q,
\qquad
S_Q=F_Q/F_{<Q}.
\]
The new $p$-primary height is one step, so $S_Q$ has order $p$.  We call $Q$ a
\emph{current}.  A binary atom is filtered-transverse at $Q$ when its centered
residue lies in $F_Q$ and has nonzero image in $S_Q$.

The signed-inverse construction supplies the physical raw rows.  Fix a large
current $Q=p^e$, choose a fixed comparable carrier interval below $Q$, and let
$b$ run over the carrier primes.  Since $b\ne p$, let $c_+$ be the inverse of
$b$ modulo $Q$, put $c_-=Q-c_+$, and write
\[
bc_+=Qk_++1,
\qquad
bc_-=Qk_--1.
\tag{T1}\label{eq:signed-inverse-r14}
\]
Then
\[
k_++k_-=b,
\qquad
1\le k_\pm<b<Q,
\]
so at least one $k_\sigma$ is a $p$-unit.  Apart from $O(1)$ carriers for each
sign, every prime-power factor of the companion denominator
$bc_\sigma=Qk_\sigma+\sigma1$ is below $Q$: a failure would force
$c_\sigma=\lambda b$ with bounded $\lambda$ and hence the unit quadratic
congruence
\[
\lambda b^2\equiv\sigma1\pmod Q,
\]
which has uniformly boundedly many roots.  Thus the consecutive pair
\[
\{Qk_\sigma,Qk_\sigma+\sigma1\}
\]
is a filtered-transverse binary atom.

Its simple value is intrinsic.  If $u k_\sigma\equiv1\pmod p$, then
\[
\frac1{Qk_\sigma}-\frac uQ
=
\frac{1-u k_\sigma}{Qk_\sigma}
\in F_{<Q},
\]
and the companion reciprocal also has lower filtration support.  Hence the
simple-factor image is the nonzero class $u[1/Q]\in S_Q$.

Comparable-prime supply and bounded coefficient multiplicity give
\[
|R_Q|\gg Q/\log Q
\tag{T2}\label{eq:raw-row-size}
\]
after the finite deletions, with uniformly bounded physical envelopes and a
globally injective distinguished centre.  One obtains the proof-only resource
bounds
\[
O(p^{-2})\quad(Q=p),
\qquad
O(\log Q/Q^2)\quad(Q=p^e,\ e\ge2).
\tag{T3}\label{eq:raw-resource}
\]
At a prime current the retained simple values may be taken distinct.  At a
proper prime power every simple-value fibre has the standard bounded
multiplicity needed below.

Apply the quota-packing theorem of Appendix~\ref{app:packing} once to the
finite collection of rows below a chosen endpoint, including any immediate
predecessor row required at the source boundary.  All local families used
below then live in disjoint subsets of one globally independent pool, so their
later physical union is automatic.  Any $c$-transparent finite obstacle only
changes the fixed thinning denominator.

\subsection{The common local interface}

Let
\[
F^-\le F,
\qquad S=F/F^-
\]
be one prime-order simple step.  A \emph{homogeneous simple cover} of grade
$m$ and resource $\rho$ is a finite binary physical family $X$ such that every
branch is obstacle-compatible, has grade exactly $m$ and resource at most
$\rho$, its observation lies in $F$, and
\[
X\longrightarrow S
\]
is surjective.  No construction coordinate survives this interface.

\subsection{Width-bearing prime currents}

Let $Q=p$ be prime.  After obstacle deletion and global packing, the simple
value image contains a set
\[
X_p\subset S_p\cong\mathbf F_p,
\qquad
N_p:=|X_p|\gg p/\log p
\]
of distinct points.  The dense convolution theorem of
Appendix~\ref{app:atomic-convolution} now applies.  Choose once a proof-local
constant $C$ beyond its threshold and define
\[
\boxed{
m_p:=\left\lceil C(\log p)^3\right\rceil.}
\tag{T4}\label{eq:mp-definition}
\]
For every fixed $L>1$, eventually
\[
Lm_p\le N_p/2,
\]
and every integer
\[
M\in[m_p,Lm_p]
\tag{T5}\label{eq:atomic-grade-interval}
\]
has a homogeneous simple cover.  Indeed the theorem produces $M$ disjoint
two-point slots in the distinct simple values; pulling those slots back to the
packed physical atoms gives the actual branch family.  Each branch contains
exactly one atom from every slot and therefore has grade $M$.

By \eqref{eq:raw-resource}, uniformly on the fixed interval
\eqref{eq:atomic-grade-interval}, its resource is
\[
O((\log p)^4/p^2).
\tag{T6}\label{eq:atomic-resource}
\]

\subsection{Proper prime powers}

We use only the following elementary fact.  If $B_1,\ldots,B_{p-1}$ are
two-point subsets of a prime-order additive group, then repeated
Cauchy--Davenport gives
\[
B_1+\cdots+B_{p-1}=S.
\tag{T7}\label{eq:two-point-cover}
\]
Thus $p-1$ mutually compatible same-grade slots whose alternatives have
nonzero simple difference form a homogeneous simple cover.

At the first proper $p$-power current $Q=p^2$, the packed row has
$\gg p^2/\log p$ atoms while a simple-value fibre has size $O(p)$.  Hence,
after finitely many exceptions, one can greedily choose $p-1$ disjoint pairs
of atoms with unequal simple values.  These are the slots of
\eqref{eq:two-point-cover}; the common branch grade is $p-1$ and the resource
is
\[
O(\log p/p^3).
\tag{T8}\label{eq:square-resource}
\]

For $Q=p^e$, $e\ge3$, choose $p-1$ unused current atoms and $p-1$ unused atoms
from the immediate predecessor row $p^{e-1}$.  The predecessor observations
lie in the source of the new simple quotient and are therefore current-neutral,
whereas every current atom has nonzero simple value.  Pairing them gives
$p-1$ same-grade two-point slots and hence a full simple cover of grade $p-1$.
The capacity demand is only $O(p)$ against row size
$\gg p^e/(e\log p)$, uniformly after finitely many stages.  If the predecessor
straddles the Tail source boundary, the source-closed transparency theorem
includes exactly this boundary row.  Its index escapes to infinity with the
source.  A convenient summable resource majorant is
\[
O\!\left(\frac{e\log p}{p^{2e-3}}\right)
\qquad(e\ge3).
\tag{T9}\label{eq:deep-resource}
\]

The terms ``square'', ``deep'' and ``donor'' have reached their last consumer
in this proof.  Above this subsection every proper prime power is represented
only by a homogeneous simple cover of grade $p-1$.

\subsection{Target-specific filtered lifting}

The simple quotient does not remember the lower residue shadow, so we do not
choose a splitting.  Let
\[
H_0\le H_1\le\cdots\le H_n
\]
be a finite source-to-endpoint filtration and put
$S_i=H_i/H_{i-1}$.  Let
\[
v_i:X_i\to H_i
\]
be homogeneous simple-cover families such that
$X_i\to S_i$ is surjective.

\begin{lemma}[Targeted filtered lifting]\label{lem:targeted-filtered-lifting}
For every generalized target
\[
r:T\to H_n/H_0,
\]
the object of tuples $(x_n,\ldots,x_1)$ satisfying
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n v_i(x_i)=r\pmod{H_0}
\tag{T10}\label{eq:targeted-match}
\]
projects surjectively to $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Project $r$ to the last simple quotient $S_n$ and pull back the surjection
$X_n\to S_n$.  Retain the whole pullback fibre; no section is chosen.  For an
actual branch $x_n$ the difference $r-v_n(x_n)$ lands canonically in
$H_{n-1}/H_0$.  Repeat with $X_{n-1}\to S_{n-1}$.  Every step is a pullback of
a surjection, hence the finite composite is surjective.  At the bottom the
residual belongs to $H_0$, which is exactly \eqref{eq:targeted-match}.
\end{proof}

Because all $X_i$ lie in one global packed universe, the matched tuple maps by
literal physical union to a binary physical family.  If its local grades are
$h_i$, every branch has total grade $\sum_i h_i$, and the branchwise resource
bounds add.  The higher branch is forgotten only after its lower shadow has
become the next target.

\subsection{Grade finality}

Fix a sufficiently late source $\beta$ and a numerical endpoint $X$.  For each
prime current choose independently
\[
M_p\in[m_p,Lm_p],
\]
while every proper prime power contributes its fixed resolver grade $p-1$.
Consequently the exact image of the local-grade-tuple sum is one integer
interval
\[
[A_\beta(X),B_{\beta,L}(X)]\cap\N.
\tag{T11}\label{eq:r14-grade-interval}
\]
No grade tuple is selected: for a requested total grade we retain the full
fibre of the addition map.

The fixed-ratio prime-band estimate gives
\[
\sum_{\beta<p\le X}m_p\asymp X(\log X)^2
\tag{T12}\label{eq:mp-sum}
\]
for $X$ sufficiently beyond the fixed source.  Indeed on the final comparable
band there are $\asymp X/\log X$ primes and
$m_p\asymp(\log X)^3$, giving the lower bound; decomposing earlier primes into
geometric bands gives the matching upper bound.

The total proper-power resolver shift through $X$ is lower order.  Squares
contribute at most
\[
\sum_{p\le\sqrt X}(p-1)=O(X),
\]
and all exponents $e\ge3$ contribute
$O(X^{2/3}\log X)$ by a crude integer bound.

Let $\Lambda>1$ be the fixed endpoint dilation from comparable-prime supply.
The increment
\[
A_\beta(\Lambda X)-A_\beta(X)
\]
is therefore at most $C_1X(\log X)^2$ for one fixed $C_1$, whereas the width
already present at endpoint $X$ is
\[
B_{\beta,L}(X)-A_\beta(X)
=(L-1)\sum_{\beta<p\le X}m_p
\ge c_1(L-1)X(\log X)^2.
\]
Choose the fixed $L$ once so that $c_1(L-1)>C_1$.  Then eventually
\[
A_\beta(\Lambda X)\le B_{\beta,L}(X)+1.
\tag{T13}\label{eq:r14-overlap}
\]
Successive endpoint intervals overlap or are adjacent, and their lower
endpoints tend to infinity.  Their union is therefore a final subset of
$\N$.

\subsection{Resource decay}

The prime-current majorant \eqref{eq:atomic-resource} has convergent sum
\[
\sum_p\frac{(\log p)^4}{p^2}<\infty.
\]
The square majorant \eqref{eq:square-resource} and higher-power majorant
\eqref{eq:deep-resource} are dominated by convergent integer/geometric
series.  Boundary-predecessor contributions form a subseries of the same
majorant and escape every fixed finite set as the source advances.  Hence the
total branchwise resource beyond the source is the tail of one convergent
positive series and tends to zero along the source filter.

\subsection{Direct late-source targeted residue response}

Let $H_\beta$ be a sufficiently late source, let $O$ be a transparent finite
obstacle, and let
\[
r\in A/H_\beta.
\]
Choose a proof-local finite endpoint through which $r$ factors.  Start the
geometric endpoint sequence beyond that stage.  For each total grade in the
final interval, retain the full local-grade-tuple fibre and then apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:targeted-filtered-lifting} to the same local physical families.
The resulting matched family has source residue exactly $r$, not merely full
simple-factor images.  The same branches carry the resource bound.

We have proved the response consumed by exactification.

\begin{theorem}[Direct late-source targeted saturation]\label{thm:ltar-closed}
Fix a positive transparency parameter $c$ and $\varepsilon>0$.  Eventually in
the eligible Tail source $\beta$, for every $c$-transparent finite obstacle
$O$, every residue
\[
r\in A/H_\beta,
\]
and every sufficiently large grade $h$, there is a finite obstacle-compatible
binary physical family of component profile $(h,0)$, source residue $r$, and
branchwise reciprocal resource $<\varepsilon$.
\end{theorem}

The source-valid locus is uniform in $O$ and $r$; after the source is fixed the
grade threshold may depend on $r$ through the proof-local endpoint.  This is
exactly the quantifier order required by the fixed-source mesh obstruction.
