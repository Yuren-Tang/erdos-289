\subsection{Canonical trimming and explicit grade overlap}
\label{subsec:grade-overlap}

We now verify the grade-finality assertion of Section~\ref{sec:ltar-provider}
using only the bounded-thinning role interface and the restricted-fold
threshold. The raw rates are used only to prove Proposition~\ref{prop:roles};
they do not enter the aggregation argument below.

Fix once and for all a number
\[
0<\vartheta<\frac12.
\]
Choose the constant $C$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:roles} sufficiently large for
Lemma~\ref{lem:supercritical} at this $\vartheta$.

For a representative block at a prime-order simple factor, define the canonical
trimmed cardinality
\[
m(C,p)=\min\{n\ge1:Cp\le n^2\}.
\tag{G1}\label{eq:canonical-trim}
\]
Whenever Proposition~\ref{prop:roles} supplies an injective representative
block $\mathrm{Rep}$ with $Cp\le|\mathrm{Rep}|^2$, take a finite subblock of
exact cardinality $m(C,p)$. Minimality gives
\[
\sqrt{Cp}\le m(C,p)<\sqrt{Cp}+1.
\tag{G2}\label{eq:trim-size}
\]
No oversized representative cardinality is retained after this step.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:restricted-fold} to the trimmed block. With
\[
t_p=\left\lceil\frac{2p}{m(C,p)}\right\rceil,
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:supercritical} gives
\[
[t_p,m(C,p)-t_p]\subseteq I_D(p,m(C,p)),
\qquad
t_p\le\vartheta m(C,p).
\tag{G3}\label{eq:trimmed-local-interval}
\]
Thus every atomic current contributes an interval of width
\[
\gg (1-2\vartheta)\sqrt p,
\tag{G4}\label{eq:atomic-width}
\]
and lower endpoint at most
\[
O(\vartheta\sqrt p).
\tag{G5}\label{eq:atomic-lower}
\]
The same statements hold at a first successor after its disjoint export block
has been removed. A deep successor contributes the singleton resolver grade
$p-1$.

Fix the dilation $\Lambda>1$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-band} and let
\[
J_Y=[A_Y,B_Y]\cap\N
\]
be the Minkowski interval obtained from all local supported-grade sets between
the fixed source and endpoint $Y$.

\begin{lemma}[Atomic band width]\label{lem:atomic-band-width}
There is a constant $c_1>0$ such that the atomic currents in
\[
Y/\Lambda<p\le Y
\]
contribute at least
\[
c_1(1-2\vartheta)\frac{Y^{3/2}}{\log Y}
\]
to $B_Y-A_Y$ for all sufficiently large $Y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-band} gives $\gg Y/\log Y$ primes in the fixed-ratio
band. Every such prime has $\sqrt p\asymp\sqrt Y$, and
\eqref{eq:atomic-width} gives width $\gg(1-2\vartheta)\sqrt Y$ per prime.
Widths add under Minkowski addition.
\end{proof}

We compare this existing width with the lower displacement introduced when the
endpoint moves from $Y$ to $\Lambda Y$.

For the new atomic currents, Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-band} and
\eqref{eq:atomic-lower} give
\[
\Delta_{\rm atom}(Y)
\le
c_2\vartheta\frac{Y^{3/2}}{\log Y}
\tag{G6}\label{eq:atomic-displacement}
\]
for a constant $c_2$ depending only on the fixed prime-band constants and
$C$. Choose $\vartheta$ sufficiently small, before the final source is fixed,
that
\[
c_2\vartheta<\frac12c_1(1-2\vartheta).
\tag{G7}\label{eq:vartheta-choice}
\]

For first successors $Q=p^2\le\Lambda Y$, canonical trimming gives minimum
grade $O(\sqrt p)$. Even the crude bound over all integer bases yields
\[
\Delta_{\rm first}(Y)
=O(Y^{3/4}).
\tag{G8}\label{eq:first-displacement}
\]
For deep successors $Q=p^e$, $e\ge3$, the singleton grade is $p-1$, and
\[
\Delta_{\rm deep}(Y)
=O(Y^{2/3}\log Y).
\tag{G9}\label{eq:deep-displacement}
\]
Both are
\[
o\!\left(\frac{Y^{3/2}}{\log Y}\right).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Eventual overlap]\label{prop:eventual-overlap}
For all sufficiently large $Y$,
\[
A_{\Lambda Y}\le B_Y+1.
\]
Consequently the union of the endpoint grade intervals contains a final
segment of $\N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:atomic-band-width} supplies an already-present width of order
$Y^{3/2}/\log Y$. By \eqref{eq:vartheta-choice}, the new atomic lower
displacement consumes less than half of this leading width, while
\eqref{eq:first-displacement} and \eqref{eq:deep-displacement} are lower-order.
Hence eventually
\[
A_{\Lambda Y}-A_Y<B_Y-A_Y+1,
\]
which is the stated adjacency/overlap inequality.

The lower endpoints tend to infinity because every newly appearing current has
positive minimum supported grade and there are infinitely many atomic currents.
Iterating the fixed-ratio endpoint map therefore produces one final ray in
$\N$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Explicit resource summability}
\label{subsec:resource-sum}

Canonical trimming also simplifies the resource calculation.

At an atomic current $Q=p$, every supported local state uses at most
$m(C,p)=O(\sqrt p)$ atoms, while every retained prime atom has reciprocal mass
$O(p^{-2})$. Hence the atomic local resource is
\[
O(p^{-3/2}).
\tag{R1}\label{eq:atomic-resource}
\]
The sum over primes converges by comparison with
\[
\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-3/2}.
\]

At a first successor $Q=p^2$, the trimmed representative block has
$O(\sqrt p)$ elements and a generic current atom has mass
$O(\log p/p^4)$. The local restricted-fold cover therefore costs at most
\[
O\!\left(\frac{\log p}{p^{7/2}}\right).
\tag{R2}\label{eq:first-resource}
\]
This is again dominated by a convergent ordinary integer series.

At a deep successor $Q=p^e$, $e\ge3$, the resolver uses $O(p)$ own and
predecessor-donor atoms. The signed-inverse certificate gives the convenient
majorant
\[
O\!\left(\frac{e\log p}{p^{2e-3}}\right).
\tag{R3}\label{eq:deep-resource}
\]
For fixed $p$ the inner sum over $e\ge3$ is geometrically dominated, and the
resulting sum over primes is bounded by a convergent integer series. Boundary
predecessor donors form a subseries of the same majorant; their current indices
escape every fixed finite set as the source advances.

\begin{proposition}[Vanishing Tail resource]\label{prop:resource-tail}
Let $\mathcal R(B)$ be the total branchwise resource majorant contributed by
currents beyond source base $B$. Then
\[
\mathcal R(B)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(B\to\infty).
\]
Consequently, for every $\varepsilon>0$, every sufficiently late source admits
the full grade-final BinaryTail construction with branchwise reciprocal mass at
most $\varepsilon$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equations \eqref{eq:atomic-resource}--\eqref{eq:deep-resource} define a
convergent nonnegative series on the current filtration. Tails of such a series
tend to zero. Proposition~\ref{prop:eventual-overlap} uses only subfamilies of
the same role reservoirs, so every exact grade tuple is bounded by this common
tail majorant.
\end{proof}

Thus the two consumers
\[
\text{grade image is final},
\qquad
\text{resource tail tends to zero}
\]
are verified on the same canonical role system. No raw cardinality rate or
chosen oversized representative survives the response boundary.
