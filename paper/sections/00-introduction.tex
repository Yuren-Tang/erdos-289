\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Let $\mathcal C$ denote the family of finite subsets $S\subset\N_{>0}$ whose connected components in the path graph on $\N_{>0}$ have cardinality $2$ or $3$.  Write
\[
\nu(S)=|\pi_0(S)|,
\qquad
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$ there exists $S\in\mathcal C$ such that
\[
\nu(S)=k,
\qquad
W(S)=1.
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus every sufficiently large $k$ is realized by exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint, nonadjacent integer intervals, all of length $2$ or $3$.  This proves the strengthened formulation currently listed as Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 \cite{ErdosProblem289}.  Erd\H{o}s and Graham posed the underlying interval-reciprocal problem in \cite[p.~34]{ErdosGraham1980}; their printed formulation does not explicitly impose distinctness, disjointness, or nonadjacency.  The current problem list records the strengthened version used here \cite{ErdosProblem289}.

The proof separates three layers.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Structural layer.}
The interval configurations form the label object for a locally posetal $2$-category $\mathbf{Rel}_{\mathcal C}$ of labelled finite relations.  Composition is fibre product followed by compatible union of labels.  Forgetting labels defines a normal oplax $2$-functor
\[
U:\mathbf{Rel}_{\mathcal C}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Rel};
\]
its comparison $2$-cell is precisely the inclusion created by discarding incompatible label tuples.  Once joint compatibility is established, this comparison is an identity.  The residue and component-count observations are additive, while prime-power torsion in $\Q/\Z$ supplies one-dimensional simple quotients.  The remaining structural passages are pullbacks and quotient maps.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Interface layer.}
At a prime-power step, a concrete interval family is compressed to a finite relation on the simple quotient and the component count.  The transfer theorem is uniform over every sufficiently dense restriction of that family.  After a finite cutoff has been chosen, Haxell's theorem supplies a compatible dense restriction, and an affine pullback lifts the simple-quotient statement to the global torsion filtration.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Arithmetic input layer.}
The inputs are comparable-prime supply, the signed-inverse construction, the Dias da Silva--Hamidoune fixed-cardinality sum theorem, a reciprocal-neutral identity, a finite initialization, and Haxell's independent-transversal theorem.  Their role is to provide the density, surjectivity, compatibility, and reciprocal-mass estimates required by the structural argument.

The proof proceeds as follows.  Signed-inverse families are constructed at the prime-power stages, and a finite affine initialization is enlarged by reciprocal-neutral switches to produce an arbitrarily wide interval of component counts.  The simple-quotient transfer is then applied uniformly under dense restriction.  Prime steps contribute width of order $Q/\log Q$, whereas the accumulated loss from composite prime powers is $o(Q/\log Q)$; this yields eventual coverage of all component counts.  For a fixed terminal cutoff, Haxell's theorem produces compatible literal intervals, and the affine lifting lemma carries the observable calculation through the torsion filtration.  Finally the commutative square
\[
\begin{CD}
\mathcal C @>{W}>> \Q\\
@V{\overline W}VV @VV{q_{\Z}}V\\
\Q/\Z @= \Q/\Z
\end{CD}
\]
turns $\overline W=0$ and $0<W<2$ into $W=1$.
