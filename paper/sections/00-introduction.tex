\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Let $\mathcal C$ denote the family of finite subsets $S\subset\mathbf N_{>0}$ whose connected components in the path graph on $\mathbf N_{>0}$ have cardinality $2$ or $3$ and are pairwise nonadjacent.  Write
\[
  \nu(S)=|\pi_0(S)|,
  \qquad
  W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n.
\]
Our main result is the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$ there exists $S\in\mathcal C$ such that
\[
  \nu(S)=k,
  \qquad
  W(S)=1.
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus every sufficiently large $k$ is realized by exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint, nonadjacent integer intervals, all of length $2$ or $3$.  This proves the strengthened formulation currently listed as Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 \cite{ErdosProblem289}.  Erd\H{o}s and Graham posed the underlying interval-reciprocal problem in \cite[p.~34]{ErdosGraham1980}; their printed formulation does not explicitly impose distinctness, disjointness, or nonadjacency of the intervals.  The current problem list records this distinction and adopts the strengthened formulation above \cite{ErdosProblem289}.  We therefore regard Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, rather than a problem number, as the mathematical statement proved in this article.

The proof is most transparent after separating three layers.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Structural layer.}
Finite interval configurations carry three additive observations: reciprocal value in $\mathbf Q$, residue in $\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z$, and component count in $\mathbf N$.  Prime-power torsion stages of $\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z$ give one-dimensional simple quotients.  Labelled finite correspondences are composed by pullback, and their observable images form relations.  The only genuinely non-strict phenomenon is that forgetting interval labels is oplax with respect to composition; in the locally posetal bicategory $\mathbf{Rel}$ this is an inclusion $2$-cell.  Joint compatibility turns that comparison $2$-cell into an identity.  Quotient lifting, finite realization, and the final residue-zero argument are then ordinary pullback and exactness statements.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Interface layer.}
At each prime-power step, a concrete arithmetic family is compressed to a finite relation on the simple quotient together with a component-count interval.  A uniform theorem is proved for every sufficiently dense restriction of that family, before any independent set is chosen.  Later, one finite independent-transversal argument supplies an actual compatible restriction, and a single affine-lifting square carries the simple-quotient statement back to the global torsor filtration.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Ordinary input layer.}
The remaining inputs are elementary or standard: comparable-prime supply, a signed-inverse congruence construction, a restricted fixed-cardinality subset-sum theorem, a reciprocal-neutral identity, one finite initialization certificate, and Haxell's independent-transversal theorem.  These inputs certify surjectivity, density, compatibility, and reciprocal-mass bounds for the structural correspondences; they are not additional categorical structure.

For a finite jointly compatible string of labelled correspondences $R_1,\ldots,R_n$, let $U(R_i)$ be the relation obtained by forgetting its interval label.  In $\mathbf{Rel}$ there is a canonical comparison $2$-cell
\[
  U(R_n\odot\cdots\odot R_1)
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  U(R_n)\circ\cdots\circ U(R_1),
\tag{1.1}\label{eq:oplax-comparison}
\]
where a $2$-cell is simply inclusion of relations.  The comparison becomes the identity on exactly the finite diagrams for which the ordinary composability fibre product factors through the genuine joint-compatibility domain.  This is the categorical reason that the target calculation can be completed before the final finite realization by intervals.

The proof then has a simple logical order.  First we construct the prime-power families and a wide affine initial cover.  Next we forget interval labels and prove an eventual interval theorem for the component count.  Only after a finite terminal cutoff has been selected do we apply Haxell's theorem once and return to literal interval configurations.  Finally the commutative square
\[
\begin{CD}
\mathcal C @>{W}>> \mathbf Q\\
@V{\overline W}VV @VV{q_{\mathbf Z}}V\\
\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z @= \mathbf Q/\mathbf Z
\end{CD}
\]
turns $\overline W=0$ and $0<W<2$ into the exact equality $W=1$.
