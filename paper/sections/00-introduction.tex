\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

We use $\N=\Z_{\ge0}$ and $\N_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$.  Let $\mathcal C$ denote the family of finite subsets $S\subset\N_{>0}$ whose connected components in the path graph on $\N_{>0}$ have cardinality $2$ or $3$.  Write
\[
\nu(S)=|\pi_0(S)|,
\qquad
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$ there exists $S\in\mathcal C$ such that
\[
\nu(S)=k,
\qquad
W(S)=1.
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus every sufficiently large $k$ is realized by exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint, nonadjacent integer intervals, all of length $2$ or $3$.  Accordingly, the theorem gives an affirmative answer to the strengthened formulation currently listed as Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 \cite{ErdosProblem289}, and is slightly stronger in that every interval is forced to have cardinality $2$ or $3$.  Erd\H{o}s and Graham posed the underlying interval-reciprocal problem in \cite[p.~34]{ErdosGraham1980}; their printed formulation does not explicitly impose distinctness, disjointness, or nonadjacency.  The current problem list records the strengthened version used here \cite{ErdosProblem289}.

The current problem page records a simple construction for the unrestricted wording, so separation is essential to the modern formulation.  It also records an earlier separated-interval example: in the solution of Monthly problem E2689 proposed by L.-S. Hahn, Hickerson and Montgomery represented $2$ by reciprocal sums over five separated intervals.  These precedents do not address eventual realization of every sufficiently large component count at reciprocal value $1$ \cite{ErdosProblem289}.

The final reduction is elementary.  A nonempty configuration with
\[
0<W(S)<2,
\qquad
W(S)\equiv0\pmod{\Z}
\]
must have $W(S)=1$.  We therefore work modulo $1$ through most of the construction and retain a fixed margin below reciprocal mass $2$.

\subsection*{Proof architecture}

Three constructions drive the proof.  Reciprocal-neutral identities vary the component count without changing reciprocal value.  Signed-inverse interval families supply controlled residues in each simple prime-power quotient.  Finally, after a finite cutoff has been fixed, Haxell's theorem selects mutually compatible physical intervals from the local choices.  The categorical setup records how these constructions compose and where information is forgotten or recovered.

The proof is organized by top-down specification, bottom-up verification of the required constructions, and final pasting.  Two structures are kept distinct.  One is the prime-power filtration of $A=\Q/\Z$, along which the local transfer relations are composed.  The other is an information-forgetting chain from literal interval configurations to observable residue-and-count data, then to simple-quotient count supports, and finally to the scalar width of a count interval.  Observation and quotient maps belong to this second direction.  The lower-information layers are used for planning; affine lifting, finite compatibility selection, and the terminal pullback recover the information needed for the final configuration.

The overall construction/dependency flow is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:master-proof-flow}
\begin{gathered}
\text{categorical setup and auxiliary inputs}\\
\Downarrow\\[-1mm]
\mathcal A_Q\xrightarrow{\ \phi_Q\ }S_Q^\times
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
R_B:\{0_M\}\rightsquigarrow
\operatorname{Fib}_{E_B}(\gamma_B)\times I_B\\
\Downarrow\\[-1mm]
\text{uniform simple-quotient transfer }(\delta_\rho,\mu_\rho)\\
\Downarrow\\[-1mm]
\text{scalar continuation: }X,\ k\in I_X\\
\Downarrow\\[-1mm]
\text{finite Haxell selection }P_X
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{strict covering labelled relation }R_X\\
\Downarrow\\[-1mm]
\tau_X^{-1}(0,k)\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(\overline W,\nu)=(0,k),\ 0<W<2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad W=1.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:master-proof-flow} is the dependency diagram for the proof; the commutative diagrams below record equalities of mathematical composites.

The categorical spine is the labelled-relation calculus of Section~\ref{sec:states-filtration}.  Observable relations may compose even when the interval labels witnessing their edges cannot coexist.  Compatible configurations therefore form a partial commutative monoid, and
\[
\Omega=(\overline W,\nu):\mathcal C\longrightarrow A\times\N
\]
is additive only on its compatibility domain.  The resulting labelled relations form a locally posetal $2$-category $\mathbf{Rel}_{\mathcal C}^{\Omega}$, and forgetting labels gives a normal oplax $2$-functor
\[
U:\mathbf{Rel}_{\mathcal C}^{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Rel}.
\]
Its comparison $2$-cell measures the loss of witnesses caused by incompatible labels.  After the finite cutoff $X$ has been fixed, Section~\ref{sec:finite-realization} constructs physical covering translations $\mathscr T_1,\ldots,\mathscr T_m$ and proves the witnesswise compatibility needed for the strict pasting
\begin{equation}\label{eq:master-strict-pasting}
\begin{aligned}
R_X&=\mathscr T_m\odot\cdots\odot\mathscr T_1\odot R_B,\\
U(R_X)&=U(\mathscr T_m)\circ\cdots\circ U(\mathscr T_1)\circ U(R_B).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second equality is exactly the point at which the normal oplax comparison becomes an identity.

A second reusable ingredient is the affine torsor extension principle proved in Section~\ref{sec:states-filtration}.  For finite subgroups $H\le K\le A$, covering one $H$-torsor and covering the quotient $K/H$ by increments automatically covers the corresponding $K$-torsor.  The bridge induction of Section~\ref{sec:wide-start} and the prime-power translation step of Section~\ref{sec:finite-realization} are two specializations of this single mechanism.

The arithmetic constructions supply the remaining inputs.  Section~\ref{sec:signed-inverse} constructs, at each sufficiently large prime-power stage $Q=p^e$, a family $\mathcal A_Q$ with controlled simple-quotient residues, density, incompatibility and reciprocal mass.  Section~\ref{sec:local-profile} proves a transfer theorem that is uniform under every sufficiently dense restriction of $\mathcal A_Q$; its only scalar outputs are the required width $\delta_\rho(Q)$ and, at prime stages, the positive gain $\mu_\rho(q)$.  Section~\ref{sec:scalar-finality} retains only the interval width and solves the resulting one-dimensional continuation problem, producing for every large $k$ a finite cutoff $X$ at which the affine centre has vanished and $k$ lies in the available count interval.  Only then does Section~\ref{sec:finite-realization} invoke Haxell to choose mutually compatible physical representatives and lift the resulting cutoff choice back to the labelled layer.

At the terminal state the categorical calculation produces residue $0$, component count $k$, and reciprocal mass below $2$.  The commutative square
\[
\begin{CD}
\mathcal C @>{W}>> \Q\\
@V{\overline W}VV @VV{q_{\Z}}V\\
\Q/\Z @= \Q/\Z
\end{CD}
\]
then shows that the positive reciprocal value is an integer strictly below $2$, and hence equals $1$.

Section~\ref{sec:states-filtration} develops the labelled-relation calculus and the torsion filtration.  Section~\ref{sec:auxiliary-inputs} records the auxiliary inputs.  Sections~\ref{sec:signed-inverse}--\ref{sec:scalar-finality} construct the arithmetic families, the wide affine start, the uniform transfer, and the scalar continuation.  Sections~\ref{sec:finite-realization} and~\ref{sec:terminalization} realize the finite compatible configuration and recover the exact reciprocal sum.  Appendices~\ref{app:prime-supply} and~\ref{app:restricted-fold} prove the two auxiliary estimates used in the main text, while Appendix~\ref{app:prefix-seed} records the finite initialization.
