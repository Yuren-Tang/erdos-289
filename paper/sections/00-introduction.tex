\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

We use $\N=\Z_{\ge0}$ and $\N_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$.  Let $\mathcal C$ denote the family of finite subsets $S\subset\N_{>0}$ whose connected components in the path graph on $\N_{>0}$ have cardinality $2$ or $3$.  Write
\[
\nu(S)=|\pi_0(S)|,
\qquad
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$ there exists $S\in\mathcal C$ such that
\[
\nu(S)=k,
\qquad
W(S)=1.
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus every sufficiently large $k$ is realized by exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint, nonadjacent integer intervals, all of length $2$ or $3$.  This proves the strengthened formulation currently listed as Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 \cite{ErdosProblem289}.  Erd\H{o}s and Graham posed the underlying interval-reciprocal problem in \cite[p.~34]{ErdosGraham1980}; their printed formulation does not explicitly impose distinctness, disjointness, or nonadjacency.  The current problem list records the strengthened version used here \cite{ErdosProblem289}.

The basic reduction is elementary in spirit.  It is enough to construct a nonempty interval configuration with $0<W<2$ and $W\equiv0\pmod{\Z}$, because then $W$ must equal $1$.  We therefore work modulo $1$ until the final step.  The least-common-multiple filtration of $\Q/\Z$ changes only at prime powers: prime stages can be made to enlarge the available interval of component counts by order $q/\log q$, whereas the accumulated loss at higher prime powers is only $o(X/\log X)$.  The remaining task is to realize this residue-and-count calculation by literal compatible intervals without exhausting the fixed reciprocal-mass margin.

The proof separates a structural calculus from its arithmetic inputs.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Structural calculus.}
Compatible interval configurations form a partial commutative monoid, and
\[
\Omega=(\overline W,\nu):\mathcal C\longrightarrow
(\Q/\Z)\times\N
\]
is additive on every compatible union.  We use a locally posetal $2$-category $\mathbf{Rel}_{\mathcal C}^{\Omega}$ whose objects are finite sets of observable states and whose labelled edges obey the conservation law
\[
y=x+\Omega(L).
\]
Composition is fibre product followed by compatible union of labels.  Forgetting labels defines a normal oplax $2$-functor
\[
U:\mathbf{Rel}_{\mathcal C}^{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Rel};
\]
its comparison $2$-cell records exactly the loss caused by incompatible label tuples.  Once joint compatibility is established, this comparison is an identity.  Prime-power torsion in $\Q/\Z$ supplies one-dimensional simple quotients, and the passage from a simple quotient back to an affine torsor is a pullback calculation inside the same observable calculus.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Arithmetic inputs.}
The inputs are comparable-prime supply, the signed-inverse construction, the Dias da Silva--Hamidoune fixed-cardinality sum theorem, a reciprocal-neutral identity, a finite initialization, and Haxell's independent-transversal theorem.  They provide the density, restricted-sum surjectivity, compatibility and reciprocal-mass estimates required by the structural argument.

The proof passes through an observable reduction.  Signed-inverse families are constructed at the prime-power stages, and a finite affine initialization is enlarged by reciprocal-neutral switches to produce an arbitrarily wide interval of component counts.  On the simple quotients these data give a scalar process: prime steps contribute width of order $Q/\log Q$, whereas the accumulated loss from composite prime powers is $o(Q/\log Q)$.  This yields eventual coverage of all component counts before any global compatible family is chosen.  For a fixed terminal cutoff, Haxell's theorem then produces compatible literal intervals.  Their canonical observable translation relations lift the simple-quotient calculation through the torsion filtration, and joint compatibility makes labelled composition exact.  Finally the commutative square
\[
\begin{CD}
\mathcal C @>{W}>> \Q\\
@V{\overline W}VV @VV{q_{\Z}}V\\
\Q/\Z @= \Q/\Z
\end{CD}
\]
turns $\overline W=0$ and $0<W<2$ into $W=1$.
