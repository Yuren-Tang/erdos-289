\section{Finite realization by compatible intervals}
\label{sec:finite-realization}

We now return from the observable component-count argument to literal intervals.  This is the first point at which Haxell's theorem is used to choose an independent set.

Let $\eta>0$ be the reciprocal-mass margin in Proposition~\ref{prop:wide-start}.  By \eqref{eq:role-summable}, choose $B_{\mathrm{mass}}$ such that
\[
\sum_{Q>B_{\mathrm{mass}}}\omega_Q<\eta.
\tag{7.1}\label{eq:resource-tail}
\]
Choose also $B_{\mathrm{pack}}$ so large that every compatible subfamily $\mathcal A_Q(B)$ occurring after that point has cardinality at least the quota denominator $L_P$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:haxell-quota}.  This is possible by \eqref{eq:family-size} and Proposition~\ref{prop:wide-start}.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-terminality} with
\[
B_\dagger=\max\{B_{\mathrm{mass}},B_{\mathrm{pack}}\}.
\tag{7.2}\label{eq:global-start-cutoff}
\]
Thus the affine initial cover and the entire observable component-count process are fixed only after the reciprocal-mass tail and the packing threshold have been fixed.

Fix $X\in\mathcal H_B$.  Only finitely many prime-power steps lie between $B$ and $X$.  For each such $Q$, Proposition~\ref{prop:wide-start} gives
\[
|\mathcal A_Q(B)|
\ge\sigma|\mathcal A_Q|
\ge\kappa\sigma\frac Q{\log Q}.
\tag{7.3}\label{eq:survivor-density}
\]
Join two members of the finite union of these families when they are incompatible as interval components.  Proposition~\ref{prop:signed-inverse-capabilities} gives a uniform maximum degree.  Proposition~\ref{prop:haxell-quota}, applied once to the finitely many families through $X$, yields an independent set $P_X$ with
\[
|P_X\cap\mathcal A_Q(B)|
\ge\rho_P|\mathcal A_Q(B)|
>\rho\frac Q{\log Q}.
\tag{7.4}\label{eq:packed-density}
\]
Hence
\[
P_X(Q):=P_X\cap\mathcal A_Q(B)
\in\mathcal D_\rho(Q)
\]
for every step through $X$.  The constants $\delta_\rho$ and $\mu_\rho$ were fixed before $P_X$ existed.

\subsection{From the simple quotient to the affine fibre}

The local theorem concerns $S_Q=F_Q/F_{<Q}$, while the global induction concerns affine fibres in $A=\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z$.  The connection is the pullback square
\[
\begin{CD}
F_Q @>>> A\\
@V{q}VV @VV{q_{F_{<Q}}}V\\
S_Q=F_Q/F_{<Q} @>>> A/F_{<Q},
\end{CD}
\tag{7.5}\label{eq:simple-factor-pullback}
\]
where the bottom arrow is the natural inclusion.  Equivalently, two elements of $F_Q$ have the same image in $S_Q$ exactly when their difference belongs to $F_{<Q}$.

\begin{lemma}[Affine lifting across one prime-power step]\label{lem:affine-current-lift}
Let $Q$ be a prime power.  Suppose an incoming labelled correspondence covers
\[
\operatorname{Tor}_{F_{<Q}}(\alpha)\times I,
\qquad \alpha\in A/F_{<Q},
\]
and let $\bar\alpha\in A/F_Q$ be the image of $\alpha$.  Suppose a finite local relation has residues in $F_Q$ and has the following property: for every $s\in S_Q$ and $k'\in I'$, its full finite solution relation contains an interval label $L$ and $i\in I$ such that
\[
[\overline W(L)]_{S_Q}=s,
\qquad i+\nu(L)=k'.
\]
If the incoming and local labels are jointly compatible, then their composite covers
\[
\operatorname{Tor}_{F_Q}(\bar\alpha)\times I'.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $(y,k')$ in the target fibre and choose one point $x_0\in\operatorname{Tor}_{F_{<Q}}(\alpha)$.  Since $y$ and $x_0$ have the same image in $A/F_Q$, their difference belongs to $F_Q$.  Let $s$ be its class in $S_Q$.  Choose a local solution $(L,i)$ of simple class $s$ and total component count $k'$.  By the pullback property \eqref{eq:simple-factor-pullback},
\[
y-\overline W(L)-x_0\in F_{<Q}.
\]
Thus $y-\overline W(L)$ lies in the incoming affine fibre, where surjectivity supplies an incoming label of component count $i$.  Its union with $L$ gives residue $y$ and component count $k'$.  The argument is fibrewise existential; no section of either quotient or finite solution relation is chosen.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Joint compatibility and exact composition}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-profile} retained the full finite solution relations in both the large-image and heavy-fibre cases.  Apply those relations to the actual dense restriction $P_X(Q)$.  Since $P_X$ is independent in the incompatibility graph, all its interval components are pairwise disjoint and nonadjacent; every finite subset therefore belongs to $\mathcal C$.  Moreover, membership in $\mathcal A_Q(B)$ means compatibility with every label of the affine initial cover.

Consequently the ordinary fibre product of all composable edges in the finite chain has its label map
\[
P_n\longrightarrow\mathcal C^n
\]
factor through $D_n$.  The comparison $2$-cell \eqref{eq:oplax-2cell} is therefore an identity by \eqref{eq:rel-strictification}.  At each prime-power step, Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-profile} gives the simple-quotient rectangle and Lemma~\ref{lem:affine-current-lift} lifts it to the next endpoint fibre.  Induction through the finitely many steps up to $X$ yields a finite labelled correspondence $R_X$ whose target projection covers
\[
\operatorname{Tor}_{E_X}(\gamma_X)\times I_X.
\tag{7.6}\label{eq:finite-response}
\]
Here $\gamma_X$ is the image of $\gamma_B$ under the endpoint quotient map.

\subsection{Reciprocal-mass control}

If $S_0$ is a label in the affine initial cover, Proposition~\ref{prop:wide-start} gives $W(S_0)<2-\eta$.  Each later prime-power step contributes reciprocal mass at most $\omega_Q$.  By \eqref{eq:mass-subadditive} and \eqref{eq:resource-tail}, every interval configuration $S$ labelling an edge of $R_X$ satisfies
\[
W(S)
\le W(S_0)+\sum_{B<Q\le X}\omega_Q
<2.
\tag{7.7}\label{eq:physical-W-less-2}
\]
At this point the incompatibility graph, the independent set $P_X$, the joint-compatibility factorization and the series $(\omega_Q)$ have all served their final purpose.

\subsection{The realization family}

For $X\in\mathcal H_B$, let $\mathcal R_X$ be the set of all finite realization data through $X$: an independent quota set, the local finite-solution witnesses, the factorization through $D_n$, and the resulting correspondence $R_X$ satisfying \eqref{eq:physical-W-less-2}.  The preceding argument shows
\[
\mathcal R_X\ne\varnothing
\qquad(X\in\mathcal H_B).
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal R=\coprod_{X\in\mathcal H_B}\mathcal R_X.
\]
Then the evident projection is surjective:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-surjection}
\mathcal R\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal H_B.
\end{equation}
Different cutoffs may use unrelated finite independent sets; no coherent infinite choice or compactness argument is involved.
