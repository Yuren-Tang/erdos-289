\begin{abstract}
We prove that every sufficiently large integer $k$ is the number of connected components of a finite set $S\subset\N_{>0}$ whose components are pairwise nonadjacent integer intervals of cardinality $2$ or $3$ and whose reciprocal sum is $1$.  This settles the strengthened disjoint, nonadjacent formulation currently listed as Erd\H{o}s Problem 289.  Signed-inverse interval families control the prime-power quotients of $\Q/\Z$, while reciprocal-neutral switches create wide intervals of possible component counts at arbitrarily small reciprocal cost.  A uniform restricted-sum transfer reduces the construction to scalar width dynamics, where gains at prime stages dominate the accumulated losses at composite prime powers.  A finite independent-transversal step then realizes the local data by mutually compatible intervals, and a terminal pullback at residue zero forces the reciprocal sum to equal one.
\end{abstract}
