\section{Finite Prefix seed certificate}
\label{app:prefix-seed}

The WideStart construction uses one finite early certificate.  Its numerical coordinates are proof data only; this appendix records enough exact arithmetic to make the finite input independently checkable.

Put
\[
P(m)=\frac1m+\frac1{m+1}.
\]
The empty state together with the following five strict binary states gives the six residue values in $H_6$:
\[
\frac16=
\sum_{m\in\{35,56,90,119,152,170,455,494,714\}}P(m),
\]
\[
\frac13=
\sum_{m\in\{17,35,44,84,104,119,132,170,272,285,455,494,560,714,935\}}P(m),
\]
\[
\frac12=
\sum_{m\in\{9,17,34,44,77,84,90\}}P(m),
\]
\[
\frac23=
\sum_{m\in\{7,16,34,44,51,65,77,119,132,153,170,272,285,455,494\}}P(m),
\]
\[
\frac56=P(2).
\tag{PS1}\label{eq:prefix-root-identities}
\]
Their grades are
\[
0,9,15,7,15,1.
\tag{PS2}
\]
Direct rational summation verifies \eqref{eq:prefix-root-identities}; every displayed start set has gaps at least three, so the states are physical.  Since $\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,3)=6$, these six values exhaust the first affine $H_6$-fibre.

The two early quotient bridges used before the uniform bridge regime have lower/upper moduli
\[
6\to12,
\qquad
12\to60.
\]
For $p=2$ use coefficient set $\{1\}$, giving bridge base $12$.  For $p=5$ use coefficients $\{1,2,3\}$, giving bases
\[
60,30,20.
\]
Their subset sums are surjective modulo $2$ and $5$ respectively; for $p=5$ the residues $0,1,2,3,4$ are witnessed by
\[
\varnothing,\quad\{1\},\quad\{2\},\quad\{3\},\quad\{1,3\}.
\]
The bridge identity is the same as in Section~\ref{sec:wide-start}: at lower modulus $D$ and quotient prime $p$, the alternatives at base $a=pD/r$ differ in reciprocal value by $r/(pD)$.

The finite physical-boundary bases that must be checked before the structural separation argument takes over are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-boundary-bases}
12,20,30,60,140,210,420,840,1260,2520.
\tag{PS3}
\end{equation}
Direct comparison shows that every such bridge envelope is nonadjacent to every root envelope and that the bridge envelopes in \eqref{eq:prefix-boundary-bases} are pairwise compatible.

For the resource margin, the largest root value is $5/6$.  For the four finite seed bridge bases $12,20,30,60$, the crude envelope bound $<3/a$ has total
\[
\frac3{12}+\frac3{20}+\frac3{30}+\frac3{60}
=\frac{11}{20}.
\]
Hence the finite seed is bounded by
\[
\frac56+\frac{11}{20}<\frac32.
\tag{PS4}\label{eq:finite-seed-bound}
\]

For a later universal bridge at lower modulus $D$, let $k$ be least with $k(k+1)/2\ge p-1$.  Minimality gives
\[
T_k=\frac{k(k+1)}2<2p.
\]
At base $a_r=pD/r$, every binary or ternary alternative has reciprocal mass $<3/a_r$.  Thus an entire branch of the bridge row has mass
\[
<\sum_{r=1}^k\frac{3r}{pD}
=\frac{3T_k}{pD}
<\frac6D.
\tag{PS5}\label{eq:future-row-bound}
\]
After the finite seed the first lower modulus is at least $60$, and successive nontrivial lcm jumps multiply the lower modulus by at least two.  Therefore the whole accumulated future bridge has branch mass
\[
<\sum_{j\ge0}\frac6{60\,2^j}
=\frac15<\frac14.
\tag{PS6}\label{eq:future-bridge-total}
\]
Combining \eqref{eq:finite-seed-bound} and \eqref{eq:future-bridge-total},
\[
\frac32+\frac14<2,
\tag{PS7}
\]
leaving a fixed positive margin before the arbitrarily small neutral-cube cost is added.

All finite statements in the first part of this appendix are exact-rational checks.  The repository accompanying the manuscript contains a short replay script for the identities and compatibility lists; the proof above does not use the script as a mathematical oracle.
