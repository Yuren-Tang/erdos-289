\section{Independent transversals and quota packing}
\label{app:packing}

The only graph-theoretic input used by the reciprocal provider is the classical
independent-transversal theorem of Haxell~\cite{Haxell1995,Haxell2001}.

\begin{theorem}[Haxell]\label{thm:haxell}
Let a finite graph have maximum degree at most $\Delta$, and let its vertex set
be partitioned into nonempty blocks, each of cardinality at least $2\Delta$.
Then the graph has an independent transversal: an independent set containing
one vertex from every block.
\end{theorem}

We use it only through the following immediate quota consequence.

\begin{proposition}[Quota packing]\label{thm:quota-packing}
Let a finite graph of maximum degree at most $d$ be partitioned into rows
$R_i$.  If $d>0$, there is an independent set meeting every row $R_i$ in
exactly
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{|R_i|}{2d}\right\rfloor
\]
vertices.  For $d=0$, the whole vertex set is independent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Assume $d>0$.  In every row split off
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{|R_i|}{2d}\right\rfloor
\]
disjoint chunks of cardinality $2d$, discarding fewer than $2d$ residual
vertices.  Restrict the conflict graph to the union of these chunks.  Its
maximum degree is still at most $d$, and the chunks form a vertex partition
whose blocks all have size $2d$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:haxell} gives one independent
representative from every chunk.  Their union is independent and has exactly
the displayed quota in each original row.  The case $d=0$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

The factor $2d$ is proof technology only.  In the application the conflict
degree is bounded uniformly after the finite obstacle has been absorbed into
the thinning constant, so quota packing preserves a fixed positive fraction of
every sufficiently large row.
