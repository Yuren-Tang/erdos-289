\begin{abstract}
We prove that every sufficiently large integer $k$ is the number of connected components of a finite set $S\subset\N_{>0}$ whose components are pairwise nonadjacent integer intervals of cardinality $2$ or $3$ and whose reciprocal sum is $1$.  This settles the strengthened disjoint, nonadjacent formulation currently listed as Erd\H{o}s Problem 289.  The proof uses the prime-power torsion filtration of $\Q/\Z$.  Signed-inverse interval families give uniform simple-quotient control, while reciprocal-neutral switches create wide component-count intervals.  A dense-restriction transfer theorem converts these local data into a scalar process in which prime gains dominate the accumulated loss at composite prime powers.  Haxell's independent-transversal theorem is applied only after a finite terminal cutoff has been chosen; a final pullback at residue zero then forces the reciprocal sum to equal one.
\end{abstract}
