\title{Fibrewise Exactification and Reciprocal Interval Profiles\\
\large A categorical proof of Erd\H{o}s Problem 289}
\author{Yuren Tang}
\date{4 September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 in the stronger subsystem in which every
connected component has size $2$ or $3$.  If $b_2$ and $b_3$ denote the
numbers of binary and ternary components, then there exists $d_*$ such that,
for every fixed $d\ge d_*$, all sufficiently large binary counts $b$ occur in
an exact reciprocal representation of $1$ with component profile $(b,d)$.
Consequently every sufficiently large total component count occurs.

The proof is organized around regular images, pullbacks and a target quotient.
The target $1$ is centered by the universal quotient $\Q\to\Q/\Z$.  The
application-specific root consists of two source-filtered responses: a finite
affine ray and a direct late-source targeted binary-residue response.  Their
source filters are intersected, one source is frozen before defect and final
grade are quantified, and exactification is the pullback of opposite literal
cosets in $\Q/\Z$.  The affine provider uses remote finite rational mobility
and scale-proper simple bridges; the Tail provider uses finite convolution
support at prime simple factors and target-specific filtered lifting.  The
small-resource quantifier necessarily precedes the eventual source quantifier:
fixed-source arbitrarily-small positive saturation is false.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For a finite set $S\subset\N_{>0}$ write
\[
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n.
\]
Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 asks whether, for every sufficiently large integer $k$,
one can find exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint and non-adjacent finite intervals of
positive integers, each of length at least $2$, whose reciprocal weights sum
to $1$.

Rather than treating an interval decomposition as primitive data, we regard a
finite subset of the path graph on $\N_{>0}$ as the physical state and obtain
its blocks from connected components.  We restrict to the subsystem in which
every component has cardinality $2$ or $3$.

For such a state put
\[
b_2(S)=\#\{\text{components of size }2\},
\qquad
b_3(S)=\#\{\text{components of size }3\}.
\]
The intrinsic additive exact label is the component profile
\[
\pi(S)=(b_2(S),b_3(S))\in\N^2,
\]
while the usual number of intervals is the consumer
\[
g(S)=b_2(S)+b_3(S).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Fibrewise profile saturation]\label{thm:fibrewise}
There exists $d_*\in\N$ such that for every $d\ge d_*$ there is $b_0(d)$ with
the following property.  For every $b\ge b_0(d)$ there exists a finite support
$S\subset\N_{>0}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item every connected component of $S$ has size $2$ or $3$;
\item distinct components are non-adjacent;
\item $W(S)=1$;
\item $(b_2(S),b_3(S))=(b,d)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Erd\H{o}s Problem 289]\label{cor:erdos289}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$, there exist exactly $k$ pairwise
disjoint and non-adjacent finite intervals of positive integers, each of
length at least $2$, whose reciprocal weights sum to $1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix one $d\ge d_*$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:fibrewise} realizes every sufficiently
large $b$ with profile $(b,d)$.  Hence every sufficiently large total component
count $b+d$ occurs.  The components already have sizes $2$ or $3$.
\end{proof}

For every finite $D\ge d_*$, the theorem also gives the stronger finite-defect
uniformity statement: all sufficiently large total grades are realizable at
every prescribed ternary count $d\in[d_*,D]$, with the configuration allowed
to depend on $d$.

\subsection{The active proof architecture}

The shortest dependency graph is
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& \text{regular images / target quotient} \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{component-profile specialization} \arrow[dl] \arrow[dr] & \\
\text{filtered affine ray} \arrow[dr]
&& \text{direct late-source targeted Tail} \arrow[dl] \\
& \text{source-filter intersection and source freeze} \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{opposite-coset pullback} \arrow[d] & \\
& W=1,\ (b_2,b_3)=(b,d) \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{projection to total component count.} &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The proof retains neither a common Tail across distinct defects nor an
independent ordinary exact-grade construction.

\subsection{Why the source must move}

Let
\[
H_n=n^{-1}\Z/\Z\le\Q/\Z.
\]
If $0<W<1$ and the source residue of $W$ modulo $H_n$ is zero, then
$[W]\in H_n$ and therefore
\[
W\in\left\{\frac1n,\frac2n,\ldots,\frac{n-1}{n}\right\}.
\]
Thus $W\ge1/n$.  No positive-grade response from one fixed source can have
zero source residue and arbitrarily small positive mass.  Consequently the
Tail theorem must quantify
\[
\varepsilon>0
\quad\text{before}\quad
\forall^\infty\text{ source}.
\]

\section{Physical states and component profiles}

Let $P$ be the path graph on $\N_{>0}$.  For a finite support $S$, its block
object is the connected-component quotient
\[
E(S)\rightrightarrows S\longrightarrow\pi_0(S)
\]
of the induced graph.  Admissibility requires every component to have
cardinality in $\{2,3\}$ and distinct components to be separated by at least
one unused vertex.

The reciprocal weight and component profile are additive on physically
compatible unions:
\[
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n,
\qquad
\pi(S)=(b_2(S),b_3(S)).
\]
Write $C^{23}$ for this partial physical state system and
\[
\Sigma^{23}_{1}
=
\im\bigl(C^{23}_{W=1}\xrightarrow{\pi}\N^2\bigr)
\]
for its exact component-profile spectrum.  The ordinary grade map factors
through
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
C^{23}_{W=1} \arrow[r,"{(b_2,b_3)}"] \arrow[dr,"g"']
& \N^2 \arrow[d,"+"]\\
& \N,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
so the ordinary exact-grade spectrum is a regular-image projection of the
joint spectrum.

\section{A regular correction calculus}
\label{sec:correction-engine}

Fix a regular category $\mathcal E$.  In the application $\mathcal E=\mathbf{Set}$,
so regular epimorphisms are simply surjections.  Let $V$ be an exact-value
object, $C$ a physical-state object and $L$ an internal additive monoid of
exact auxiliary labels, with
\[
W:C\to V,
\qquad
\lambda:C\to L.
\]
Let
\[
Q_0\xrightarrow{u}Q_1\xrightarrow{v}Q_2
\]
be internal abelian groups with compatible observations
\[
\rho_i:V\to Q_i,
\qquad
u\rho_0=\rho_1,
\qquad
v\rho_1=\rho_2.
\]
Put
\[
\omega_i=(\rho_iW,\lambda):C\to Q_i\times L.
\]

\subsection{Requirements and covers}

Over a parameter base $S$, a correction over $u$ is
\[
\phi=(u;d,\ell),
\qquad
d:S\to Q_1,
\quad
\ell:S\to L.
\]
Its required source object is the pullback
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
T_S(\phi) \arrow[r] \arrow[d]
& Q_0\times L \arrow[d,"u\times\mathrm{id}"]\\
S \arrow[r,"{(d,\ell)}"']
& Q_1\times L.
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{C1}\label{eq:required-pb}
\]
For a physical family $F\to S$, its realizer object is the pullback of the
physical observation along $T_S(\phi)$.  We say that $F$ covers $\phi$ when
\[
\Real_F(\phi)\twoheadrightarrow T_S(\phi)
\]
is regular epi.  No chosen realizer belongs to the definition.

\subsection{Literal matching and physical completion}

Let
\[
\phi=(u;d,\ell),
\qquad
\psi=(v;e,k)
\]
be composable.  Their correction composite is
\[
\boxed{
\psi\circ\phi=(vu;\,v(d)+e,\,\ell+k).}
\tag{C2}\label{eq:correction-composition}
\]
Write $\theta=\psi\circ\phi$.  A point of $T_S(\theta)$ with source coordinate
$z\in Q_0$ forces the second requirement
\[
\delta(z)=u(z)-d\in Q_1,
\qquad
v(\delta(z))=e.
\]
If $R$ covers $\psi$, pull its realizer cover back along this forced map:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
P_R \arrow[r] \arrow[d,two heads]
& \Real_R(\psi) \arrow[d,two heads]\\
T_S(\theta) \arrow[r,"\delta"']
& T_S(\psi).
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{C3}\label{eq:second-pullback}
\]
A point of $P_R$ contains an actual second physical state $r$.  Let
\[
y=\rho_0W(r)\in Q_0.
\]
Since $u(y)=\delta(z)$, the same literal composite requirement forces
\[
x=z-y,
\qquad
u(x)=d.
\]
If $F$ covers $\phi$, pull its realizer cover back along this first requirement:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\operatorname{Cmp}(F,R;\phi,\psi)
\arrow[r]
\arrow[d,two heads]
& \Real_F(\phi) \arrow[d,two heads]\\
P_R \arrow[r] & T_S(\phi).
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{C4}\label{eq:first-pullback}
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{Cmp}(F,R;\phi,\psi)
\twoheadrightarrow T_S(\theta)
\tag{C5}\label{eq:literal-match-cover}
\]
is regular epi.  All literal matched realizers remain in the fibre.

Literal matching does not by itself imply physical compatibility.  Let
\[
\operatorname{Cmp}_{\rm phys}
\hookrightarrow
\operatorname{Cmp}(F,R;\phi,\psi)
\]
be the compatible subobject.  The additional physical hypothesis is precisely
\[
\operatorname{Cmp}_{\rm phys}\twoheadrightarrow T_S(\theta).
\tag{C6}\label{eq:physical-match-cover}
\]
Compatible union then gives the commuting square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\operatorname{Cmp}_{\rm phys} \arrow[r,"\sqcup"] \arrow[d]
& C \arrow[d,"\omega_0"]\\
T_S(\theta) \arrow[r] & Q_0\times L,
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{C7}\label{eq:physical-completion}
\]
so the image family of physical unions covers the composite correction.

This is the generic form of the rule
\[
\boxed{\text{compose literal witnesses before passing to their image}.}
\]
In the reciprocal Tail the local occurrences are chosen from one globally
independent packed pool, which supplies the physical hypothesis.

\subsection{Exact transfer}

For a target $\tau:S\to V$, exact states are the pullback
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
C_\tau \arrow[r] \arrow[d]
& C \arrow[d,"W"]\\
S \arrow[r,"\tau"']
& V.
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{C8}\label{eq:exact-state-pb}
\]
Their exact label spectrum is the regular image
\[
C_\tau\twoheadrightarrow\Sigma_\tau\hookrightarrow S\times L.
\]
For a target correction $(u;\rho_1\tau,\ell)$, naturality gives the canonical
required-source section
\[
\sigma_\tau=(\rho_0\tau,\ell):S\to T_S(u;\rho_1\tau,\ell).
\]
Pulling a realizer cover back along this section gives
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\operatorname{TarReal}_F \arrow[r] \arrow[d,two heads]
& \Real_F(u;\rho_1\tau,\ell) \arrow[d,two heads]\\
S \arrow[r,"\sigma_\tau"']
& T_S(u;\rho_1\tau,\ell).
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{C9}\label{eq:target-realizer-pb}
\]
If the resulting state map factors through $C_\tau$, regular-image uniqueness
forces the target label graph to factor through $\Sigma_\tau$.  The final
reciprocal root uses the still thinner special case in which opposite literal
cosets are matched directly and $0<W<2$ literalizes the centered class to the
integer $1$.

\section{Support reflection and finite convolution}
\label{sec:support-enrichment}

For a realization-label monoid $R$, a finite witness family is a map $X\to R$.
Its observable realizability support is the regular image
\[
X\twoheadrightarrow\Supp(X)\hookrightarrow R.
\]
For two witness families, form the literal product family first and then take
the regular image of label addition.  In $\mathbf{Set}$, when $R$ is finite,
this same operation has a cardinality refinement in the semiring $\N[R]$:
product families become convolution, and ordinary regular-image support is the
support of the resulting coefficient function.  Appendix
\ref{app:atomic-convolution} uses exactly this finite decategorification to
prove local prime-order coverage; characters appear only inside the proof that
all convolution coefficients are positive.

The distinction is important: physical compatibility is consumed before
support reflection, while coefficient multiplicities may be retained below a
provider boundary when they are useful for proving surjectivity.

\section{Target quotient and compact resolution}
\label{sec:target-quotient}

The marked target is the morphism
\[
\Z\xrightarrow{1\mapsto1}\Q.
\]
Its universal target-annihilating quotient is its cokernel
\[
A:=\Q/\Z.
\]
Thus $\Q/\Z$ is forced by the target rather than chosen as an auxiliary
coordinate.

The finitely generated subgroups of $A$ form a filtered compact observation
diagram.  Every such subgroup is
\[
H_n=\ker([n]:A\to A)
=n^{-1}\Z/\Z,
\]
and
\[
H_n\le H_m\iff n\mid m.
\]
Hence gcd and lcm are merely coordinates for meet and join in the compact
subgroup lattice.  A cover in one $p$-primary height has prime-order quotient;
no basis or splitting of that quotient is canonically chosen.

\section{A non-used generic weakening}
\label{sec:further-compression}

For one specified target section, full correction-fibre coverage is generically
stronger than necessary: it is enough for the forced requirements to lie in
the corresponding realizable-requirement images and for the matched physical
completion to cover the target section.  This sectionwise matched-image
weakening is conceptually useful but is not needed for the proof above.  The
active Tail is already target-specific, and the manuscript does not pursue a
further provider compression.

\section{Provenance and formalization status}
\label{sec:provenance}

The mathematical route developed independently before the proof claim posted
on 4 September 2026.  A standalone concrete proof manuscript had been
completed in mid-August, followed by categorical reorganization and an ongoing
Lean formalization programme.  The present manuscript uses the later
categorical reorganization rather than reproducing the older concrete proof
architecture.

The Lean formalization is incomplete and is not claimed here as a formal
verification of the final theorem.  Its development has nevertheless exposed
witness-lifetime, compatibility, quantifier-order and dependency issues that
motivated several of the categorical normalizations used above.

The proof development used substantial assistance from large language models.
All claims in the public manuscript are intended to be presented in a
self-contained human-checkable form except for the explicitly cited
independent-transversal theorem.
