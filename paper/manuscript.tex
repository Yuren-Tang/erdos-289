\title{Fibrewise Exactification and Reciprocal Interval Profiles\\
\large A categorical proof of Erd\H{o}s Problem 289}
\author{Yuren Tang}
\date{4 September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We prove Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 in the stronger subsystem in which every
connected component has size $2$ or $3$.  There exists $d_*$ such that, for
every fixed $d\ge d_*$, all sufficiently large binary component counts $b$
occur in an exact reciprocal representation of $1$ with component profile
$(b_2,b_3)=(b,d)$.

The proof separates a regular-categorical exactification layer from two
reciprocal providers.  The target $1$ is centered by the universal quotient
$\Q\to\Q/\Z$.  A source-closed finite Prefix supplies a cofinal affine residue
cover with uniform physical transparency, while a direct late-source Tail
uses finite convolution support at prime simple factors and target-specific
filtered lifting.  The two eventual source loci are intersected, one endpoint
source is frozen before defect and final grade are quantified, and exact value
$1$ follows from a pullback of opposite literal cosets in $\Q/\Z$ together
with the interval $0<W<2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For a finite set $S\subset\N_{>0}$ put
\[
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n.
\]
Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 asks whether, for every sufficiently large integer $k$,
one can find exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint and non-adjacent finite intervals of
positive integers, each of length at least $2$, whose reciprocal weights sum
to $1$.

We regard a finite support in the path graph on $\N_{>0}$ as the physical
state; its intervals are the connected components of the induced graph.  We
work in the stronger subsystem in which every component has size $2$ or $3$.
Write
\[
b_2(S)=\#\{\text{components of size }2\},
\qquad
b_3(S)=\#\{\text{components of size }3\}.
\]
The intrinsic additive label is
\[
\pi(S)=(b_2(S),b_3(S))\in\N^2,
\]
while the ordinary number of intervals is the consumer
\[
g(S)=b_2(S)+b_3(S).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Fibrewise profile saturation]\label{thm:fibrewise}
There exists $d_*\in\N$ such that for every $d\ge d_*$ there is $b_0(d)$ for
which every $b\ge b_0(d)$ is realized by a finite support $S$ satisfying
\begin{enumerate}
\item every connected component has size $2$ or $3$;
\item distinct components are non-adjacent;
\item $W(S)=1$;
\item $(b_2(S),b_3(S))=(b,d)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Erd\H{o}s Problem 289]\label{cor:erdos289}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$, there exist exactly $k$ pairwise
disjoint and non-adjacent finite intervals of positive integers, each of
length at least $2$, whose reciprocal weights sum to $1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix one $d\ge d_*$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:fibrewise} realizes every sufficiently
large $b$ with profile $(b,d)$, hence every sufficiently large total component
count $b+d$ occurs.
\end{proof}

The active proof graph is
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& \text{target quotient / regular-image calculus} \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{component-profile specialization} \arrow[dl] \arrow[dr] & \\
\text{source-closed affine ray} \arrow[dr]
&& \text{direct targeted Tail} \arrow[dl] \\
& \text{common endpoint source} \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{opposite-coset pullback} \arrow[d] & \\
& W=1,\quad (b_2,b_3)=(b,d) \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{projection to total component count.} &
\end{tikzcd}
\]

\section{Physical states and exact labels}

For a finite support $S$, let
\[
E(S)\rightrightarrows S\longrightarrow\pi_0(S)
\]
be the connected-component quotient of the induced path graph.  Admissibility
requires every component to have cardinality in $\{2,3\}$ and distinct
components to be separated by at least one unused vertex.  Reciprocal weight
and component profile are additive on physically compatible unions.

Write $C^{23}$ for this partial physical state system and
\[
\Sigma^{23}_1
=
\im\bigl(C^{23}_{W=1}\xrightarrow{(b_2,b_3)}\N^2\bigr)
\]
for its exact profile spectrum.  The ordinary grade factors through
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
C^{23}_{W=1}\arrow[r,"{(b_2,b_3)}"]\arrow[dr,"g"']
&\N^2\arrow[d,"+"]\\
&\N,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
so the ordinary exact-grade spectrum is a regular-image projection of the
joint spectrum.

\section{A regular correction calculus}
\label{sec:correction-engine}

We record the generic bookkeeping in a regular category $\mathcal E$; the
application is in $\mathbf{Set}$, where regular epimorphisms are surjections.
Let
\[
W:C\to V,
\qquad
\lambda:C\to L
\]
be the exact-value map and an additive auxiliary-label map.  Let
\[
Q_0\xrightarrow{u}Q_1\xrightarrow{v}Q_2
\]
be internal abelian groups with observations
\[
\rho_i:V\to Q_i,
\qquad
u\rho_0=\rho_1,
\qquad
v\rho_1=\rho_2.
\]
In the first compatibility the symbol is the morphism $u:Q_0\to Q_1$; explicitly,
\[
u\rho_0=\rho_1.
\tag{C0}\label{eq:obs-compat}
\]
Put $\omega_i=(\rho_iW,\lambda)$.

Over a parameter base $S$, a correction over $u$ is
\[
\phi=(u;d,\ell),
\qquad
d:S\to Q_1,
\quad
\ell:S\to L.
\]
Its required source object is the pullback
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
T_S(\phi)\arrow[r]\arrow[d]
&Q_0\times L\arrow[d,"u\times\mathrm{id}"]\\
S\arrow[r,"{(d,\ell)}"']
&Q_1\times L.
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{C1}\label{eq:required-pb}
\]
A physical family covers $\phi$ when its realizer pullback maps regularly
epimorphically to $T_S(\phi)$; no chosen realizer is part of the definition.

For composable corrections
\[
\phi=(u;d,\ell),
\qquad
\psi=(v;e,k),
\]
the composite is
\[
\psi\circ\phi=(vu;v(d)+e,\ell+k).
\tag{C2}\label{eq:correction-composition}
\]
A literal point of the composite requirement with source coordinate $z\in Q_0$
forces the second requirement
\[
\delta(z)=u(z)-d,
\qquad
v(\delta(z))=e.
\]
Pulling the second realizer cover back along this map gives
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
P_R\arrow[r]\arrow[d,two heads]
&\Real_R(\psi)\arrow[d,two heads]\\
T_S(\psi\circ\phi)\arrow[r,"\delta"']
&T_S(\psi).
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{C3}\label{eq:second-pullback}
\]
A point of $P_R$ contains an actual second physical state $r$.  If
$y=\rho_0W(r)$, the first source coordinate is forced to be
\[
x=z-y,
\qquad
u(x)=d.
\]
Equivalently, the last equation is $u(x)=d$.  Pulling the first realizer cover
back along this requirement gives a regular-epimorphic literal
matched-realizer object over the composite requirement.  Restricting to
physically compatible matched pairs and taking physical union gives the
composite cover.  Thus literal witnesses are composed before their
regular-image support is taken.

For a target $\tau:S\to V$, exact states form
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
C_\tau\arrow[r]\arrow[d]
&C\arrow[d,"W"]\\
S\arrow[r,"\tau"']
&V.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Pulling a target correction cover back along the canonical target section and
then factoring through $C_\tau$ sends its label graph into the regular image of
the exact-label map.  The final reciprocal proof uses an even thinner instance:
opposite literal cosets are matched directly, and integrality together with
$0<W<2$ forces the value $1$.

\section{Support reflection and finite convolution}
\label{sec:support-enrichment}

For a finite witness family $X\to R$, its observable realizability support is
the regular image
\[
X\twoheadrightarrow\Supp(X)\hookrightarrow R.
\]
Literal product families are formed before this reflection.  When $R$ is a
finite additive group, the same construction has a cardinality refinement in
$\N[R]$: product families become convolution and ordinary image support is the
support of the coefficient function.  Appendix~\ref{app:atomic-convolution}
uses this finite decategorification to prove prime-order local coverage.
Characters occur only inside the proof that all convolution coefficients are
positive; they do not survive the provider boundary.

\section{Target quotient and compact endpoints}
\label{sec:target-quotient}

The target $1$ is the marking
\[
\Z\xrightarrow{1\mapsto1}\Q.
\]
Its universal target-annihilating quotient is
\[
A:=\Q/\Z.
\]
For $n\ge1$ put
\[
H_n=n^{-1}\Z/\Z=\ker([n]:A\to A).
\]
These are exactly the finite subgroups of $A$ and
\[
H_n\le H_m\iff n\mid m.
\]

A numerical endpoint cutoff is a different coordinate.  Put
\[
L_B=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B),
\qquad
E_B:=H_{L_B}.
\tag{E1}\label{eq:endpoint-subgroup}
\]
Equivalently, $E_B$ is the join of all prime-power current subgroups of rank at
most $B$.  The endpoint system is cofinal in the finite-subgroup lattice.  An
eligible Tail source is such an endpoint $E_B$ subject to the boundary-row
condition used below.

The fixed-source smallness obstruction is already visible here.  For a fixed
$H_n$, a positive $0<W<1$ with zero residue modulo $H_n$ satisfies $W\ge1/n$.
Thus the Tail tolerance must be fixed before the eventual endpoint source is
chosen.

\section{Provenance and formalization status}
\label{sec:provenance}

The mathematical route developed independently before the proof claim posted
on 4 September 2026.  A standalone concrete proof manuscript had been
completed in mid-August, followed by categorical reorganization and an ongoing
Lean formalization programme.  The present manuscript uses the later
categorical organization rather than reproducing the older concrete proof
architecture.

The Lean formalization is incomplete and is not claimed here as a formal
verification of the final theorem.  The proof development used substantial
assistance from large language models.  The present text is intended to be
human-checkable; its only external combinatorial theorem is the explicitly
cited independent-transversal theorem.
