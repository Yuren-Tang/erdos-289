\title{Fibrewise Exactification and Reciprocal Interval Profiles\\
\large A categorical proof of Erd\H{o}s Problem 289}
\author{Yuren Tang}
\date{4 September 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We prove Erd\H{o}s Problem 289.  In fact we work in the stronger subsystem in which every connected component has size $2$ or $3$.  If $b_2$ and $b_3$ denote the numbers of binary and ternary components, respectively, then there exists $d_0$ such that for every fixed $d\ge d_0$, all sufficiently large binary counts $b$ occur in an exact reciprocal representation of $1$ with component profile $(b,d)$.  Consequently every sufficiently large total component count occurs.

The proof is organized around a regular-categorical exactification mechanism.  Correction requirements are pullbacks, physical coverage is expressed by regular epimorphisms from realizer pullbacks, and physical composition is performed on literal matched-realizer objects before passage to regular-image support.  The target $1$ is centered by its universal annihilating quotient $\Q\to\Q/\Z$, and the resulting coefficient system is resolved by compact subgroups.  The application-specific mathematics enters through only two response theorems: a finite affine response at fixed component profile and a centered cofinal saturation response whose binary-count image is final.  The latter is proved from transverse reciprocal supply, bounded-thinning physical packing, a prime-order restricted-fold theorem, and summable resource control.  Once these two responses are available, the passage to exact reciprocal profiles is diagrammatic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For a finite set $S\subset\N_{>0}$ write
\[
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n.
\]
Erd\H{o}s Problem 289 asks whether, for every sufficiently large integer $k$, one can find exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint and non-adjacent finite intervals of positive integers, each of length at least $2$, whose reciprocal weights sum to $1$.

We prove a stronger statement.  Rather than treating an interval decomposition as primitive data, we regard a finite subset of the path graph on $\N_{>0}$ as the physical state and obtain its blocks from the connected-component quotient.  We then restrict to the subsystem in which every component has cardinality $2$ or $3$.

For such a state let
\[
b_2(S)=\#\{\text{components of size }2\},
\qquad
b_3(S)=\#\{\text{components of size }3\}.
\]
The intrinsic additive label is the component profile
\[
\pi(S)=(b_2(S),b_3(S))\in\N^2.
\]
The usual number of intervals is only the consumer
\[
g(S)=b_2(S)+b_3(S).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Fibrewise profile saturation]\label{thm:fibrewise}
There exists $d_0\in\N$ such that for every $d\ge d_0$ there is $b_0(d)$ with the following property.  For every $b\ge b_0(d)$ there exists a finite support $S\subset\N_{>0}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item every connected component of $S$ has size $2$ or $3$;
\item distinct components are non-adjacent;
\item $W(S)=1$;
\item $(b_2(S),b_3(S))=(b,d)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Erd\H{o}s Problem 289]\label{cor:erdos289}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$, there exist exactly $k$ pairwise disjoint and non-adjacent finite intervals of positive integers, each of length at least $2$, whose reciprocal weights sum to $1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix any $d\ge d_0$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:fibrewise}, every sufficiently large $b$ occurs with profile $(b,d)$.  The total number of components is $b+d$, and hence every sufficiently large total component count occurs.  The components already have sizes $2$ or $3$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:fibrewise} also gives a stronger finite-uniformity statement.  For each fixed $d\ge d_0$ the set of realizable total grades is final in $\N$; finite intersections of these final sets are again final.  Thus, for every finite interval of sufficiently large ternary counts, all sufficiently large total grades can be realized at every ternary count in that interval.  We state this later as a corollary rather than using it in the shortest proof of Erd\H{o}s Problem 289.

\subsection{The proof architecture}

The proof separates into a generic exactification spine and two application-specific response theorems.  Schematically,
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& \text{regular correction / support calculus} \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{target centering and compact resolution} \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{reciprocal component-profile specialization} \arrow[dl] \arrow[dr] & \\
\mathsf{AFF}_{289}(d) \arrow[dr] && \mathsf{SAT}_{289} \arrow[dl] \\
& \text{one-defect exactification} \arrow[d] & \\
& \{b:(b,d)\text{ is exact}\}\text{ is final} \arrow[d] & \\
& \text{projection to total component count.} &
\end{tikzcd}
\]

The left response is finite and may carry a non-zero affine correction.  The right response is centered and cofinal.  A canonical least absorber kills the affine mismatch before the two responses are composed.

The generic part of the proof uses only pullbacks, regular epimorphisms, regular images, internal group/monoid operations in observation coordinates, and finite intersections of final subobjects.  The remaining arithmetic and combinatorics occur below the two response arrows.

\subsection{Compose before reflect}

A recurring point is that physical compatibility belongs to actual witnesses, not to aggregate labels.  We therefore retain literal witness tuples until physical composition has been certified.  Only afterwards do we pass to the regular-image support of the label map.  In symbols,
\[
\boxed{
\text{witness tuples}
\longrightarrow
\text{physical composition}
\longrightarrow
\text{regular-image support}
\longrightarrow
\text{profile calculus}.}
\]
This order prevents an aggregate label from being realized by a physically unrelated decomposition.

\section{Physical states and component profiles}

Let $P$ be the path graph on $\N_{>0}$.  For a finite support $S$, let $E(S)$ be the edge object of the induced subgraph.  Its connected-component object is the coequalizer
\[
E(S)\rightrightarrows S\longrightarrow\pi_0(S).
\]
Admissibility is imposed by requiring every component to have cardinality in $\{2,3\}$ and requiring distinct components to be separated by at least one unused vertex.

The reciprocal weight and component profile are additive on physically compatible unions:
\[
W(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n,
\qquad
\pi(S)=(b_2(S),b_3(S)).
\]
We write $C^{23}$ for this partial physical state system and
\[
\Sigma^{23}_{1}
=
\im\bigl(C^{23}_{W=1}\xrightarrow{\pi}\N^2\bigr)
\]
for its exact component-profile spectrum.

The ordinary grade map factors as
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
C^{23}_{W=1} \arrow[r,"{(b_2,b_3)}"] \arrow[dr,"g"']
& \N^2 \arrow[d,"+"]\\
& \N.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Consequently the ordinary exact-grade spectrum is the regular-image projection of $\Sigma^{23}_{1}$.

\section{The regular correction engine}

\subsection{Required objects and physical covers}

We work first in a regular category $\mathcal E$.  Let $V$ be an exact-value carrier, $L$ an internal monoid of exact auxiliary labels, and
\[
W:C\to V,
\qquad
\lambda:C\to L
\]
be the physical observations.

For one internal group homomorphism $u:Q_0\to Q_1$, a parameterized correction over a base $S$ consists of maps
\[
d:S\to UQ_1,
\qquad
\ell:S\to L.
\]
Its required source object is the pullback
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
T_S(\phi) \arrow[r] \arrow[d]
& UQ_0\times L \arrow[d,"Uu\times\mathrm{id}"]\\
S \arrow[r,"{(d,\ell)}"']
& UQ_1\times L.
\end{tikzcd}
\]

For a physical family $F\to S$, define the realizer pullback $\Real_F(\phi)$.  The cover condition is exactly that
\[
\Real_F(\phi)\twoheadrightarrow T_S(\phi)
\]
be a regular epimorphism.

\subsection{Literal matching and physical completion}

For two composable corrections, a composite requirement canonically forces the required second observation.  Pulling a second realizer back along that map then forces the required first observation.  This produces a literal matched-realizer object
\[
\operatorname{Cmp}(F,R;\phi,\psi)
\twoheadrightarrow
T_S(\psi\phi).
\]

The generic theorem does not require a globally defined multiplication on $C$.  It requires only a requirement-preserving physical certificate relation over this matched-realizer object whose projection to the composite requirement is regular epi.  Compatible union in the reciprocal application is one construction of such a certificate.

\subsection{Exact transfer}

For a target $\tau:S\to V$, exact states are the pullback
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
C_\tau \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & C \arrow[d,"W"]\\
S \arrow[r,"\tau"'] & V,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
and the exact auxiliary-label spectrum is the regular image
\[
\Sigma_\tau
=
\im(C_\tau\to S\times L).
\]
A target correction has a canonical target section of its required object.  Pulling a correction cover back along this section and factoring its physical state map through $C_\tau$ forces the target label graph to factor through $\Sigma_\tau$.

\section{Witness semantics and support enrichment}

Actual labelled witness families live over the label object.  Their support is obtained by regular-image factorization.  Witness convolution is performed before support reflection, and support convolution is the image of label addition.  Pathwise physical composition certificates therefore induce inclusions of path tensors into realizable support profiles.  Joining over paths yields the free enriched realization theorem used in the centered saturation response.

This section will state the generic support-reflection and free-path theorems in the minimum assumptions needed by the reciprocal application.

\section{Target centering and compact resolution}

For the reciprocal target $1$, the universal target-annihilating quotient is
\[
\Q\longrightarrow\Q/\Z.
\]
The compact observation stages are the finitely generated subgroups of $\Q/\Z$, equivalently the finite cyclic subgroups
\[
H_n=\ker([n]:\Q/\Z\to\Q/\Z).
\]
Their order is divisibility; gcd and lcm are only coordinate descriptions of meet and join.

For a mismatch class $m\in (\Q/\Z)/H$, its least absorber is the pullback of the cyclic subgroup generated by $m$.  Passing to any later compact stage above this absorber kills the mismatch.

Simple compact-stage factors have prime cardinality.  The additive-combinatorial theorem required later depends only on this prime-order additive group, not on a chosen field basis.

\section{The two reciprocal response theorems}

\subsection{Finite affine response}

We isolate a finite response theorem at a fixed ternary count.

\begin{theorem}[Affine response, root-facing form]\label{thm:aff}
There exists $d_0$ such that, for every $d\ge d_0$, there is a finite homogeneous physical family covering one affine correction and having one common component profile $(b,d)$ for some $b\in\N$, with all realizing reciprocal values strictly between $0$ and $2$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is obtained as a point-fibre regular-image weakening of the stronger historical affine-slab construction.  Equal-value component-profile translations are kernel-pair pullbacks; fixed-cardinality selector fibres force a prescribed ternary count without changing reciprocal value or total grade.  Cross-defect common correction, common grade, and common tail are not retained above this provider boundary.

\begin{remark}
There is also an independent, very short finite affine-nucleation theorem using arbitrarily light remote equal-grade rational deformations and cyclic orbit coverage.  It is useful conceptually and for other targets.  We do not use it alone to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:aff}, because the fixed-defect threshold needed by the present root is supplied directly by the stronger historical slab provider.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Centered saturation response}

Fix a source compact stage $H$, a lower endpoint request $K\ge H$, a finite physical obstacle $O$, and $\varepsilon>0$.  Let
\[
\operatorname{CentResp}_{K,<\varepsilon}(H,O)
\]
be the total object of centered binary correction responses satisfying those requests, and let
\[
J(H,K,O,\varepsilon)
=
\im\bigl(
\operatorname{CentResp}_{K,<\varepsilon}(H,O)
\to\N
\bigr)
\]
be the regular image of binary component count.

\begin{theorem}[Centered saturation]\label{thm:sat}
For every $H\le K$, every finite physical obstacle $O$, and every $\varepsilon>0$, the set
\[
J(H,K,O,\varepsilon)
\]
is final in $\N$.
\end{theorem}

The proof will be given through the free enriched realization engine.  The ordinary mathematical inputs are isolated as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item comparable-prime supply;
\item bounded-degree quota packing;
\item transverse reciprocal physical supply;
\item restricted-fold surjectivity in a finite additive group of prime order;
\item bounded-thinning transference, interval overlap, and summable resource control.
\end{enumerate}
The historical quantitative signed-inverse rates are used only to prove these provider properties and disappear before Theorem~\ref{thm:sat}.

\section{Fibrewise exactification}

Fix $d\ge d_0$ and choose an affine response from Theorem~\ref{thm:aff}, with component profile $(b,d)$, stage $H$, physical family $F$, and affine mismatch $m$.  Let
\[
K=H[m]
\]
be the least absorber.

Because $F$ is finite and every realizing state has reciprocal value in $(0,2)$, the canonical safe-budget set
\[
\operatorname{SafeBudget}(F)
=
\{\varepsilon>0:\forall f\in F,\ W(f)+\varepsilon<2\}
\]
is nonempty.  Fix a generalized $\varepsilon$ in this cover and let $O(F)$ be the finite obstacle consisting of the complete affine physical footprint.

Theorem~\ref{thm:sat} gives a final set
\[
J_d=J(H,K,O(F),\varepsilon).
\]
For $h\in J_d$ choose a centered binary response of profile $(h,0)$.  The endpoint lies above the least absorber, so the affine mismatch dies.  The component-profile addition square is
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
F\times T_h \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\pi\times\pi"']
& C^{23} \arrow[d,"\pi"]\\
\{(b,d)\}\times\{(h,0)\}
\arrow[r,"+"']
& \{(b+h,d)\}.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The safe-budget inequality and centered residue place every target-looking composite state in
\[
(0,2)\cap\Z=\{1\}.
\]
Exact transfer therefore gives
\[
(b+h,d)\in\Sigma^{23}_{1}.
\]
Since $J_d$ is final, so is its translate $b+J_d$.  This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:fibrewise}.

\section{Finite-defect uniformity}

For fixed $d\ge d_0$, let
\[
B_d=\{b:(b,d)\in\Sigma^{23}_{1}\}.
\]
Then $B_d$ is final, and so is
\[
G_d=d+B_d,
\]
the set of total component counts realizable at ternary count $d$.

For every finite $D\ge d_0$,
\[
\bigcap_{d=d_0}^{D}G_d
\]
is final.  Hence every sufficiently large total component count can be realized simultaneously at every prescribed ternary count in $[d_0,D]$ (with a configuration depending on the prescribed ternary count).

\section{Further categorical compression}

For one specified target section, full correction-fibre coverage is generically stronger than necessary.  A sectionwise matched-image theorem only requires the forced second and first requirements to land in the corresponding realizable-requirement images, together with a physical completion certificate.  We record this theorem separately.

The present reciprocal application is deliberately not weakened to that boundary: its arithmetic provider is used through the already established stronger full simple-fibre coverage.  No claim that the restricted-fold theorem can be removed is needed for the proof above.

\section{Provenance and formalization status}

The mathematical route developed independently before the proof claim posted on 4 September 2026.  A standalone concrete proof manuscript had been completed in mid-August, followed by categorical reorganization and an ongoing Lean formalization programme.  The present manuscript uses the later categorical reorganization rather than reproducing the older concrete proof architecture.

The Lean formalization is incomplete and is not claimed here as a formal verification of the final theorem.  Its role has nevertheless been useful in exposing witness-lifetime, compatibility, and dependency issues that motivated several of the categorical normalizations used above.

The proof development used substantial assistance from large language models.  All mathematical claims in the public manuscript are intended to be presented in a self-contained human-checkable form.
