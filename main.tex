\begin{abstract}
We prove that for every sufficiently large integer $k$ there is a finite set $S\subset\N_{>0}$ that is the disjoint union of exactly $k$ pairwise nonadjacent integer intervals, each of cardinality $2$ or $3$, and satisfies $\sum_{n\in S}1/n=1$.  This gives an affirmative answer, in a slightly stronger bounded-length form, to the strengthened disjoint, nonadjacent formulation currently listed as Erd\H{o}s Problem 289.  The proof follows the prime-power filtration of $\Q/\Z$: signed-inverse interval families provide controlled local residues, while reciprocal-neutral identities provide freedom in the number of components.  Restricted-sum estimates yield eventual coverage of all sufficiently large component counts; after a finite cutoff, Haxell's independent-transversal theorem realizes the local choices by mutually compatible intervals.  Affine-fibre pullbacks and a compatible covering theorem assemble these inputs.  A final mass bound lifts residue zero to the exact reciprocal value $1$; all three-element components may be confined to a fixed finite region.
\end{abstract}
